The vehicle · Chapter 2

Power and wiring

Two LiPo packs feed one battery bus. A kill switch cuts the thrusters and nothing else. Two regulator modules power everything else, and one CAN cable connects the boards. Where every amp goes, and how to check it.

The short version
  • Two smart battery housings, each holding a MaxAmps 8000 mAh 5S LiPo (18.5 V nominal, 148 Wh) with its own protection board. An ideal-diode OR on the POAC board lets whichever pack is higher carry the bus.
  • The kill switch only cuts MOTOR_PWR, the ESC supply. The Orin, sensors and boards stay on, so a kill never reboots anything.
  • Two 20 A regulator modules make 15 V (Orin, DVL) and 5 V (logic, switch, fans). Every board makes its own 3.3 V. The rail called twelve_volt_voltage is really 15 V.
  • Data: one CAN bus (1 Mbit/s) runs through every board to the Orin (can0). The batteries sit on a second, slower bus. Ethernet joins the Orin, the DVL and the tether.
  • Thrusters dominate the peak draw: about 26 A at the peak of an aggressive pool run, estimated from T200 data. The ESCs' own current telemetry is not usable.

This chapter follows the power from the cells to every load, then the data wiring. It stays at the level of the whole vehicle. Each board gets its own page in The circuit boards, and what the firmware does with them is in Firmware on the boards. The facts come from the Altium designs in electric_boogaloo, the board firmware in titan_firmware and the team's design reports. Where a fact is inferred rather than confirmed, the page says so.

Each block is a board, a regulator or a load. Power links are drawn by what they carry, and data links by bus. The layout follows the team's own architecture diagram in the 2026 design report. The nets and connectors come from the board designs.

The batteries

Talos carries two identical smart battery housings, one on each side. Each holds one pack and two boards: a battery-management board and a microcontroller board. Both housings use the same design, and the team's next vehicle, Triton, shares them (2026 report).

Pack
MaxAmps LiPo 8000 mAh 5S2P, 18.5 V (design reports; the gas-gauge notes are written for this pack)
Cells
5 in series, 2 in parallel: 3.7 V nominal per cell
Energy
148 Wh per pack, 296 Wh for both
Usable range
About 20.75 V charged (4.15 V per cell) down to 16 V (3.2 V per cell). Swap by 17.5 V (3.5 V per cell, MaxAmps' landing voltage).
Current
45 A for 5 s, or 80 A for 2 s, then the BMS cuts out. A 45 A self-control fuse sits in series.
Housing
CNC-milled aluminium, o-ring lid, a pressure-relief valve, a window for the display, and two SubConns: power and telemetry (2023 report)

What "smart" means

  • Protection. A TI BQ40Z80 gas gauge watches every cell. It opens two 80 V, 100 A MOSFETs on undervoltage, overvoltage, overcurrent or temperature, and balances the cells while charging.
  • A dead connector until it's plugged in. The output stays off until the cable ties the housing's DETECT pin to pack negative. The 2023 bring-up test checks exactly this. A housing on the table has no voltage on its pins.
  • Display. A 128×32 OLED behind the window shows the state of charge. A magnet on the reed switch wakes it, so the pack can sleep while it is stored.
  • Telemetry. The microcontroller publishes state/battery (BatteryStatus: pack voltage, current, average current, time to empty, state of charge, side) on the external CAN bus. On 2026-10-27 the team commented out the Orin's agent for that bus (can1) in copro_agent.launch.py, "until poac/batteries happy", so battery telemetry doesn't reach ROS at the moment.
  • Remote power-off. On command/electrical, CYCLE_ROBOT and KILL_ROBOT_POWER make the housings switch their output off, briefly or latched (ros.c). These only work while that bus is connected.
  • Self shut-off. The microcontroller turns itself off when the pack is idle and the display is off. The gauge ships itself after a week untouched.

The limits below come from the team's gas-gauge notes (BQ40Z80 Configuration.txt), which explain each setting.

  • A quirk in the gauge config. The exported gauge settings (sbh_bms_config.gg.csv) say 2000 mAh and 37 Wh. That isn't a different pack. The team set the gauge's Scale Factor to 4 so it can report up to 80 A, and every current and capacity in the export is stored divided by 4 (notes; the 2023 report's bring-up steps say bqStudio shows "the actual current / 4"): 2000 mAh × 4 = 8000 mAh, 37 Wh × 4 = 148 Wh, and the 11.25 A overcurrent limit × 4 = 45 A.
  • Two configs, and they differ. The export is from January 2024. The notes are from August 2024 (committed as notes "from changing battery 5"), and most voltage limits changed in between: the export has undervoltage at 3.0 V per cell (notes 3.2 V), permanent failure at 2.2 V for 5 s (notes 3.0 V for 10 s), no charging below 2.2 V (notes 2.9 V), 4.2 V charging (notes 4.15 V) and auto-ship after one day (notes one week). The limits on this page are the notes' values. Which config is on each pack isn't recorded: check in bqStudio.
  • The chemical fuse is off. The gauge can blow a fuse as a last resort, but FUSE_EN is 0 (line 281). The notes record that a permanent-failure lockout fired at RoboSub 2024 (line 10), so they are cautious.
  • Battery IDs. Each housing uses its own serial number (1 to 5) as its CAN ID. The pre-test tool expects IDs 1 and 2, which is only right if housings 1 and 2 are the ones fitted.

Charging and handling

LiPo safety
  • Charge at no more than 5 A. The charge path has a 5 A fuse, and the BMS cuts charging if the current stays over 5 A for 5 s.
  • Charge only between 0 and 50 °C. Discharge only between 0 and 60 °C. The BMS refuses outside these ranges.
  • A pack that read below 2.9 V per cell won't charge at all, and one below 3.0 V per cell for 10 s is locked out for good. Never force-charge a pack the BMS refuses. Charging a deeply flat LiPo is what makes it dangerous.
  • Swap by 17.5 V. The gauge sets an alarm flag there, but nothing on Talos reads or shows it: watch balanced_voltage in state/electrical or the voltage in the ESC telemetry. Don't run the packs down to the 16 V cutoff on purpose.

How the team charges today isn't recorded in the repos. The 2024 report describes the manual procedure: power and data cables on each housing, swapped in a set order. It also describes a custom smart-battery charger the team designed to replace that procedure (SBH_Charger in electric_boogaloo), with fuses on the main rails, overcharge protection and USB-C or barrel-jack input. Ask an electrical lead which one is in use before you charge.

Two packs, one bus

The packs go into the POAC board on two Anderson PP45 connectors. The schematic calls the next stage the "battery balancer", but it is an ideal-diode OR: an LTC4412 controller and two P-channel MOSFETs per pack.

  • Whichever pack has the higher voltage supplies V+. As it drains to the other's voltage, both share the load.
  • Current never flows from one pack into the other, so a full pack and a half-empty one can be plugged in together.
  • A MOSFET instead of a plain diode avoids a diode drop of a few tenths of a volt at tens of amps.
  • Each controller's status pin says whether its pack is supplying. They show up as port_powering and stbd_powering in state/electrical. state/batteries_balanced is true when both are.

The kill switch: what it cuts

The physical kill switch is a Hall-effect sensor in the board cage (2024 report). Swimmers "insert" the switch before a run and pull it to stop Talos. It enters the POAC on J1, along with a second Hall sensor, the AUX switch.

  • It cuts one rail. Three P-channel MOSFETs connect V+ to MOTOR_PWR, which feeds only the two ESC boards. Everything else keeps power.
  • Hardware and software must both allow it. The switch line passes through an analog switch (DG448) that the POAC's microcontroller controls with SOFTWARE_KILL. The MOSFETs turn on only when the switch is in and the microcontroller says "run". A resistor holds SOFTWARE_KILL at "kill" while the chip is booting or dead.
  • The microcontroller also reads the switch on an interrupt (PHYS_KILLSWITCH, kill slot 0) and reports it in state/kill and state/firmware.
  • Power comes back slowly. After any kill the ESCs lose power, so when it returns the ESC boards hold the thrusters off for 5 s (ESC_POWERON_DELAY) while the ESCs start. They treat the ESCs as powered above 9 V.
  • AUX is published as state/aux. The autonomy node meant to use it as a start button (HeadlessInterface.py) is commented out of autonomy.launch.py.

The software side (the four kill slots, who owns which, timeouts) is in Commanding Talos, with a simulator and the kill circuit schematic.

Before you touch a propeller
  • Pull the physical switch. It is the only kill that works with no software running and no tether.
  • A software kill (the viewer's KILL) depends on the microcontroller and the network. Treat it as a convenience, not a lockout.
  • Killed does not mean unpowered. The battery bus, the regulators and every board stay live. Unplug the batteries before working on the wiring.

Regulators and rails

Everything except the thrusters runs from two identical TDK-Lambda i6A4W020A033V modules on the POAC board. Each is a non-isolated DC-DC converter rated 9 to 53 V in and 20 A out, set to its output voltage by one trim resistor. The team's old website says the pair are "capable of providing up to 250W of power".

  • Vreg. The modules don't take V+ directly. It first passes a Schottky diode (MBRB2545CT), which the 2024 report calls a diode "to prevent back-flow current".
  • 15 V (PS1, trim 1.24 kΩ) feeds the Orin, the DVL and the 12 V servo regulator.
  • 5 V (PS2, trim 8.56 kΩ) feeds board logic and small loads.
  • 12 V: a 3 A linear regulator (MIC29300-12) from 15 V, for the servo only.
  • 3.3 V: an LM1117 on each board in the stack and the camera cage, from 5 V. A fault on one board's 3.3 V stays on that board. The battery housings make their own 3.3 V from the pack with buck converters.

Pre-built modules save designing and laying out a 20 A switching converter on a team board.

Power in the camera cage

One 8-pin Mini-Fit cable carries 15 V, 5 V and the internal CAN bus from the POAC to the camera cage breakout board, which distributes them inside the cage. It can switch three loads with MOSFETs, controlled over command/electrical:

CommandHandled byDoes
CYCLE_COMPUTERCamera cage boardOrin power off for 5 s, then on
ENABLE_FANS / DISABLE_FANSCamera cage board, POACFans on or off (on at boot)
ENABLE_LEDS / DISABLE_LEDSCamera cage boardRGB status strip
CYCLE_ROBOT, KILL_ROBOT_POWERBattery housingsPack output off: brief or latched

The DVL gets power and data on one cable. The board injects 15 V and ground on pins 7 and 8 of the DVL's Ethernet cable (silkscreen "DVL SUBCONN"), and passes the data pairs on to the network switch through a second jack. The schematic note reads "Ethernet Power Injection. Route all signals with 100ohms differential". That keeps one connector, not two, on the hull for the DVL.

Thruster power

MOTOR_PWR leaves the POAC on Redcube M4 press-fit terminals (rated 85 A) and goes to each ESC board's own Redcube terminals. Ground returns on separate Redcube terminals. On each ESC board:

  1. Reverse-voltage protection: two P-channel MOSFETs, so a swapped cable doesn't destroy four ESCs.
  2. A 60 A fuse for the board.
  3. One 15 A fuse per ESC, then four APD 80F3 ESCs (rated 15 to 35 V, 80 A).
  4. The motor phases leave on one 12-pin Mini-Fit (three per thruster) to the lid SubConns.

The ESC is rated far above what its fuse allows. The 15 A fuses and the 45 A battery protectors are the real limits, and the next section shows how the thrust limits fit inside them. DShot, telemetry and the ESC firmware patch are in Thrusters and ESCs.

Power budget

The electronics draw a roughly steady load. The thrusters swing from almost nothing while holding still to tens of amps in a hard manoeuvre. Their current comes from Blue Robotics' published T200 performance data: current against thrust at supply voltages from 10 to 20 V, parsed by tools/electrical/t200_data.py.

  • Current grows faster than thrust. At 18 V, 6 N takes 0.8 A, 12 N takes 2.3 A and 24 N takes 7 A. Spreading thrust over more thrusters is cheaper.
  • Reverse costs more: 24 N in reverse takes about 10 A, because the propeller is shaped for forward.
  • The software caps thrust at 24 N per thruster and 73 N total, summed over all eight (individual_thrust_limit and system_thrust_limit in talos.yaml, total_thrust_limit in the MPC model). The comment in mpc.yaml calls 73 N the "hardware budget". Where the number came from isn't recorded.

A real pool run

The untethered identification run of 2026-10-26 lasted 20.5 minutes. Talos held six attitudes, ramped each thruster, ran the null-space patterns, drove surge and sway out and back at 0.2 to 0.7 m/s, heave at 0.1 to 0.3 m/s and yaw turns, then was released at different attitudes. The bag has the MPC's commanded force for every thruster (20 Hz) and the ESC boards' telemetry (about 2 Hz per board). tools/electrical/pool_power.py bins both to 1 s and estimates the current from the T200 data at the measured bus voltage.

    Connectors, penetrators and harness

    Every cable that leaves a housing goes through a SubConn, a wet-mate connector from MacArtney (the MC, HP and D series, per the design reports). Inside, the boards use a small set of connector families, chosen by the current they carry:

    • Cages slide out. The board stack and camera cage are 3D-printed frames on rails, so a cage comes out as a unit. Since 2025 the downward camera connects to its cage through a "quick-connect slide-in adapter", and the gyro through a pogo-pin mount that replaced its fragile pins (2025 report).
    • The harness isn't in the repos. The board designs show every connector, but not the cables between them, such as where the ESC boards' 5 V comes from. When in doubt, check the vehicle, not the screen.

    CAN and Ethernet

    Every board has its own RP2040 microcontroller and talks to the Orin over CAN. There are two buses:

    BusProtocolWhoOn the Orin
    InternalClassic CAN, 1 Mbit/sPOAC (1), ESC boards (2, 3), camera cage (4), LED (6), IVC (8)can0
    ExternalClassic CAN, 250 kbit/sBattery housings (their serial numbers)can1
    • Classic, not FD, in practice. robots/talos.h configures the internal bus for CAN FD with a 5 Mbit/s data phase, but the boards send only classic frames ("TODO: Enable CAN FD") and the Orin brings can0 up as classic CAN at 1 Mbit/s (configure_jetpack.bash). Details in Firmware on the boards, CAN and CANmore.
    • One chain. The internal bus runs Orin HAT → camera cage board → POAC → ESC board 0 → ESC board 1 → IVC → LED board, on the same Mini-Fit cables as the power (2026 report).
    • Termination. A CAN bus needs about 120 Ω at each end and none in the middle. Every board has a slide switch for split termination (two 60.4 Ω resistors). Only the two boards at the ends of the chain should have it on. The designs don't say which switches are on in Talos: check before adding a board.
    • The external bus leaves the Orin HAT through the camera cage board, whose EXT. CAN jacks also carry 15 V. The 2026 diagram connects it to both battery packs.
    • Ethernet joins the Orin, the DVL and the tether through a 5 V switch in the camera cage. Addresses and the tether are in Computers and networks.

    Why CAN

    • Built for the job. CAN is differential, so the signal holds up over longer cables. It detects errors, and many nodes share one pair of wires (2023 report).
    • One RP2040 per board. Why every board got its own microcontroller (a failed I2C run at a 2021 pool test) is told in The circuit boards, One microcontroller on every board.

    Grounding and noise

    No document in the repos describes a grounding scheme for the whole vehicle. These are the design choices that deal with noise and return current:

    • Thruster current has its own path. The kill switch and ESC power use separate Redcube terminals for ESC PWR and ESC GND. The heavy, switching ESC current never runs through the Mini-Fit stack link the logic shares.
    • Isolated supplies for analog. The IVC board's transmit amplifier runs from an isolated ±24 V converter (TRACO THN 15-2425N) and its hydrophone receiver from an isolated ±5 V converter. The usual reason for this is to keep a small analog signal off the ground that the motors share.
    • Differential where it's long. CAN between boards, Ethernet to the DVL (routed as 100 Ω pairs) and RS-422 to the gyro are all differential, so noise picked up equally on both wires cancels.
    • Magnets near the IMU. The torpedo launcher dropped electromagnets for a spring and cam because they disturbed the IMU's magnetometer (2023 report). Keep motors, magnets and high-current cables away from the camera cage.
    • Copper. The 2023 report says Mark 2 moved to 2 oz copper for "higher power designs". The team's JLCPCB 4-layer stackup file below is 1 oz outside and 0.5 oz inside, and the repo doesn't record which boards are ordered with 2 oz.

    Designing a board

    The team designs its boards in Altium Designer, in one repo, osu-uwrt/electric_boogaloo. The Altium setup, the shared RP2040_with_CAN sheet and the order steps are in The circuit boards, From idea to a board. The team's JLCPCB 4-layer design rules (JLCPCB_4Layers.RUL, with the matching stackup) are parsed below.

    Before a new board goes into Talos, check these, all from this chapter:

    • A CAN client ID that's free on its bus (client_ids.h), and termination off unless it is at an end of the chain.
    • Which rail it draws from. V+ swings from 16 to 21 V and sags under thrust. 15 V and 5 V each come from one shared 20 A module.
    • Whether it needs power while killed. If it doesn't need to stop with the thrusters, it shouldn't hang off MOTOR_PWR.
    • Reverse-polarity protection on any input a person plugs in by hand. The ESC and camera cage boards both have it.

    Power-up, step by step

    The order things come alive once the packs go in:

    1. Plug in the packs. A housing's output switches on only once its cable ties DETECT to pack negative (above).
    2. V+ comes up through the ideal-diode OR. The 15 V and 5 V modules start, and every board boots on its own 3.3 V.
    3. The Orin and fans switch on. As soon as its firmware starts, the camera cage board turns on the fans and the Orin's 15 V (main.c). The Orin then boots.
    4. The thrusters stay off. The POAC holds SOFTWARE_KILL at "kill": a resistor while it boots, then its firmware ("Default to kill since safety is not initialized", safety_interface.c). MOTOR_PWR comes on only when the kill switch is in and the software kill allows it, for example when someone enables Talos from the viewer (Commanding Talos).
    5. The ESCs wait. Once MOTOR_PWR is on, the ESC boards hold the thrusters off for 5 s (ESC_POWERUP_DELAY_MS in dshot.h) while the ESCs start.

    The repos don't record a power-down order. Ask an electrical lead before you unplug packs with the Orin running.
