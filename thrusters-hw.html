The vehicle · Chapter 5

Thrusters and ESCs

Eight propellers are the only way Talos moves. This chapter follows one thrust command from the controller to a spinning propeller, and shows how to check the thrusters and what goes wrong with them.

The short version
  • Eight Blue Robotics T200 thrusters in one plane: four outer ones push fore and aft, four inner ones at 45° push up or down and sideways at once.
  • The MPC turns each force into a target RPM and sends it over CAN, 20 times a second, to two ESC boards. Each board holds the RPM of four APD 80F3 ESCs, which it talks to over DShot.
  • Thrusters 0, 3, 4 and 7 carry the opposite-hand propeller, marked type: 1 in talos.yaml. The ESC firmware spins them backwards, so a positive command always pushes the same way.
  • The thruster test spins each one at 300 rpm for 3 s, with the robot enabled on its stand. Check that the right one spins and that it blows water the right way.

How the controller chooses the eight forces is in Thrusters and allocation; thruster power is in Power and wiring, the ESC board in The circuit boards and its code in Firmware on the boards.

Eight thrusters, two jobs

All eight sit in one plane across the robot, within 2 cm of the centre of mass fore and aft, two per corner module:

  • The outer four (V…) point along the robot: surge, plus pitch (top against bottom) and yaw (port against starboard).
  • The inner four (H…) are tilted 45°, each pushing up or down and sideways at once: heave, sway and roll.

Names in talos.yaml read Heave/Vector, Upper/Lower, Port/Starboard: HLP is heave, lower, port. The index (0 to 7) is the position in that list, used by the firmware, CAN and the thruster test.

  • Every motion uses several thrusters. None of the six motions above comes from a single thruster, and most thrusters help with several, so the controller works out all eight at once.
  • Long lever arms. The outer four are about 0.4 m to each side of the centre of mass and 0.24 m above or below it: a small force, a strong torque.
  • One file for the geometry. The MPC's thruster matrix (fossen_model.cpp) and the URDF frames both come from these poses.

From newtons to a spinning propeller

Every 50 ms the MPC turns its eight forces into target RPMs and sends them straight onto CAN, one frame per ESC board, with no ROS in between (send_thruster_cmd_canbus.c). Each board holds those RPMs until the next frame.

The MPC only drives the real thrusters when it runs on real time and the Orin's CAN port can0 exists (configureHardwareOutput), so a simulation can't spin them. The boards run micro-ROS over the same CAN bus: telemetry comes back that way, and they also accept RPMs on command/thruster_rpm (the force-gauge tool uses that). If can0 is down, commands and telemetry both stop.

Pick a thruster and a force to see the numbers at every step.

ESC boards, ESCs and DShot

An ESC (electronic speed controller) turns battery DC into the three phase currents a brushless motor needs. Talos has eight, four on each of two identical boards.

ESC
APD 80F3 (schematic: 15 to 35 V, 80 A), one 15 A fuse each
Board
60 A input fuse; power from the POAC's MOTOR_PWR, which the kill switch cuts
Which thrusters
Board 0: thrusters 0–3, board 1: 4–7, set by a solder jumper; one firmware image fits both

DShot

Instead of a PWM pulse, which needs calibrating and picks up noise, DShot sends a 16-bit digital frame: 11 bits of throttle, a telemetry-request bit and a 4-bit checksum. In bidirectional DShot the ESC answers each frame on the same wire with how long one electrical turn of the motor took. The T200's motor has 7 pole pairs, so 7 electrical turns make one propeller turn; that gives the RPM the board's loop runs on (dshot_get_rpm).

Both directions use "3D mode", where the value range is split in two: 48 to 1047 turns the motor one way, 1049 to 2047 the other, 0 stops it (dshot_update_thrusters). Step 5 of the figure above shows a real frame.

The ESC firmware patch

Stock APD firmware could only reverse in DShot with a "change direction" command and a long pause, and PWM would have lost the RPM reply. So a team member patched 3D mode into APD's firmware (version 2.3.0) with Ghidra (README). An unpatched ESC won't reverse, so a replacement ESC needs the patched firmware and Talos's settings:

  • Firmware. The patched binary is not in the repository, because APD doesn't publish its firmware. It is in the team's Teams files (navionics channel, "Mk 2 stuff/ESCs") with APD's tools. If that copy is lost, the patch itself, esc_firmware_patching/apd_f3x_2.3.0_3ddshot.patch, applies to APD's stock APD_F3X_Standard_2.3.0.bin. Flash it with APD's flashing tool through the ESC's FLASH CONN header.
  • Settings. Load Talos_Config.cfg with APD Configurator (main rows below).
  • Known quirk. The author notes the patched ESCs still stumble when starting from rest and when switching direction, probably electrical noise on the board.

Why an RPM loop

On the team's force gauge (below) the same throttle gave different thrust on different thrusters and battery levels; the same RPM gave nearly the same thrust (2024 design report). So each board holds an RPM instead. For each thruster it starts from a feed-forward guess, the throttle a fitted curve says that RPM needs, and adds a PI correction: one part proportional to the RPM error, one to its running sum. The result is clamped to ±725 of the ESC's ±999 (Firmware on the boards).

Reversed propellers

A spinning propeller twists the robot the other way; if all eight spun the same way, every surge would also roll Talos. So half carry the opposite-hand propeller. talos.yaml marks them type: 1, thrusters 0, 3, 4 and 7; its comment calls the type "the attack direction of the thruster", which must match the way the thruster spins in the thruster test (talos.yaml).

The ESC firmware spins exactly the type: 1 thrusters backwards (mask 0b1001 on each board, core1_init), so every positive command pushes along the arrow above. The catch: the mask is hard-coded and nothing reads type, so nothing checks that the two agree.

Swapping a thruster or propeller

  • Same position, same ESC slot, same propeller hand as the one you take off (look before you remove it). Which hand (CW or CCW) goes on which index isn't written down in the repos.
  • If an index's propeller type must change, change its type in talos.yaml and the mask in core1.c, and flash both ESC boards.
  • Then run the thruster test (below) and check the wash on every thruster.

Thrust, RPM and current

The MPC's force-to-RPM is a fit to Blue Robotics' T200 test-stand data at six voltages (spreadsheet, 2019). Thrust grows with the square of speed:

|F| = K_T\,\rho\,D^4 \left(\frac{n}{60}\right)^2, \qquad K_T = a + b\,n

with n in rpm, \rho = 998.2 kg/m³ and D = 0.076 m. Fitted to every sheet: forward a = 0.3964, b = 1.48×10⁻⁵; reverse a = 0.3114, b = 1.25×10⁻⁵ (fit_thrust_curve.py). The MPC inverts this with Newton's method (forceToRpm).

  • Command RPM, not throttle. 1800 µs gives 33.5 N at 16 V but 38.9 N at 20 V; at a given RPM the voltage doesn't matter.
  • Reverse is weaker: about 20 % less thrust at the same speed.
  • Current is expensive. It rises roughly with thrust to the power 1.5, so two thrusters at 12 N draw less than one at 24 N.

Measuring thrust: the force gauge

In 2023 the team built a force gauge: a T200 on a lever arm in a tank, with a load cell read by an Arduino (2024 design report, appendix D). thruster_calibration_tools steps one thruster's raw command, waits 5 s per step, and logs command, RPM, current and force to a CSV.

  • It publishes raw values on command/thruster_rpm, so the ESC board must be in raw mode (dshot_tune/set_raw; raises the RAW_MODE fault).
  • The launch file defaults to the old robot, Tempest: pass robot:=talos. Five runs from 2023-10-10 are in riptide_mpc/data.

The runs read 70 to 76 % of Blue Robotics' thrust at the same RPM. In October 2026 the MPC dropped the old controller's force-to-RPM curves for the Blue Robotics data: the commit says the old curves had forward and reverse swapped and came from a low-reading gauge (03c8ac1). If you rebuild the gauge, calibrate the load cell with known weights first.

What comes back: telemetry

Every 500 ms each ESC board publishes state/thrusters/telemetry (start_thruster_num 0 or 4):

FieldWhat it tells you
disabled_flagsWhy the thrusters are held at zero: one bit per reason, listed below
escs_poweredFalse when the board has no MOTOR_PWR (under 9 V): killed, or its 60 A fuse is blown
vcc_voltageThe ESC supply as the board measures it
esc_telemetry[i].rpmMotor RPM from the ESC's UART packet, in steps of about 14 rpm (100 electrical rpm)
.thruster_readyFalse after 20 missed RPM replies, which also zeroes that thruster
.dshot_offline_cntNon-zero means that thruster was switched off for a while
  • Slow: one message per board every 500 ms (2 Hz), and the RPM inside can be up to 0.16 s old. Too coarse to see a spin-up.
  • The sign is the motor's: the type: 1 thrusters show a positive target as negative RPM.
  • The current isn't usable: near 0.04 A in pool runs (Power and wiring). Use the curves above.

Why a thruster won't spin: disabled_flags

Any set bit holds all four thrusters on that board at zero (core1.h):

  1. 0
    SAFETY_KILLED

    Talos is killed. The usual one on the bench and between runs.

  2. 1
    ESC_BOARD_OFF

    No ESC power (MOTOR_PWR under 9 V): killed, or the board's 60 A fuse is blown. Usually set together with bit 0.

  3. 2
    ESC_POWERON_DELAY

    Power came back less than 5 s ago; the ESCs are still starting up. Normal after every enable.

  4. 3
    TGT_RPM_STALE

    No targets for 500 ms: the MPC isn't running or isn't sending on CAN.

  5. 4
    TGT_RPM_STALE_PENALTY

    Targets stopped while the thrusters were spinning; the board holds them off for 1 s more and raises a fault.

  6. 5
    CONTROLLER_UNCONFIGURED

    The RPM loop's gains were never set, so the board won't run it.

The thruster test

thruster_test_canbus (source) spins thrusters 0 to 7 in turn at 300 rpm for 3 s each, until Ctrl-C, which sends zeros. How to run it is in Pool test day. Look for:

  • The right one spins. At Testing Thruster 2..., HUS should turn. Anything else means swapped motor cables or ESCs.
  • It pushes the right way: along its arrow above, blowing water the opposite way. Watch a thread in the wash. Telemetry can't check this.
  • Telemetry matches. Only the ESC under test shows about 300 rpm (negative for the type: 1 thrusters); pretest guided checks this.

300 rpm is only about 0.33 N, so Talos won't move on its stand, but a turning propeller can still catch fingers and cables: keep clear. Out of the water, keep runs short.

Before you run it
  • Stop the controller first (control_system.launch.py): the MPC also writes to CAN, and on an enabled robot it spins the thrusters on its own.
  • Robot on its stand, hands, cables and tools clear of every propeller, someone at the kill switch.
  • Then give the ESCs power: kill switch in, robot enabled in the Nereus viewer, and wait 5 s (ESC_POWERON_DELAY). Killed, nothing spins.
  • Pulling the kill switch cuts ESC power even while the program commands; pretest guided checks every thruster stops within 1 s.

What the thrusters do in the pool

The best recording is the untethered run of 2026-10-26, a system-identification run (System identification): six holds (level twice, pitch ±, roll ±), a ramp on each thruster, null-space patterns (thrusters pushing against each other), speed runs up to 0.7 m/s (the normal limit is 0.6 m/s) and five releases with every thruster off. The figure drops the releases, ramps and null-space patterns, where the thrusters were told what to do, leaving the 13.3 of 20.6 minutes where the MPC chose.

  • Single thrusters rarely saturate: at 24 N under 0.2 % of the time, only outer ones, only in surge runs.
  • The total limit binds in the fast runs. The sum was at the 73 N cap 8 % of the time (median 25 N), about 70 % of that in the speed runs (sway from 0.35 m/s, surge from 0.5 m/s, heave down at 0.3 m/s) and the rest diving and re-levelling around the releases. The MPC then scales all eight down together (Thrusters and allocation).
  • Holding still takes thrust. In the two level holds (20 s) the thrusters pushed about 1.7 N down and 2.6 N·m nose-down: Talos floats up a little and wants to pitch nose-up (buoyancy and trim). That is why rows 0 and 1 (upper outer) lean forward and rows 6 and 7 (lower outer) lean reverse.
  • Time near zero. The inner thrusters sat within ±0.5 N for 10 to 23 % of the time. The ESCs take about 0.8 s to start a motor from rest (2026-10-03 testing; see the start-up quirk in the patch notes). hardware.spin_bias in mpc.yaml can keep every thruster turning with no net force, but is off.
