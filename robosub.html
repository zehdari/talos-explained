RoboSub and the course

Talos is built to score points at one competition. This chapter explains that competition, walks the 2026 course task by task, and shows which parts of Talos each task uses.

The short version
  • RoboSub is RoboNation's international student competition for autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs). In July 2026 UWRT placed 4th in its Autonomy Challenge with Talos.
  • During a run nobody may talk to the vehicle. It must pass the gate first; after that, any task in any order. Surfacing outside the octagon ends the run.
  • The 2026 course: a gate where you pick a role, a slalom, bins with magnet lights, a torpedo board, an octagon with a table of objects to sort, and back through the gate.
  • Talos runs all of it from one behaviour tree, RepairCompMpcTree, as the Survey & Repair role, flying between positions in its map of the course.
  • A second, separate competition is judged on land: a report, website, video, presentation and system assessment (860 points), ranked on its own.

What RoboSub is

RoboSub is run by RoboNation with the US Office of Naval Research. Student teams (high school or university, at least 75 % students) build an AUV and bring it to one pool for a week. Each team may enter one or two vehicles. The pool is 2.1 m (7 ft) deep. Everything here comes from the RoboSub 2026 Team Handbook unless a link says otherwise.

Two competitions run side by side and are ranked separately:

  • The Autonomy Challenge: runs in the pool, scored by judges watching the vehicle.
  • Design Documentation: what the team writes, films and presents about how and why it built the vehicle.

Rules that shape the vehicle

A vehicle may not enter the water until it passes a static safety inspection. These rules explain a lot of Talos's design:

Fully autonomous
All decisions on board; no communication with people or off-board computers during a run (a team's two vehicles may talk to each other, which scores as inter-vehicle communication). So the computer, cameras and the mission tree all live inside Talos (Computers and networks).
Submerged
Everything attached must go under with the vehicle. No tether in a scored run.
Kill switch
Clearly marked, easy for a diver to pull, cuts battery power to all propulsion, and the props must not start spinning again when it is put back (Commanding Talos).
Shrouded props
Commercial shrouded thrusters count; Talos's are Blue Robotics T200s (Thrusters and ESCs).
Floats when killed
Positively buoyant by at least 0.5 % of its mass with the kill switch pulled (Hull, frame and layout).
Size
Fits in a 0.9 × 0.9 × 1.8 m box.
Weight
Over 60 kg is disqualified; over 38 kg costs points; under 38 kg earns 4.4 points per kg below 38. Talos's config gives 32.4 kg dry (weighed for the October 2026 pool tests, claw fitted), about +24 points (the official number is the competition's own scale).
Batteries
Sealed, at most 60 V open-circuit, never charged inside a sealed housing (Power and wiring).
Markers, torpedoes
At most two of each, each within 51 × 51 × 152 mm and 0.91 kg, marked with the team's name or emblem (Torpedoes, dropper and claw).

How a run works

  • Practice and qualifying. Teams book 30-minute practice slots. A team qualifies by passing through the gate autonomously, or ahead of time with a prequalification video (below).
  • Semi-finals. Two days, one 20-minute slot per day, on two different courses. The first 5 minutes are setup with the vehicle out of the water. A new run forfeits the previous run's points, so only the last run counts.
  • Finals. The top teams, all scores reset. The best run counts.
  • Start. The vehicle starts on the surface and must submerge before it leaves the start zone. For extra points the team can ask for a coin flip that decides which way it faces.
  • Gate first. Every other task needs the gate passed first. After that tasks can be done in any order, and a row's score only goes up: revisiting a task keeps the better result.
  • Breaching. Surfacing inside the octagon is a task. Breaking the surface anywhere else ends the run.
  • Several courses. The pool holds practice courses A, B1, B2, C1, C2 and D. Semi-finals run on A and D; the finals combine A, B1 and B2. Task positions differ between courses, and no three elements are ever in a line.
  • Attempts. Each task has an imaginary 3 m radius sphere around it. Entering it starts an attempt; leaving ends it.
  • Time bonus. A vehicle that passes one slalom row, drops one marker in a bin (or fires one torpedo through the board) and surfaces fully in the octagon earns 100 points per minute left on the clock.

Talos's detector has no path-marker class: the tree flies straight to positions in its map instead. The pingers are used once, to choose the order of the torpedo and table tasks.

What each task uses on Talos

One tree for the whole run

On competition day the operator starts one behaviour tree, RepairCompMpcTree.xml (about 4 100 lines). Its root sets repair_mission = 1 and runs CompMissionTreeMpc; RescueCompMpcTree.xml is the same mission for the other role. In order:

  1. Wait for the kill switch to be inserted, then 5 s more. Lock the map, reload and disarm the actuators.
  2. Dive in place to 0.75 m and hold the starting heading (the tree asks for 0.56 m; PrimitiveMovePosition never goes shallower than 0.75 m).
  3. Gate, slalom, pinger. The pinger check sets pinger_is_table: true means table first, false means torpedo first.
  4. Torpedo and table, in that order or the other.
  5. Bins, which ends with the magnet lights.
  6. Home through the gate, then stop the controller.
The pinger order looks backwards

The tree listens at the torpedo stop, then at the table stop. As written, a louder torpedo pinger sends Talos to the table first, which looks inverted against the rule (unconfirmed: it may be a bug). Until it is settled, don't count on the pinger bonus. Details in The pinger picks the order.

Four design choices run through it:

  • The role is chosen by the operator, not detected. RepairCompMpcTree sets repair_mission = 1 and RescueCompMpcTree sets it to 0; that is the only behavioural difference between the two files. For the role coin flip, the team starts the tree that matches the flip.
  • Every task is wrapped in ForceSuccess. A task that times out or fails a check doesn't end the run; the tree moves on to the next one. Points only go up, so giving up on a task costs nothing but the time spent.
  • Transits are paths, not searches. Between tasks the tree builds a list of waypoints (pre_torpedo_pose, pre_bin_pose, …) and hands it to the MPC with FollowPath, between 0.75 m and 2.5 m deep. Those waypoints are priors in the mapping config, measured before the run (Frames, targets and the mission tree, Where to go: profiles and paths). The map above uses the competition layout from the built (installed) config; the branch's config.yaml currently has the table moved for pool testing.
  • The cameras refine, the map decides. Each task starts from its prior position, looks with the camera that task needs, and lets mapping move the prop before Talos commits to a shot, drop or grab (The course map).

The tree's nodes and how to write one are in Behaviour trees and The competition mission.

How it scores

Judges score the run live. Nereus implements the same 2026 rules (robosub_2026.cpp, points in tasks.yaml), so a simulated run shows a scorecard in the viewer. A few rows are left to the judges: pinger awards (no acoustics in Nereus yet), maintaining control through the gate, weight, and inter-vehicle communication.

Three things stand out. The octagon and table is the largest single task, up to 7 700 points, and almost all of it needs a way to pick things up: that is why Talos got a claw for 2026. The torpedo's 1 400-point sequence bonus needs both shots through the right holes, large first. And the two coin flips at the gate add 450 points for a vehicle that can start facing any way and take either role.

Prequalification

Before the event a team may qualify from home with an unbroken video of one autonomous run on a simple course: a 2 m horizontal gate 1 m under the surface, and a vertical marker 10 m beyond it. The vehicle starts submerged 3 m behind the gate, passes through, circles the marker and comes back through, without breaking the surface. Teams that pass go straight to the semi-finals. Talos prequalified for 2026 in March.

The current version of that run is PreQualMpcTree.xml: dive to 1 m, then a single path through the gate, out 3 m to the side of the pole, a half circle behind it while facing it, and back through the gate nose first. The gate and pole are just two priors in the mapping config, prequal_gate and prequal_pole, 10 m apart.

The team's robots, and their names in the code

UWRT started with ROVs and switched to AUVs for RoboSub in 2015; the software subteam was founded then. The stack is still called Riptide after the first AUV, which is why every package is riptide_* (team website). Every AUV since is below; from 2025 the team also built a second, smaller vehicle beside the main one.

Old names survive in the code. You will meet them in:

  • Vehicle configs. riptide_descriptions/config has talos.yaml, tempest.yaml, puddles.yaml, liltank.yaml and simulator.yaml. Launch files take a robot argument and load the matching file, so puddles_ekf.yaml or a tempest default in an old teleop launch is history, not a second robot.
  • liltank. liltank.yaml (added May 2025) describes a 22.7 kg vehicle with two thrusters and an active ballast tank, and only a forward camera. That matches the tracked, ballast-driven SS Flip Barker, so liltank is almost certainly its code name. LiltankCompTree.xml is the tank's own run: it dives, then waits for Talos (WaitForTalos). DeployTankTree.xml is Talos's side: it lines up talos/hitch_link (the hitch in talos.yaml) over the table to drop the tank there. The release step itself is still a placeholder in that tree.
  • Triton, the 2026 second vehicle, runs a separate stack, Mercury. For 2026 the team planned it for the gate, the slalom and the return home, talking to Talos acoustically (inter-vehicle communication).
