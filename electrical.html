Electrical · Chapter 1

Power and wiring

Two LiPo packs feed one battery bus. A kill switch cuts the thrusters and nothing else. Two regulator modules make 15 V and 5 V for the electronics; only the LED drivers and the IVC transmitter take the battery bus directly. One CAN bus, chained board to board, carries the data.

The short version
  • Two sealed battery housings, each a 5S LiPo (18.5 V nominal, 148 Wh). An ideal-diode OR on the POAC (power and actuator board, the bottom board of the stack) lets whichever pack is higher carry the bus.
  • The kill switch is the handle on the port tube's bow window. It only cuts MOTOR_PWR, the ESC supply. The Orin, sensors and boards stay on, so a kill never reboots anything.
  • Two 20 A regulator modules make 15 V (Orin, DVL) and 5 V (logic, switch, fans). Only the LED drivers and the IVC transmitter run straight from the battery bus. Every board makes its own 3.3 V. The field called twelve_volt_voltage is really the 15 V rail.
  • Data: one CAN bus (1 Mbit/s) runs through every board to the Orin (can0). The batteries sit on a second, slower bus. Ethernet joins the Orin, the DVL and the tether.
  • Thrusters dominate the peak draw: about 26 A at the peak of an aggressive pool run, estimated from T200 data.

This chapter follows the power from the packs to every load, then the data wiring, for the whole vehicle. Each board has its own page in The circuit boards, the packs in Batteries and charging, the firmware in Firmware on the boards. The facts come from the board designs (electric_boogaloo), the firmware (titan_firmware) and the team's design reports; inferred facts are marked.

The whole system, one step at a time

Four views, one path each: thruster power, the regulated rails, the boards on the CAN bus, and what plugs into the Orin.

The batteries

Talos carries two identical smart battery housings, one on each side, outside the hull. Each holds a MaxAmps 8000 mAh 5S LiPo: five cells in series, 18.5 V nominal, 148 Wh. A BMS (battery-management) board in each housing switches the pack off on undervoltage, overvoltage, overcurrent or temperature: it allows 45 A for 5 s, or 80 A for 2 s (BMS settings).

Two things matter for the wiring. A housing's output stays off until its cable ties the DETECT pin to pack negative, so a housing on the table has no voltage on its pins. And each housing reports its voltage, current and charge on the external CAN bus, but the Orin's agent for that bus has been commented out since 2026-10-27, "until poac/batteries happy" (copro_agent.launch.py), so battery telemetry doesn't reach ROS at the moment.

LiPo safety
  • Charge at no more than 5 A, and only between 0 and 50 °C.
  • Swap packs by 17.5 V. Nothing on Talos warns you: watch balanced_voltage in state/electrical.
  • Never force-charge a pack the BMS refuses. A deeply flat LiPo is the dangerous one.
  • How to charge, step by step: Batteries and charging.

Two packs, one bus. The packs plug into the POAC on Anderson PP45 connectors and meet at the ideal-diode OR (per pack, an LTC4412 controller and two MOSFETs, a transistor used as a switch): whichever pack is higher carries V+, the battery bus, and no current flows from one pack into the other, so a full pack and a half-empty one can go in together. Each half reports whether its pack is supplying, as port_powering and stbd_powering in state/electrical.

The kill switch: what it cuts

The kill switch is a handle on the port tube's bow window. A Hall-effect sensor (a chip that detects a magnet) on the board stack's fan block senses the handle's magnet through the window (2024 report). Seat the handle before a run; pull it off to cut thruster power. The sensor's line enters the POAC on J1, with a second Hall sensor, the AUX switch.

  • It cuts one rail. Three P-channel MOSFETs connect V+ to MOTOR_PWR, which feeds only the two ESC boards. Everything else keeps power.
  • Hardware and software must both allow it. The switch line passes through an analog switch (DG448) that the POAC's microcontroller controls with SOFTWARE_KILL, so the MOSFETs turn on only when the handle is in and the microcontroller says "run". A resistor holds "kill" while the chip is booting or dead. The microcontroller also reports the switch in state/kill.
  • Power comes back slowly. When MOTOR_PWR returns, the ESC boards hold the thrusters off for 5 s while the ESCs start (ESC_POWERUP_DELAY_MS in dshot.h).

The software side (the four kill slots and their timeouts) is in Commanding Talos, with a simulator.

Before you touch a propeller
  • Pull the kill switch handle. It is the only kill that works with no software running and no tether.
  • A software kill (the viewer's KILL) depends on the microcontroller and the network. Treat it as a convenience, not a lockout.
  • Killed does not mean unpowered: every board stays live. Unplug the batteries before working on the wiring.

Regulators and rails

Apart from the thrusters, only the LED drivers and the IVC transmitter run straight from V+. Everything else runs from two identical TDK-Lambda i6A4W020A033V modules on the POAC: DC-DC converters, 9 to 53 V in, 20 A out. One trim resistor sets each module's output, so the same part makes both 15 V and 5 V.

  • Vreg. V+ reaches the modules through a Schottky diode (a diode with a low voltage drop), there "to prevent back-flow current" (2024 report).
  • 12 V: a 3 A LDO (low-dropout linear regulator) from 15 V, for the servo connector on the lid.
  • 3.3 V: an LDO on each board, from 5 V, so a fault on one board's 3.3 V stays on that board.

Power in the camera cage

One 8-pin Mini-Fit cable carries 15 V, 5 V and the internal CAN bus from the POAC to the camera cage board, which hands them out inside the cage and switches three loads on command/electrical:

CommandDoes
CYCLE_COMPUTEROrin power off for 5 s, then on
ENABLE_FANS / DISABLE_FANSFans on or off (on at boot; the POAC switches the stack fan too)
ENABLE_LEDS / DISABLE_LEDSRGB status strip

The DVL gets power and data on one cable: the board puts 15 V and ground on pins 7 and 8 of the DVL's Ethernet cable and passes the data pairs to the network switch through a second jack, so the DVL needs one hull connector, not two.

Thruster power

MOTOR_PWR leaves the POAC on Redcube M4 press-fit terminals (rated 85 A) and goes to each ESC board's own Redcube terminals. Ground returns on separate terminals. On each ESC board:

  1. Reverse-voltage protection: two P-channel MOSFETs, so a swapped cable doesn't destroy four ESCs.
  2. A 60 A fuse for the board.
  3. One 15 A fuse per ESC, then four APD 80F3 ESCs (rated 15 to 35 V, 80 A).
  4. The motor phases leave on one 12-pin Mini-Fit (three per thruster) to the lid SubConns.

The 15 A fuses and the packs' 45 A limit, not the ESC rating, are the real limits. DShot and the ESC firmware are in Thrusters and ESCs.

Power budget

The electronics draw a roughly steady load; the thrusters swing from almost nothing to tens of amps. Their current comes from Blue Robotics' T200 performance data (current against thrust, 10 to 20 V).

  • Current grows faster than thrust. At 18 V, 6 N takes 0.8 A, 12 N takes 2.3 A and 24 N takes 7 A. Spreading thrust over more thrusters is cheaper.
  • Reverse costs more: 24 N in reverse takes about 10 A, because the propeller is shaped for forward.
  • The software caps thrust at 24 N per thruster and 73 N in total, summed over all eight (talos.yaml). The MPC's config calls 73 N the "hardware budget"; where the number came from isn't recorded.

A real pool run

The untethered identification run of 2026-10-26 lasted 20.5 minutes: holds at six attitudes, a ramp on each thruster, null-space tests and drives out and back on each axis. The bag has the commanded force for every thruster and the ESC telemetry; tools/electrical/pool_power.py estimates the current from the T200 data at the measured bus voltage.

    CAN and Ethernet

    Every board has its own RP2040 microcontroller and talks to the Orin over CAN, a two-wire differential bus that many boards share, so "only power and two data lines travel between electronics boards" (2023 report). There are two buses:

    BusSpeedWho (CAN id)Orin
    Internal1 Mbit/sPOAC (1), ESC boards (2, 3), camera cage (4), LED (6), IVC (8)can0
    External250 kbit/sBattery housings: port 1, starboard 2 (set by a side-detect pin)can1
    • Classic CAN, not CAN FD. robots/talos.h configures the internal bus for CAN FD, but the boards send only classic frames ("TODO: Enable CAN FD") and the Orin brings can0 up as classic CAN (configure_jetpack.bash). More in Firmware on the boards.
    • One bus, chained. The internal bus runs from the Orin HAT through every board to the LED board, on the same Mini-Fit cables as the power (2026 report), as step 3 shows.
    • Termination. A CAN bus needs 120 Ω at each end and none in the middle (termination). Every board has a slide switch for it. The designs don't say which switches are on in Talos: check before adding a board.
    • Ethernet joins the Orin, the DVL and the tether through a 5 V network switch (Computers and networks).

    Adding a board

    How the team designs and orders boards is in Designing a board. Before a new board goes into Talos, check:

    • A CAN client ID that's free on its bus (client_ids.h), and termination off unless it sits at an end of the chain.
    • Which rail it draws from. V+ swings from 16 to 21 V and sags under thrust. 15 V and 5 V each come from one shared 20 A module.
    • Whether it needs power while killed. Only loads that should stop with the thrusters belong on MOTOR_PWR.
    • Reverse-polarity protection on any input a person plugs in by hand. The ESC and camera cage boards both have it.
    • Keep heavy current off the logic cables. The ESC boards get power and ground on their own Redcube terminals, so thruster current never runs through the stack-link cables.
    • Keep motors, magnets and high-current cables away from the camera cage. The launcher dropped its electromagnets because they disturbed the IMU's compass (2023 report).

    Power-up, step by step

    1. Plug in the packs. A housing's output switches on only once its cable ties DETECT to pack negative (above).
    2. V+ comes up through the ideal-diode OR. The 15 V and 5 V modules start, and every board boots on its own 3.3 V.
    3. The Orin and fans switch on. The camera cage board turns them on as soon as its firmware starts (main.c), and the Orin boots.
    4. The thrusters stay off until the handle is in and someone enables Talos, for example from the viewer: until then the POAC holds SOFTWARE_KILL at "kill" (safety_interface.c).
    5. The ESCs wait 5 s once MOTOR_PWR is on, then the thrusters can spin.

    No power-down order is recorded: ask an electrical lead before you unplug packs with the Orin running.
