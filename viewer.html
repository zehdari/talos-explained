Running Talos · Chapter 3

The Nereus viewer

One program on the operator's laptop shows Talos in 3D, enables and kills it, drives it, starts its trees and shows what its cameras see. It works the same against the simulator and against the real robot in the pool.

The short version
  • nereus-viewer is the team's operator interface for sim and pool alike. It replaced RViz.
  • Its Enable / KILL button owns software-kill slot 1. The viewer starts killed, and Enable latches, so a run survives the tether coming off.
  • Panels around a 3D pool view: Motion (drive), Autonomy (run trees), Mapping, Vision, two camera cards, and Recording, Bagging, Electrical and Actuators.
  • It talks to Talos only through ROS 2 topics, services and actions, over Zenoh from the laptop.
  • Ctrl+P finds every control and takes typed moves like turn left 45 forward 2. Enable and KILL are never on the keyboard.

What it is

Nereus (osu-uwrt/nereus) has three parts: nereus-sim, the simulator (Chapter 2); nereus-viewer, this chapter; and the YAML packs that describe the robot, pool and course. The viewer and the simulator share no code. Both only talk ROS 2, so the viewer can't tell a simulated Talos from the real one, apart from a few sim-only tools.

Runs on
The operator laptop (op-top at the pool), or any team laptop with the robot workspace built. Never on the Orin.
Built with
C++, OpenGL and Dear ImGui, from the Nereus repo with ./build.sh (README).
Started by
sim.launch.py in sim, robot.launch.py at the pool (below).
Configured in
talos_uwrt_panels.yaml (panels, topics, toolbar) and talos_uwrt_host.yaml (window, pose source, overlays).
Manual
docs/viewer.md, and F1 inside the program.

Why not RViz? The team used to run RViz with its own riptide_rviz panels. The viewer took those jobs over one by one: the control panel's kill switch on slot 1, the electrical panel, the bag presets, and the mapping meshes "drawn like RViz" from riptide_rviz/config/markers.yaml. It adds what RViz couldn't do: the same window drives the simulator and the robot, shows simulator truth beside the estimate, scores runs, and edits the prior map. RViz is history now. Don't run its control panel next to the viewer: two senders on slot 1 count as competing operators.

A tour of the window

Every window can be moved, docked, tabbed or closed, but everyone starts from the Standard layout below: the Motion panel and the Mapping / Vision / Autonomy tabs on the left, the pool view in the middle, and the camera cards and the other panels on the right. Step through it, or tap a number.

Enable and KILL

Whether Talos may run is decided in firmware, on the POAC board, from four kill-switch slots. The full rules are in Commanding Talos. The short form: the robot is killed if any slot asserts a kill, or if a slot that promised heartbeats goes quiet for 500 ms (kill_switch.c). Slot 0 is the physical switch on the hull. Slot 1 is the viewer's.

  • It starts killed. From its first tick the viewer sends a KillSwitchReport on command/software_kill every 50 ms: slot 1, asserting kill unless enabled (report()). Opening the viewer kills a running robot until someone presses Enable. Measured in sim: 20.3 Hz.
  • Enable latches. The report carries switch_needs_update = false: "enable latches … untethered runs continue". Close the viewer or unplug the tether, and slot 1 stays clear. Untethered, the physical switch is the only kill.
  • One operator per slot. Each viewer signs its reports viewer_<host>_<pid>_<n>. If anyone else reports on slot 1, the viewer drops its enable at once ("Another operator owns the enable switch") and refuses Enable until that sender has been quiet for 1 s.
  • KILL does three things: slot 1 asserts, the controller gets a DISABLED command at the last commanded pose, and a running tree is stopped (composition.cpp).
  • The viewer never kills on its own. If its window freezes for 0.75 s, the pose goes stale for 1 s, or a mode request hangs for 3 s, it only releases manual control: Talos keeps holding its last command (releaseLocked).
  • Never from the keyboard. Enable and KILL are buttons in the command bar (and at the top of Motion). They aren't in Ctrl+P and have no shortcut.
At the pool
  • Enabled with the switch in means the thrusters can spin. Whoever holds the physical switch watches the robot, not the screen (the kill switch rules).
  • KILL only works while its messages reach the robot. Untethered, or with the link down, it does nothing. Pull the switch instead.
  • One viewer at a time. A second laptop running the viewer against the same robot fights the first one for slot 1.

Driving with the Motion panel

Motion is the manual controls. Every command goes out as a pair of ControllerCommand messages on controller/linear and controller/angular, in the map frame, the same topics a tree's nodes use.

  1. Enable, from the panel or the command bar.
  2. Pick Position. The viewer first calls controller_overseer's setTeleop service (with the MPC it just answers yes), then starts sending. riptide_mpc only acts on Position and Velocity commands (mpc.cpp), so leave Feedforward alone: it's the old controllers' mode, with pose feedback off.
  3. Give a target, in one of four ways:
    • Type numbers in the Target column and press Command. Current copies the actual pose in first.
    • Drag the pose gizmo in the pool view: arrows move, rings turn, Esc mid-drag puts it back.
    • Dive in place: hold x, y and heading, level, at dive_z = −0.75 m. Zero roll & pitch levels Talos where it is.
    • Drive with keys: W/S, A/D, Space/Shift, Q/E move the target one step per press, 25 cm or 15° to start ([ ] change it, 5 cm / 2° to 1 m / 45°). Holding a key doesn't repeat.

The table reads Actual (from TF), Commanded, their Error, and your Target. It also tracks commands it didn't send: during a tree the Commanded column follows the tree's setpoints, so the gizmo shows where autonomy is taking Talos. The viewer broadcasts the command as the TF frame ghost/base_link, too.

Typed moves

The fastest way to move Talos is Ctrl+P and a sentence: forward 2, turn right 45, z -1.5, go 15.5 2.8 -1.4 0, or several in a row. Moves start from the pose last commanded, not from where Talos is, so they add up while it's still travelling. The box reads the move back before you press Enter. It only sends while you could press Command: enabled, Position control, a fresh pose, and no tree or other operator driving.

Running a tree

The Autonomy panel is a client of doTask, riptide_autonomy's tree runner (Running a tree):

  • The picker lists the trees from the autonomy/list_trees service, the XML files in riptide_autonomy's trees/ folder. Refresh asks again after you add one.
  • Start sends an ExecuteTree goal on autonomy/run_tree. It's greyed out unless Motion is enabled, has a fresh pose, has no request pending and has no competing operator (mayStart). Enable first.
  • While it runs, Motion says "Autonomy owns motion" and locks, and the execution stack (autonomy/tree_stack) lists the nodes running now, outermost first: MISSION › CompMissionTreeMpc › COMP › … The panel also notices trees started by someone else, for example from a terminal.
  • Stop cancels the goal; KILL stops it too. Afterwards Motion says "Autonomy ended / Command to resume": manual control comes back only when you send a command.
  • Untethered runs. If the tree ends while the tether is off, its result never arrives. The panel settles the goal from the action's status topic once the link is back, so Start frees up without a restart.

The competition tree waits for the kill switch first: it polls state/kill until Talos is enabled, then waits 6 s more before moving (Commanding Talos). In sim, Enable plays the part of the switch going in.

Seeing what Talos sees

Camera cards

One card per robot camera: Forward camera (ffc) and Downward camera (dfc), both ZED X Mini at 1920×1200. The header shows the model, resolution and the rate actually arriving.

  • RGB / Depth switches the image. The cards show the compressed rectified colour image, <cam>/zed_node/rgb/image_rect_color/compressed.
  • Truth / ROS (sim only): Truth is the viewer's own render from the true camera pose, fast and noiseless. ROS is the images the stack actually receives. Judge the detector on ROS.
  • Main view puts that camera in the pool view, to look through it with the course and overlays drawn.

Overlays in the pool view

Labels
Names on the course elements.
TF
Axes for every frame. Its arrow opens the TF frames window: the frame tree, axis length, show or hide each frame.
MPC path
The MPC's predicted path (controller/mpc/predicted_path, 30 stages × 50 ms = 1.5 s ahead), plus any follow_path plan (Control, Chapter 4).
Covariance
Mapping's estimate of each prop as an ellipsoid (1–3 σ), from mapping/<object> (Perception, Chapter 6). At pool scale they are often too small to see.
Thrust
Each thruster's commanded force from thruster_forces as a red arrow, 0.05 m per newton.
Detections
The detector's markers, where each object was seen (Vision panel). In sim, Placement draws them from simulator truth, from the estimate (cyan outline), or both.
Point clouds
Ticks in Vision for the YOLO detections cloud and the front and down ZED clouds. A cloud is only subscribed while it's ticked: they're heavy over the tether.

Mapping and the prior map

The Mapping panel controls riptide_mapping2 (The course map):

  • Tag calibration: with Talos looking at the AprilTag board, Calibrate runs the map/model_tf action, which averages 10 samples of world → estimated_origin_frame (the tag) and, if they agree closely enough, moves the map there. A bar shows the samples taken.
  • Mapping target: which object mapping fuses now (empty = automatic, the closest), and Lock map, which freezes every estimate. Both go to the mapping_target service and read back from state/mapping (What gets fused).
  • Reset mapping throws the estimates away and starts again from the prior.

Edit map in the command bar (Ctrl+M) opens the prior map editor for riptide_mapping's config.yaml. It edits the source tree's copy, so a save reaches the robot at its next build. Move props in 2D or 3D, type poses, re-parent, and Save: only the changed values are written, and comments and order stay as they were. A running simulator pauses while you edit. A second layer, Sim course, moves the simulator's props instead, and the two can be copied into each other. That is how you test a tree against a course that doesn't match the prior.

Recording, Bagging, Electrical, Actuators

The tabs under the camera cards are for the real robot. In sim most of their services aren't there, and the panels say so.

Recording
SVO recording on the robot, one file per camera, through the ZED driver's start_svo_rec / stop_svo_rec. Capture image saves the newest front-camera frame on the robot (capture_image). A red REC chip shows in the command bar while a camera records.
Bagging
ros2 bag record on the robot over SSH (prefilled ros@orin2, bags in ~/bags), or on this laptop. A robot bag keeps recording if the viewer closes or the link drops. Pick All topics with a Leave out regex (image|point_cloud), or tick topics and save them as a preset. A red BAG chip shows while it records (Recording bags).
Electrical
The old RViz electrical panel: power commands (cycle computer, cycle robot and kill robot power ask first), LEDs, the IMU's magnetometer calibration and registers, Tare gyro (hold Talos still), the pinger and IVC.
Actuators
Arm first: Fire torpedo, Drop marker and the claw buttons need it. The POAC board only arms while Talos is enabled. Reload / disarm tells the board the torpedoes are reloaded (count back to 2). The status line shows claw and torpedo connections and rounds left.

Tools that only exist in sim

Simulation
Speed from a crawl to 10× (it sets the simulator's real_time_factor), Pause and 1×. Sync sim moves the simulated Talos to where the EKF thinks it is; Reset sim puts it back at the start. Sync sim is pinned to the toolbar by default.
Run tracking
The RoboSub 2026 scorecard: role and coin-flip options, Start run (it resets the props), the clock on sim time, per-task status, events (torpedo hits, drops, magnets) and a manual score adjustment. Nereus has no pinger, so pinger points can't score.
Display
What the pool view draws: water, walls, floor, tiles, the AprilTag board, the course (simulator truth, or the mapping estimate as a translucent ghost over it), the EKF estimate as a translucent robot, and lighting. None of this changes what the robot's cameras see.
Scene
Water appearance and lighting for this view only, and the simulator's mechanism buttons.
View › Pool
Restarts the simulator in another pool (RoboSub 2026 or the RPAC dive well). The robot stack keeps running.

Layouts, search and shortcuts

  • Layouts: Standard, Wide view (cameras and map in a strip under the pool view) and Camera wall (big feeds), on Ctrl+Shift+1/2/3. Drag a tab to dock or float any window. Save current as keeps an arrangement under a name in ~/.config/nereus/layouts/. Lock layout stops accidental drags during a run.
  • The toolbar is yours: right-click any button or checkbox in any window and Pin to toolbar (Fire torpedo, Pause, …). The pinned copy works with its window closed.
  • Search (Ctrl+P) finds windows, layouts, themes, every button and switch in every window (even closed ones), trees (Run <tree>), plots (plot odom z) and typed moves.
  • Plots and Topics (Windows menu): live plots of any field of any topic, and a topic browser. Right-click a Motion row for Plot Z (Plotting live).
  • Themes: Heat sheet (default, light, for a sunny deck) and its night twin Timing board, plus ten more under View › Theme.
  • Follow and chase: Ctrl+F keeps the camera on the focus target. The run video above also turns the camera with Talos's heading (View › Turn with the vehicle), a local change that isn't on Nereus's main branch yet, so your build may not have it.

At the pool and in sim

It's the same binary with a different launch file. In sim, sim.launch.py starts the simulator, the whole stack and the viewer, and the viewer takes its scene from the simulator's latched simulator/scenario topic. At the pool, robot.launch.py starts only the viewer. It loads the scenario pack itself and draws Talos at the EKF's estimate.

ros2 launch nereus sim.launch.py active_control_model:=mpc mpc_model:=sim
source ~/osu-uwrt/release/scripts/dds_scripts/config_rmw.bash
ros2 launch nereus robot.launch.py pool:=rpac

The camera cards show the ZED driver's compressed images, so they work at the pool too. They're the heaviest thing on the tether: close a card, or switch the layout, if the link struggles. The point clouds are heavier still, and they're off until ticked.

How it connects

The viewer is three ROS nodes on the laptop: /nereus_viewer (scene, cameras, overlays), /talos/viewer_panels (every panel) and /talos/viewer_plots (only what you plot). It never logs into the robot except for Bagging, which uses SSH. Everything else crosses as ordinary ROS traffic.

  • Over Zenoh. The team runs ROS 2 on rmw_zenoh_cpp. The Orin runs the router, and a laptop that has sourced config_rmw.bash joins it as a client at tcp/orin2:7447 (zenoh_session.json5). Same ROS_DOMAIN_ID, same RMW, and the robot workspace sourced so the viewer knows riptide_msgs2 (ROS 2 over Zenoh).
  • Names are relative to the robot namespace, /talos, so command/software_kill is /talos/command/software_kill.
  • Nothing hidden. Anything the viewer does, you can do from a terminal with the same topic or service. That's how the pre-test tool holds a bench kill on slot 3.

Where this lives

  • The viewer: nereus/integrations/ros2/viewer. src/app.cpp is the window and pool view; panels/ holds one file per panel and one per provider (uwrt_motion.cpp, uwrt_autonomy.cpp, …); src/prior_map_editor.cpp is Edit map.
  • Its config: nereus/content/viewer, with the themes in themes/.
  • Launch files: sim.launch.py and robot.launch.py.
  • Your own settings: ~/.config/nereus/ (theme, layout, saved layouts and plots, bag presets). Screenshots (F12): ~/Pictures/Nereus/.
