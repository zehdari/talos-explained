Control · Chapter 10

Commanding Talos: goals, teleop, kill

The MPC flies whatever reference it holds, and only while the hardware lets it. This chapter covers who gives it that reference, how its output reaches the propellers, and how the kill system stops all of it.

The short version
  • There are two ways to give the MPC a goal: a setpoint per half on controller/linear and controller/angular (the last message wins), or a follow_path action that flies a list of waypoints as one curve.
  • Autonomy uses both. People use the Nereus viewer's motion panel. The old riptide_teleop2 joystick package isn't built any more, and the gamepad script still installed with riptide_controllers2 was written for the Simulink controller.
  • Every 50 ms the MPC turns its thrust into RPM and writes two raw CAN frames, one per ESC board. Each board runs a speed loop per thruster over DShot.
  • The POAC board decides whether anything may spin. It kills if the physical switch is out, or if any of four software slots on command/software_kill asserts kill or goes silent. The viewer owns slot 1.
  • Several layers can stop the thrusters on their own: the kill line cuts ESC power, the ESC boards drop commands older than 0.5 s, and the MPC sends zeros when it is killed, disabled or has no odometry.

The earlier chapters built the controller: a model, a cost, a solver and an estimator, ticking at 20 Hz in mpc_controller_node.cpp. This one is about everything around it. All topic names below are relative to the robot's namespace, so controller/linear is /talos/controller/linear on the robot.

Giving the MPC a goal

The node holds one reference (where to be, or how fast to move) and three inputs can set it:

InputWhat it does
controller/linear, controller/angular
topic, ControllerCommand		Sets one half of the reference: position or velocity, attitude or turn rates. Ends any running path. Sent by autonomy and the viewer.
follow_path
action, FollowPath		A list of waypoints flown as one curve. The result comes back once Talos has settled on the last one. Sent by autonomy.
controller/FF_body_force
topic, Twist		A fixed body wrench, only used in FEEDFORWARD mode. Sent by controller_overseer at 1 Hz.

Autonomy reads back controller/scale/trust (0 to 1 per axis, "has it settled?", see Chapter 7) and the action's feedback and result. The viewer draws controller/mpc/planned_path and the MPC's predicted path.

ControllerCommand: one message per half

Position and attitude are commanded separately. Each ControllerCommand carries a mode and a setpoint:

modeOn controller/linearOn controller/angular
DISABLED = 0Either half disabled: zero thrust from the whole controller.
FEEDFORWARD = 1Both halves feedforward: the FF_body_force wrench is split straight onto the thrusters, with no MPC.
VELOCITY = 2setpoint_vect in m/s, body framesetpoint_vect as body rates, rad/s
POSITION = 3setpoint_vect: where base_link should be, in the odometry frame (odom)setpoint_quat: the attitude. An all-zero quaternion means "hold the current attitude".
  • The node starts with both halves DISABLED, so nothing moves until both halves have been given a mode (mpc.cpp).
  • A POSITION setpoint isn't chased directly. The reference glides to it along the S-curve from Chapter 4.
  • The reference outlives a kill. Only a DISABLED command clears it, so after the switch goes back in, Talos returns to its last setpoint. The viewer sends DISABLED on every Enable and KILL, so anything it starts begins from a disabled controller.

Paths: the follow_path action

A single setpoint stops at the end of every move. To fly through a list of points, autonomy sends a FollowPath goal, and the MPC plans it as one curve with one speed profile (how it plans is in Chapter 4). The goal has three fields:

path_points
Poses, each in its own TF frame. If a point's frame moves (one of mapping's object frames, say), the path moves with it while it runs. A zero quaternion keeps the previous point's attitude.
segments
Empty (straight lines, yaw blended), or one PathSegment per point: a line or an arc, and a heading rule (blend to the waypoint, face the direction of travel, or look at a point or a moving frame), plus extra spin.
max_speed
A cruise speed cap in m/s for this path only. 0 keeps the controller's own 0.6 m/s.
StageWhat happens (startPath, updatePath)
Accepted, then checkedEvery goal is accepted, then aborted at once with BAD_WYPTS if it has no points, the wrong number of segments, no odometry yet, a frame TF can't resolve, an unknown shape or heading, a negative max_speed, or a path the planner can't build. A point without a frame gives MISSING_FRAME_ID.
RunningThe previous path ends ("preempted by a new path"). Feedback every tick: current_prog and expected_prog, in metres along the path.
SucceedsThe reference is at the end and settle trust reaches 0.8, or Talos is within 10 cm, 0.1 rad, 0.1 m/s and 0.15 rad/s of the end.
FailsPROGRESS_FAIL: less than 5 cm of progress in 10 s, or the vehicle was killed, odometry went stale, or motion was disabled.
CanceledHolds where the reference can stop smoothly.
PreemptedA new path, or any ControllerCommand, ends it.

On the tree side, the FollowPath node builds the goal from a waypoint list that AddWaypoint and AddArc write. It clamps depth to between −0.55 m and −4 m by default, gives up after 60 s by default, and cancels the goal if the tree halts it. This is the shape of the MPC prequalification run in PreQualMpcTree.xml, trimmed:

<SubTree ID="WaitForKillSwitch"/>          <!-- until state/kill reads false -->
<SubTree ID="Dive" depth="-1"/>
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" frame="{gate_frame}" heading="path" x="{gate_offset}" … out="prequal_path"/>
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" frame="{gate_frame}" heading="path" x="{neg_gate_offset}" … />
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" frame="{gate_frame}" heading="path" x="{pole_x}" y="{arc_start_y}" … />  <!-- out beside the pole -->
<Action ID="AddArc" frame="{gate_frame}" cx="{pole_x}" cy="{pole_y}" radius="{pole_radius}"
        sweep="3.1416" look_frame="{pole_frame}" … />   <!-- round the pole, facing it -->
<!-- … two more waypoints back through the gate … -->
<Action ID="FollowPath" timeout="300" waypoints="{prequal_path}"/>
<SubTree ID="KillController"/>           <!-- DISABLED on both halves -->

From a goal to spinning props

Several programs and two kinds of board sit between "go there" and a turning propeller. Pick a story to light up its path, or tap any box to see what goes in and out of it.

Forces, RPM and two CAN frames

Each tick the MPC publishes its first-stage thrust on thruster_forces (Float32MultiArray, 8 values in newtons, in the order VUS, VUP, HUS, HUP, HLS, HLP, VLS, VLP from talos.yaml). In Nereus that topic is the end of the line: the simulator's bridge reads it. On the robot, sendRpm goes further:

  1. Converts each force to RPM by inverting the propeller law in the model file (Chapter 2). Zero force gives 0 rpm, which stops the motor.
  2. Rounds to a signed 16-bit integer and publishes the eight values on command/requested_rpm (DshotCommand). No board listens to this topic. It's there so bags record what was sent.
  3. Writes two raw CAN frames on can0 with the same C function the Simulink controller used (send_thruster_cmd_canbus.c). Frame 0xB0 goes to ESC board 0 (thrusters 0 to 3) and 0xF0 to board 1 (4 to 7). Each frame holds four big-endian int16 RPMs.

Step 3 only happens on the real vehicle: the model must have a hardware: section, the node must be on wall time (so a simulator running on the Orin never drives real thrusters), and can0 must exist (configureHardwareOutput). The node logs which check failed. When it shuts down, it sends one last frame of zeros. Three node parameters change this output (the last two are set in mpc.yaml):

hardware_output
auto (default) runs the checks above. off never writes CAN. on skips only the wall-time check.
table_mode
For a bench test out of the water: RPM = 500 (1 − e−|F|/2), so never more than 500 rpm. Off by default, and can be changed while running.
hardware.rpm_offset
RPM added to every nonzero request. 0 now. It was 140 to work around an ESC CAN decoding bug that has since been fixed in firmware.

On the board (Thrusters and ESCs has the hardware), the frame's four targets are stored with a 500 ms expiry. Core 1 runs one PI loop per thruster. It compares each target with the RPM the ESC reports back over bidirectional DShot600 and sends the ESC a throttle. The boards also accept targets on the ROS topic command/thruster_rpm, which some calibration scripts use. The MPC doesn't.

What can stop the thrusters

Stopping doesn't depend on any one program. These layers act on their own:

LayerStops whenEffect
Physical kill switchPulled outThe kill line removes ESC power. The POAC board sees the switch on an interrupt and kills at once.
POAC board, software slotsAny slot asserts kill, or a slot that needs updates goes 500 ms without oneIts soft-kill pin cuts ESC power even with the switch in. state/kill becomes true.
POAC board, ROS linkIt loses the micro-ROS agentKilled until it reconnects.
ESC boardsstate/kill true or silent for 500 ms. No ESC power, or less than 5 s since power came back. No RPM target for 500 ms (then a 1 s lockout if a thruster was spinning).Throttle 0, speed loops reset (core1.c).
controller_overseerMore than two telemetry messages in a row from one board with disabled flags, or no telemetry for 2 scontroller/motion_enabled false.
MPCstate/kill true, motion disabled, odometry older than 0.5 s, estimator not ready, solver error or a non-finite command, either half DISABLEDPublishes zero thrust, every time. Only a kill, disabled motion, stale odometry or an estimator that isn't ready also aborts the running path (tick).
On the bench

An enabled robot with control_system running spins the thrusters. On the bench, never start it as a plain launch. The pre-test tool starts it only while it holds a software kill on slot 3 and both ESC boards report SAFETY_KILLED. With hands near the propellers, pulling the physical switch is the only kill that works with no software running at all.

The kill system

Whether Talos is killed is decided in firmware, by the kill-switch module of titan_safety (kill_switch.c), running on the POAC board (power and actuators, on CAN as the power board, client 1). It has four slots, defined in KillSwitchReport:

SlotName in the messageUsed by, today
0KILL_SWITCH_PHYSICALThe switch on the hull. The board updates it itself on every loop. A ROS report on slot 0 is rejected.
1KILL_SWITCH_RQT_CONTROLLERNamed after the old rqt/RViz control panel. Today it belongs to the Nereus viewer.
2KILL_SWITCH_TOPSIDE_BUTTONA topside kill box: riptide_kill_switch, a Raspberry Pi with a button and a stack light, last changed in 2023. The newer opbox client has its slot-2 publisher commented out.
3KILL_SWITCH_DEBUGThe command line and scripts, e.g. the pre-test tool's bench hold.

The rule, checked on every safety tick:

\text{killed} = \text{no slot enabled} \;\lor\; \exists\,\text{enabled slot}: \big(\text{asserting} \lor (\text{needs\_update} \land \text{silent} > 500\text{ ms})\big)
  • A slot is enabled from the first report on it until the board reboots.
  • switch_needs_update = true makes the report a heartbeat. It must repeat within 500 ms or it counts as a kill, so if the sender dies, the robot is killed.
  • switch_needs_update = false latches. A latched "not killed" stays clear until the next report on that slot, or a reboot.
  • A kill takes a lock on its slot: the board records the sender_id, and only that sender can clear it. Anyone else's clear is rejected and raises FAULT_ROS_BAD_COMMAND (ros.c). A new kill from anyone takes the lock over.
  • Slot 0 is always enabled: the board reports it to itself, as a heartbeat, on every safety tick (safety_interface.c, tick). So with the switch in and no software slot asserting, Talos is enabled. Software slots can only add kills. The ESC boards are different: until they have heard state/kill, they count as killed.
  • The board starts killed, and it is killed whenever it has no micro-ROS connection: its safety code only runs while connected (main.c).
  • The kill also covers the torpedo: while killed, the POAC board refuses to arm the actuators or fire a torpedo (ros_torp.c, arming). The dropper command has no kill check of its own, and a kill doesn't disarm actuators that are already armed (a TODO in safety_handle_kill). Treat the actuators as live.
  • The board also publishes state/physkill_notify whenever the physical switch changes, for other boards to flash their LEDs (ros.c).

The result drives a soft-kill pin, which overrides the switch line and cuts ESC power (safety_interface.c). It also goes out on state/kill (std_msgs/Bool), and as bitmasks per slot in state/firmware (kill_switches_asserting_kill, …_needs_update, …_timed_out). The ESC boards treat state/kill as their own heartbeat slot: if it says killed, or stops for 500 ms, they kill too (ESCBoard ros.c). Because a kill cuts ESC power, after any kill the boards hold ESC_POWERON_DELAY for 5 s once power returns, while the ESCs start up.

Slot 1 belongs to the Nereus viewer

  • From its first tick, the viewer reports on slot 1 every 50 ms: asserting kill unless enabled, needs_update false, sender viewer_<host>_<pid>_<n> (uwrt_motion.cpp). Opening the viewer kills the robot until someone presses Enable.
  • Enable latches. The code says: "untethered runs continue". Unplug the tether or close the viewer and slot 1 stays clear.
  • Any other sender on slot 1 counts as a competing operator. The viewer drops its enable at once and refuses Enable until that sender has been quiet for 1 s.
  • The viewer's watchdogs (UI frozen for 0.75 s, stale pose, a stuck request) only release manual control. They never kill. Only the KILL button, the physical switch, a competing operator, or a viewer starting up kill.
  • KILL is a button in the always-visible command bar, never a keyboard shortcut.
  • The old RViz Control Panel worked the same way on slot 1, sending every 50 ms with the computer's hostname as sender. Its "Req Kill" checkbox set needs_update (ControlPanel.cpp).

Try the whole chain below. It runs the same rules as the firmware, the overseer, the viewer and the MPC, with their real timeouts.

A pool run, as the code sees it

  1. Switch in, viewer open: slot 1 asserts kill, so state/kill is true and the ESCs have no power.
  2. Enable: slot 1 clears and the POAC board releases ESC power. The ESC boards report ESC_POWERON_DELAY for 5 s, the overseer holds motion_enabled false, and then everything clears.
  3. Command a pose, or start a tree. A tree's WaitForKillSwitch waits 5 s, then polls state/kill until it reads false, then waits 6 s more before moving.
  4. Unplug the tether: slot 1 stays clear because it latched. The physical switch is now the kill.
  5. Pull the switch: ESC power goes, state/kill goes true, the MPC sends zeros and aborts its path. Put it back: 5 s of power-on delay, then the MPC flies to its last reference again.

Teleop, then and now

riptide_teleop (package riptide_teleop2) has PS3, Xbox and keyboard nodes, last changed in 2023. With the Xbox node, the right stick drives surge and yaw rate, the left stick sway and heave, and the D-pad roll and pitch rate. It sends VELOCITY on the linear half, and integrates the stick rates into a POSITION attitude each time odometry arrives. It has had a COLCON_IGNORE since October 2023, so colcon skips it. It wouldn't work on Talos as it is anyway:

  • It reads controller.linear.max.velocity from the vehicle YAML (xbox_teleop.py), and talos.yaml has no such key.
  • Its kill button publishes on control/software_kill, not command/software_kill, with slot 0, which the firmware would reject anyway (ps3_teleop.py).

The RViz Control Panel's Teleop button ran a different one: it forked ros2 launch riptide_controllers2 teleop.launch.py, which started RocketLeague.py. That launch file no longer exists, but the script does, and riptide_controllers2 still installs it (its CMake installs every script in src/riptide_controllers):

  • ros2 run riptide_controllers2 RocketLeague.py reads a PS3 or SHANWAN gamepad as raw evdev events, so it has to run on the machine the gamepad is plugged into. Its topics are hard-coded under /talos.
  • About 30 times a second it publishes a POSITION pair: x = 0, y = 0 and a depth you step with buttons, roll and pitch from the left stick, and yaw 0 (L314). The forward drive (triggers, D-pad nudges, boost, stunts) goes out on teleop/* and controller/stunt_state topics that only the Simulink controller used. Under the MPC it would just fly Talos to the odom origin, facing +x.
  • Two buttons fire the torpedo and the dropper through command/simple_torpedo_fire and command/simple_dropper_fire.
  • It has no kill of its own. The kill slots, and the viewer's Enable, still gate the thrusters.
  • A fix for the PS3 controller's device name and the default depth (commit a523923, August 2026) is only on the dev branch, not on mpc.

Today the viewer is the teleop. Drag the pose gizmo in the pool view, type a target and press Command, or use Dive in place, Zero roll & pitch, or Drive with keys (W/S, A/D, Space/Shift, Q/E, one step per press; [ and ] change the step). You can also type moves into Ctrl+P, like forward 0.5 or go 2 1 -1.5 90. All of these send POSITION commands, and the MPC's profile makes them smooth. The viewer code has no gamepad input.

Legacy controllers: riptide_controllers2

The launch argument active_control_model picks the controller in control_system.launch.py: mpc starts riptide_mpc, and anything else starts the Simulink complete_controller.

Watch the default

The argument defaults to hybrid, which means complete_controller. That package isn't built, so nothing flies. The defaults in bringup's launch files are hybrid too, and Nereus keeps them unless you say otherwise, so in the simulator run:

ros2 launch nereus sim.launch.py active_control_model:=mpc mpc_model:=sim

Of the remote-launch files, talos_launch_mpc.yaml, talos_launch_bench_mpc.yaml and talos_launch_sim_hil_mpc.yaml pass mpc. talos_launch_bench.yaml and talos_launch_debug.yaml pass complete, and talos_launch_sim_hil.yaml passes hybrid. Check which one you're starting.

  • complete_controller: PID and sliding-mode controllers, a thruster solver and feedforward auto-tuning, built in Simulink (riptide_controllers/models) and turned into a C++ ROS package by MATLAB (generate_cpp_packages.m). The generated package isn't in the repository or the workspace. It's legacy.
  • Still launched alongside the MPC: controller_overseer.py (motion_enabled from ESC telemetry, FF_body_force from talos.yaml's base_wrench at 1 Hz, and the setTeleop service) and calibrate_drag.py.
  • Still built and used: thruster_test_canbus, the bench thruster test. It is the same CAN sender compiled with ENABLE_THRUSTER_TEST: 300 rpm on each thruster in turn for 3 s, cycling 0 to 7, and Ctrl-C sends zeros (source). See Pool test day.

Try it from a terminal

In Nereus, or on the robot with the propellers clear and someone at the switch. First, who is killing it?

ros2 topic echo /talos/state/kill                # true = killed
ros2 topic echo /talos/command/software_kill     # every slot report, with its sender
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/firmware            # kill_switches_* bitmasks, per board

Move 1 m forward of where Talos is now, slowly, keeping its attitude. Points in the robot's own frame are resolved once, when the goal arrives, and the zero quaternion means "keep the attitude":

ros2 action send_goal --feedback /talos/follow_path riptide_msgs2/action/FollowPath \
  "{path_points: [{header: {frame_id: talos/base_link},
                   pose: {position: {x: 1.0}, orientation: {w: 0.0}}}], max_speed: 0.2}"

Hold a setpoint with the topics. A POSITION setpoint is a point in odom, not an offset: x = 0, y = 0 is wherever the estimator started, which may be metres away. So read where Talos is first, and use its x and y:

ros2 topic echo --once /talos/odometry/filtered --field pose.pose.position

Send both halves: until both have a mode, the MPC outputs nothing. ROS 2 fills an unset quaternion with w: 1, which means "face +x". Write w: 0.0 to hold the current attitude instead. Here Talos is at x = 2.1, y = −0.4 and should go to 1 m depth there:

ros2 topic pub --once /talos/controller/angular riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand "{mode: 3, setpoint_quat: {w: 0.0}}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/controller/linear  riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand "{mode: 3, setpoint_vect: {x: 2.1, y: -0.4, z: -1.0}}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/controller/linear  riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand "{mode: 0}"   # thrusters off: Talos floats

A software kill of your own goes on slot 3, with your own sender id:

ros2 topic pub -r 20 /talos/command/software_kill riptide_msgs2/msg/KillSwitchReport \
  "{sender_id: $(hostname)-cli, kill_switch_id: 3, switch_asserting_kill: true, switch_needs_update: true}"

Stopping that publisher doesn't un-kill. Slot 3 times out, and a timed-out heartbeat counts as a kill. To clear it, publish switch_asserting_kill: false with switch_needs_update: false from the same sender. That latches: slot 3 then stays clear until the board reboots.

Before you clear slot 3

If no other slot is asserting kill when you clear it, Talos is enabled at once, with nobody driving. Make sure something else holds a kill first: the viewer open and not enabled, or a needs_update kill of your own on slot 1 that will time out into a kill. That is the order in the simulator's Bench hold story above.
