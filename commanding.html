Control · Chapter 10

Commanding Talos: goals, teleop, kill

The MPC flies whatever reference it holds, and only while the hardware lets it. This chapter covers who gives it that reference, how its output reaches the propellers, and how the kill system stops all of it.

The short version
  • The MPC takes a goal two ways: a setpoint per half on controller/linear and controller/angular, or a follow_path action that flies waypoints as one curve. Autonomy uses both; people use the Nereus viewer.
  • Every 50 ms the MPC turns its thrust into RPM and writes one raw CAN frame per ESC board.
  • The POAC board kills if the physical switch is out, or if any of four software slots asserts kill or goes silent. The viewer owns slot 1.
  • Several layers stop the thrusters on their own: ESC power, the ESC boards' 0.5 s command timeout, and the MPC's zeros when killed, disabled or without odometry.
  • A kill doesn't clear the MPC's goal. It cuts a running path short and leaves the path's last point as the setpoint. Only a DISABLED command clears it.

Everything here happens around mpc_controller_node.cpp. Topic names are relative to the robot's namespace: controller/linear is /talos/controller/linear.

Giving the MPC a goal

The node holds one reference (where to be, or how fast to move). Three inputs set it:

InputWhat it does
controller/linear, controller/angular
topic, ControllerCommand		Sets one half of the reference: position or velocity, attitude or turn rates. Ends any running path. Sent by autonomy and the viewer.
follow_path
action, FollowPath		A list of waypoints flown as one curve. The result comes back once Talos has settled on the last one. Sent by autonomy.
controller/FF_body_force
topic, Twist		A fixed body wrench, only used in FEEDFORWARD mode. Sent by controller_overseer at 1 Hz.

Autonomy reads back settle trust on controller/scale/trust (Chapter 7) and the action's result.

ControllerCommand: one message per half

modeOn controller/linearOn controller/angular
DISABLED = 0Either half disabled: zero thrust from the whole controller.
FEEDFORWARD = 1Both halves feedforward: the FF_body_force wrench is split straight onto the thrusters, with no MPC.
VELOCITY = 2setpoint_vect in m/s, body framesetpoint_vect as body rates, rad/s
POSITION = 3setpoint_vect: where base_link should be, in odomsetpoint_quat: the attitude. All zeros means "hold the current attitude".
  • The node starts with both halves DISABLED, so nothing moves until both have a mode (mpc.cpp).
  • A POSITION setpoint isn't chased directly. The reference glides to it along the S-curve from Chapter 4.
  • The reference outlives a kill. Only a DISABLED command clears it, so after the switch goes back in, Talos returns to its last setpoint.
  • A kill mid-path skips the rest of the path. The kill aborts it (PROGRESS_FAIL, in tick) and finishPath makes the path's last point the setpoint. For a path you sent by hand, Talos flies straight there when the switch goes back in, past the waypoints in between. In a tree, FollowPath fails instead. If that ends the tree, the tree runner sends DISABLED on both halves, as it does whenever a tree ends (DoTask.cpp).
  • The viewer sends DISABLED on every Enable and KILL, so anything it starts begins from a disabled controller.

Paths: the follow_path action

A single setpoint stops at the end of every move. To fly through a list of points, autonomy sends a FollowPath goal, and the MPC plans it as one curve with one speed profile (Chapter 4).

path_points
Poses, each in its own TF frame. If a point's frame moves (a mapped object, say), the path moves with it. A zero quaternion keeps the previous point's attitude.
segments
Empty (straight lines), or one PathSegment per point: a line or an arc, a heading rule (blend, face the travel direction, or look at a point or frame), plus extra spin.
max_speed
A cruise cap in m/s for this path. 0 keeps the controller's 0.6 m/s.
OutcomeWhen (startPath, updatePath)
RejectedBAD_WYPTS or MISSING_FRAME_ID: no points, a frame TF can't resolve, no odometry, a path the planner can't build.
SucceedsAt the end with settle trust 0.8, or within 10 cm and 0.1 rad of it, nearly still.
FailsPROGRESS_FAIL: under 5 cm of progress in 10 s, a kill, stale odometry or disabled motion. The last point becomes the setpoint.
CanceledHolds where the reference can stop smoothly.
PreemptedA new path, or any ControllerCommand.

The tree's FollowPath node sends a list built by AddWaypoint and AddArc, as in PreQualMpcTree.xml (trimmed):

<SubTree ID="WaitForKillSwitch"/>          <!-- until state/kill reads false -->
<SubTree ID="Dive" depth="-1"/>
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" frame="{gate_frame}" heading="path" x="{gate_offset}" … out="prequal_path"/>
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" frame="{gate_frame}" heading="path" x="{neg_gate_offset}" … />
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" frame="{gate_frame}" heading="path" x="{pole_x}" y="{arc_start_y}" … />  <!-- out beside the pole -->
<Action ID="AddArc" frame="{gate_frame}" cx="{pole_x}" cy="{pole_y}" radius="{pole_radius}"
        sweep="3.1416" look_frame="{pole_frame}" … />   <!-- round the pole, facing it -->
<!-- … two more waypoints back through the gate … -->
<Action ID="FollowPath" timeout="300" waypoints="{prequal_path}"/>
<SubTree ID="KillController"/>           <!-- DISABLED on both halves -->

From a goal to spinning props

Several programs and two kinds of board sit between "go there" and a turning propeller. Pick a story to light up its path, or tap any box to see what goes in and out of it.

Forces, RPM and two CAN frames

Each tick the MPC publishes its thrust on thruster_forces (8 values in newtons, ordered VUS, VUP, HUS, HUP, HLS, HLP, VLS, VLP as in talos.yaml). In Nereus the simulator reads that topic. On the robot, sendRpm goes further:

  1. Converts each force to RPM by inverting the propeller law (Chapter 2). Zero force gives 0 rpm.
  2. Publishes them on command/requested_rpm, only so bags record them.
  3. Writes two raw CAN frames on can0 with the C function the Simulink controller used (send_thruster_cmd_canbus.c): 0xB0 to ESC board 0 (thrusters 0 to 3), 0xF0 to board 1 (4 to 7), four big-endian int16 RPMs each.

Step 3 needs a hardware: section in the model, wall time (so a simulator on the Orin never drives real thrusters) and a can0 (configureHardwareOutput). Two parameters change it:

hardware_output
auto (default) runs those checks, off never writes CAN, on skips the wall-time check.
table_mode
For the bench: RPM = 500 (1 − e−|F|/2), never more than 500 rpm. Off by default (mpc.yaml).

On each ESC board (Thrusters and ESCs), targets are kept for 500 ms, and a PI loop per thruster tracks the RPM each ESC reports over bidirectional DShot600 (core1.c).

LinkRate
MPC tick, thruster_forces, CAN frames, action feedback, trust20 Hz

What can stop the thrusters

Stopping doesn't depend on any one program. These layers act on their own:

LayerStops whenEffect
Physical kill switchPulled outESC power off.
POAC board, software slotsAny slot asserts kill, or a heartbeat slot goes 500 ms without a reportESC power off, state/kill true.
POAC board, ROS linkMore than 7 failed heartbeats to the micro-ROS agent, 100 ms apartKilled until it reconnects.
ESC boardsstate/kill true or silent 500 ms; no ESC power, or under 5 s since it returned; no RPM target for 500 ms (then a 1 s lockout); speed loop not yet configuredThrottle 0 (core1.c).
controller_overseerMore than two flagged telemetry messages in a row, or none for 2 scontroller/motion_enabled false.
MPCKilled, motion disabled, odometry older than 0.5 s, estimator not ready, solver error, a DISABLED halfZero thrust. The first four also abort a path, leaving its last point as the setpoint.
On the bench

An enabled robot with control_system running spins the thrusters. On the bench, never start it as a plain launch. The pre-test tool starts it only while it holds a software kill on slot 3 and both ESC boards report SAFETY_KILLED. With hands near the propellers, pulling the physical switch is the only kill that works with no software running at all.

The kill system

The kill is decided in firmware on the POAC board (kill_switch.c). It has four slots:

SlotNameUsed by, today
0KILL_SWITCH_PHYSICALThe switch on the hull, read by the board itself.
1KILL_SWITCH_RQT_CONTROLLERThe Nereus viewer (named after the old rqt/RViz panel).
2KILL_SWITCH_TOPSIDE_BUTTONA topside kill box, riptide_kill_switch (last changed 2023). The opbox client's slot-2 publisher is commented out.
3KILL_SWITCH_DEBUGThe command line and scripts, like the pre-test tool.

The rule, checked on every safety tick:

\text{killed} = \text{no slot enabled} \;\lor\; \exists\,\text{enabled slot}: \big(\text{asserting} \lor (\text{needs\_update} \land \text{silent} > 500\text{ ms})\big)
  • A slot is enabled from its first report until the board reboots.
  • switch_needs_update = true makes the report a heartbeat: it must repeat within 500 ms, so if the sender dies, the robot is killed. false latches until the next report on that slot.
  • A kill takes a lock: only the same sender_id can clear it. Anyone else's clear is rejected with FAULT_ROS_BAD_COMMAND, and a new kill from anyone takes the lock over (ros.c).
  • sender_id must be shorter than 32 characters, or the board rejects the report with the same fault (ros.c). Nothing tells the sender.
  • Slot 0 is always enabled (safety_interface.c), so with the switch in and no software slot asserting, Talos is enabled. Software slots can only add kills.
  • The board starts killed, and stays killed while it has no micro-ROS connection (main.c).
  • A kill blocks arming and torpedo fire (ros_torp.c), but not the dropper, and it doesn't disarm armed actuators (a TODO). Treat them as live.

The result drives a soft-kill pin that cuts ESC power (safety_interface.c) and goes out on state/kill. The ESC boards treat that as a heartbeat: killed, silent for 500 ms or not yet heard, and they kill too (ros.c). After power returns they wait 5 s (ESC_POWERON_DELAY) for the ESCs to start.

Slot 1 belongs to the Nereus viewer

  • From its first tick, the viewer reports on slot 1 every 50 ms: asserting kill unless enabled, needs_update false (uwrt_motion.cpp). Opening the viewer kills the robot until someone presses Enable.
  • Its sender is viewer_<host>_<pid>_<n>. With a long host name it can pass 31 characters, and then the board ignores every report from it, KILL included.
  • Enable latches ("untethered runs continue"). Unplug the tether or close the viewer and slot 1 stays clear.
  • Any other sender on slot 1 counts as a competing operator: the viewer drops its enable and refuses Enable until that sender has been quiet for 1 s.
  • The Enable key turns into KILL whenever the robot is seen enabled, even if this viewer didn't enable it (motion_panel.cpp). KILL also stops any running behaviour tree, even one started elsewhere (composition.cpp, uwrt_autonomy.cpp). It is a button, never a keyboard shortcut.
  • The viewer's watchdogs only release manual control. They never kill.
  • History: the old RViz Control Panel also used slot 1, with rviz_control_<hostname> as sender (ControlPanel.cpp).

Teleop, then and now

Today the viewer is the teleop. Drag the pose gizmo, type a target and press Command, or use Dive in place, Zero roll & pitch, or Drive with keys (W/S, A/D, Space/Shift, Q/E, one step per press). Ctrl+P takes typed moves such as forward 0.5. All send POSITION commands. There is no gamepad input.

The old joystick code doesn't work with the MPC:

  • riptide_teleop2 (PS3, Xbox, keyboard) has a COLCON_IGNORE and isn't built. It reads a config key talos.yaml doesn't have, and its kill goes to the wrong topic.
  • RocketLeague.py, the RViz panel's gamepad teleop, is still installed with riptide_controllers2, but was written for the Simulink controller. Under the MPC it would fly Talos to the odom origin (L314).

Legacy controllers: riptide_controllers2

The launch argument active_control_model picks the controller in control_system.launch.py: mpc starts riptide_mpc, and anything else the old Simulink complete_controller, which is no longer built.

Watch the default

The argument defaults to hybrid, so nothing flies. Bringup's launch files default to hybrid too, and Nereus keeps that unless you say otherwise, so in the simulator run:

ros2 launch nereus sim.launch.py active_control_model:=mpc mpc_model:=sim

Of the remote-launch files, only the three ending in _mpc.yaml pass mpc. The rest, plain talos_launch.yaml included, start the legacy controller. Check which one you're starting.

  • Still launched with the MPC: controller_overseer.py (motion_enabled, FF_body_force, and a setTeleop service the viewer calls that now just answers success) and calibrate_drag.py.
  • Still used: thruster_test_canbus, the bench thruster test: 300 rpm on each thruster in turn for 3 s, and Ctrl-C sends zeros (source). See Pool test day.

Try it from a terminal

In Nereus, or on the robot with the propellers clear and someone at the switch. First, who is killing it?

ros2 topic echo /talos/state/kill                # true = killed
ros2 topic echo /talos/command/software_kill     # every slot report, with its sender
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/firmware            # kill_switches_* bitmasks, per board

Move 1 m forward of where Talos is now, slowly, keeping its attitude. A point in the robot's own frame is resolved once, when the goal arrives:

ros2 action send_goal --feedback /talos/follow_path riptide_msgs2/action/FollowPath \
  "{path_points: [{header: {frame_id: talos/base_link},
                   pose: {position: {x: 1.0}, orientation: {w: 0.0}}}], max_speed: 0.2}"

Hold a setpoint with the topics. A POSITION setpoint is a point in odom, not an offset, so read where Talos is first:

ros2 topic echo --once /talos/odometry/filtered --field pose.pose.position

Send both halves. An unset quaternion becomes w: 1 ("face +x"), so write w: 0.0 to hold the attitude. Here Talos is at x = 2.1, y = −0.4 and goes to 1 m depth:

ros2 topic pub --once /talos/controller/angular riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand "{mode: 3, setpoint_quat: {w: 0.0}}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/controller/linear  riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand "{mode: 3, setpoint_vect: {x: 2.1, y: -0.4, z: -1.0}}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/controller/linear  riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand "{mode: 0}"   # thrusters off: Talos floats

A software kill of your own goes on slot 3, with a short sender id of your own (under 32 characters):

ros2 topic pub -r 20 /talos/command/software_kill riptide_msgs2/msg/KillSwitchReport \
  "{sender_id: cli_$USER, kill_switch_id: 3, switch_asserting_kill: true, switch_needs_update: true}"

Stopping that publisher doesn't un-kill: slot 3 times out, which counts as a kill. To clear it, publish switch_asserting_kill: false with switch_needs_update: false from the same sender. That latches slot 3 clear until the board reboots.

Before you clear slot 3

If no other slot is asserting kill when you clear it, Talos is enabled at once, with nobody driving. Make sure something else holds a kill first: the viewer open and not enabled, or a needs_update kill of your own on slot 1 that will time out into a kill. That is the order in the simulator's Bench hold story above.
