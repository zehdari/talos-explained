The vehicle · Chapter 6

Torpedoes, dropper and claw

The parts of Talos that act on the course: a launcher that shoots two torpedoes and drops two markers, a claw that carries objects, and a magnet on a stick. One servo, one board and a handful of ROS names do all of the firing. Here is how a shot gets from the mission tree to the spring.

The short version
  • Talos carries a launcher (two torpedoes pointing forward, two markers pointing down), a claw under the bow and a passive magnet poker.
  • One serial servo on the POAC board fires the whole launcher. From its 120° home it turns one way for torpedoes (200°, then 240°) and the other way for markers (50°, then 0°), then goes back home.
  • A tree calls a ROS service; actuator_service_translator turns it into a topic; micro-ROS carries that over CAN to the POAC, which says yes or no on state/actuator/cmd_status.
  • The firmware will not arm while the robot is killed, and will not fire a torpedo unless armed, enabled and idle. Trees arm just before a task and disarm right after.
  • Nereus takes the same services and topics, applies similar arming rules (a little stricter; see the table below) and flies real projectiles, so trees can be tested before the pool.

Several RoboSub tasks need Talos to touch the course: torpedoes through holes in a board, markers into bins, objects moved from a table into baskets, and magnets held against targets. RoboSub and the course has the rules and points. This chapter is about the hardware and code that do it. The board itself is in The circuit boards, the trees in The competition mission.

The launcher: one servo, four shots

The CAD calls it Torpedoes + Markers V8. A servo in an aluminium housing turns a spur gear, a shaft and a pair of helix gears that move a pushing arm. Going by the CAD and the firmware's angles, the arm lets go of one spring-loaded payload at a time: torpedoes from two tubes pointing forward, markers from two tubes pointing down.

Payloads
2 torpedoes forward, 2 markers down. CAD torpedo Sinky Torp: PBT, 83 mm long, 26 mm body.

The firmware knows four angles and a home (ros_torp.c, actuator.c). The servo's full range is 0–240°:

  • Home 120°. Everything loaded and held.
  • Torpedoes: 200°, then 240°. Each shot turns further the same way.
  • Markers: 50°, then 0°. The other way from home.
  • After every shot it goes back to 120°. The board counts what is left; it cannot see the tubes.

The tree assumes the first shot leaves the starboard tube: it aims torpedo_0_link at the first hole, and Nereus fires its starboard slot first. Which tube the 200° move releases on the robot is not confirmed anywhere in code or docs. Check it on the bench; if it is the port tube, the first torpedo is aimed 35 mm off.

Why one servo for four payloads? One motor, one housing and one cable through the hull instead of four. The cost is that torpedoes and markers can never fire at the same time, and that the order is fixed.

The POAC drives the servo

The launcher servo plugs into the POAC (power + actuator) board in the port hull, through its lid connector LID_CONN1 (12 V, ground, signal, check). The board's RP2040 runs the actuator code alongside power and kill-switch duties (The circuit boards, Firmware on the boards).

  • Serial bus servo. It speaks Hiwonder's bus-servo protocol (hiwonder_driver.c): half-duplex UART at 115200 baud on GPIO 27, made by a PIO state machine, level-shifted to 5 V. The servo is ID 1. A position is 0–1000 for 0–240°.
  • One packet every 10 ms. Commands queue up and go out on a 10 ms timer; every write is read back, and a mismatch is resent up to 10 times.
  • Arming = torque on. arm sends the servo's load command and reads it back. actuators_armed turns true only once the servo confirms (actuator.c).
  • A shot is a planned move. Move time = angle ÷ 300°/s, then 250 ms to settle, then back home and another 250 ms. busy is true the whole time (actuator.c).
  • Heartbeat. The board pings the servo every second; after more than 10 failed replies it reports the launcher as ERROR.
  • Timers. state/actuator/status and busy every 500 ms; the reply to a command on the next 100 ms tick (main.c).

Every command is checked in firmware before the servo moves (ros_torp.c, master):

Safety
  • Treat the launcher as live whenever the POAC has power. The servo's 12 V comes from the board's own regulators, not the thruster power that the kill switch cuts.
  • On master a kill does not disarm: safety_handle_kill still has // TODO: disarm actuators (safety_interface.c). Torpedoes are still refused while killed; the dropper is not. Disarm by hand.
  • The dropper does not check kill, arming or busy. Whether a servo with torque off moves when told to is up to the servo; don't count on it staying put.
  • Keep hands and faces out of the line of the torpedo tubes and from under the marker tubes whenever the board is on.

The claw firmware is not on master. There, the claw topic names are defined but never subscribed, and claw_state is always sent as 0 (#L377). The unmerged branch poac-multiple-servos (last commit 2026-06-28, "Claw is ID 3") adds a second servo on the same bus, ID 3, run as a continuous-rotation motor from command/actuator/claw_move_s. The trees use that topic, but which build is flashed on Talos today isn't confirmed; state/firmware tells you on the robot.

Why a translator? The firmware only takes topics, and a topic gives the sender no answer. A tree wants to know whether the shot was accepted, so actuator_service_translator.py serves each command as a service. On a request it publishes the topic, then checks every 200 ms until cmd_status arrives, and returns that as success. It is launched with autonomy (autonomy.launch.py), not with the hardware. Three things to know:

  • No timeout, and any reply counts. If no cmd_status comes (board off, agent down), the call hangs until one does. Whatever arrives next ends it, even the reply to a different command sent from the viewer, and becomes this call's success; calls queued behind it then pair with the wrong replies too. The tree gives up after its 1 s limit either way. Restarting the translator is the clean fix.
  • One call at a time. The four services share one callback group, so a second request waits for the first.
  • No reason. The reply's message is always empty. Read state/actuator/cmd_feedback to see why a command was refused.

ActuatorStatus (ActuatorStatus.msg) carries actuators_armed, a state and a count for torpedoes and droppers, and fields for a claw and a "rack" from older robots. The POAC fills the torpedo states like this:

torpedo_stateWhen
0 ERRORservo not answering (the code also checks "homed", but that is set at boot)
1 DISARMEDtorque off
4 FIRINGservo moving (for either payload)
3 CHARGEDarmed, idle, at least one left
5 FIREDarmed, idle, none left

The dropper uses the same pattern with its own numbers (0 ERROR, 1 DISARMED, 3 DROPPING, 2 READY, 4 DROPPED). 2 CHARGING is never sent: it belonged to an older, coil-fired launcher whose code is still there, commented out.

A third way in: simple_actuator_interface.py, started by the hardware launch, listens on command/simple_torpedo_fire and command/simple_dropper_fire (std_msgs/Empty). It arms if needed, fires through the services, waits for busy to clear and leaves the launcher armed (its disarm step is commented out).

How the trees use them

The competition tree is RepairCompMpcTree.xml. Four small subtrees do all the actuator work, and the task trees wrap them:

  • SetActuatorsArmed: wait 1 s, then call command/actuator/arm (1 s limit). Wrapped in a ForceSuccess, so a refusal doesn't stop the tree (L2196).
  • FireTorpedo: call command/actuator/torpedo (CallTriggerService, 1 s limit), wait 2 s, then WaitForActuators, which as written gives up at once because its timeout key is never set (The competition mission, Things to watch). Wrapped in a ForceSuccess (L1004).
  • FireDropper: check the states aren't ERROR, wait until idle, check a marker is left, call command/actuator/dropper, wait 1 s, wait until idle. Also wrapped in a ForceSuccess (L977).
  • ReloadActuators: call notify_reload up to 3 times. PrepForRun runs it, then disarms, at the start of every run.

The pattern: arm on the way in, disarm on the way out. Between tasks a stray torpedo command is then refused, and because the board refuses to arm while killed, nothing can be armed before the kill switch goes in.

  • Torpedoes disarm on both paths: TorpedoTreeMpc has its own success and failure branches, and both disarm (L2983).
  • Bins can skip the disarm. The final SetActuatorsArmed armed=0 comes after two classifier service calls (start_binary_classifier_second, stop_binary_classifier, 3 tries each). If either fails, the bins sequence stops there and the launcher stays armed (L160). The next task's arm then just gets Already Armed.

A shot that "succeeds" without firing

FireTorpedo and FireDropper both sit inside a ForceSuccess named REMOVE ME, so they report success even when the board refuses. The RetryUntilSuccessful (3 attempts) around each therefore never retries, and a refused or missing marker reads as a drop just like a refused torpedo reads as a shot. In the Nereus media run, the second torpedo hit exactly this. 5.6 s after the first shot the tasks were reset (the scorecard run was being started by hand at that moment), and a task reset also reloads and disarms. The second call, 0.7 s later, was refused, and the tree went on to the table as if it had fired. Step 7 of the timeline above ("In Nereus") shows it.

The claw and the magnet poker

The claw (CAD EMERGENCYCLAWASSEMBLY) hangs below the bow on a PET mount and an aluminium box channel. Its servo turns a pinion between two gear racks (McMaster 2662N57, 20° pressure angle); each rack carries one Gripper_V6 jaw, so the jaws close symmetrically on the centre line.

The claw is driven by time, not position. On poac-multiple-servos the claw servo runs as a continuous-rotation motor, which has no absolute position to go to. So the command is a signed number of seconds on command/actuator/claw_move_s (std_msgs/Float32): positive opens, negative closes, the board runs the motor at full speed for that long. The tree then waits as long as it asked for. Nereus models the same thing:

The magnet poker is a PET rod with a magnet held on its tip by two zip ties. Nothing on the robot moves: the course's magnet targets react to the magnet (in Nereus, an indicator light changes), so the tree only has to put magnet_link on the target and hold it there for 2 s. Nereus models it the same way, as a passive point that the task pack checks for distance.

One thing to check on the robot: the files disagree about where the magnet is. All three positions below are in base_link:

  • magnet_link (talos.yaml magnet:): 15.5 cm behind, near the centre line, 30 cm down. This is the frame the trees fly.
  • The July CAD poker: from 15 to 33 cm ahead, 12–16 cm to starboard, tip about 10 cm down. (The CAD is placed in base_link by matching its eight thrusters to talos.yaml.)
  • poker_link (talos.yaml poker:, also Nereus's poker_mount): 37 cm ahead, 2 cm to starboard, 8 cm down. Close to the CAD tip ahead and down, but not sideways, and no tree uses it.

At least one of them is out of date. Measure the poker on the robot before trusting the magnet task.

In Nereus

Nereus describes each mechanism in the Talos robot pack (robot.yaml) and runs it in mechanisms.cpp. The bridge (bridge.yaml) accepts both the firmware topics and the services, and publishes state/actuator/status, busy and cmd_status with the same numbers the POAC uses, so trees, the translator and the viewer work unchanged. Nereus, the simulator covers the rest.

  • Launcher and dropper. Two slots each, placed at the tubes (slot poses in robot.yaml), fired in slot order, starboard first. A shot releases the next slot as a free body with launch speed √(2E/(m + madded)) from a 0.047 J spring, plus the vehicle's own velocity: 2.49 m/s for a 12.2 g torpedo, 2.29 m/s for a 14.9 g marker. Torpedoes fly as finned bodies that turn under drag and buoyancy; markers keep their attitude. Anything still loose after 30 s counts as a miss.
  • One cooldown. Torpedoes and markers share a 0.5 s cooldown group, standing in for the single servo.
  • Claw. Two jaws with convex contact pads, 12 N grip and friction 1.2; an object counts as grasped after 40 ms in contact.
  • Arming. One flag for launcher, dropper and claw. It starts disarmed, can't be armed while killed, and a kill disarms it (safety).

Testing on the bench

Firing the torpedo and dropper on the bench is fine. The team's pre-pool-test tool (pretest_checks, guided step G5) does it the same way. The rule that matters is about the thrusters, not the launcher:

Before you enable
  • The controller must not be running. An enabled robot with the controller up spins the thrusters. The launcher can only arm while the robot is enabled, so this comes first.
  • Clear the area in front of the torpedo tubes and below the marker tubes. Load only what you mean to fire.
  • On the poac-multiple-servos build the claw moves without arming and without enable: claw_move_time() checks neither and always accepts. Keep fingers out of the jaws whenever the board is on.
  1. Robot on the bench supply, hardware stack up. Check the board answers: state/actuator/status at 2 Hz with torpedo_state not 0.
  2. Kill switch in, then Enable in the Nereus viewer.
  3. Arm from the viewer's Actuators panel, or from a shell.
  4. Fire one torpedo or marker. The count drops by one, busy goes true for under a second.
  5. Disarm, then Disable in the viewer.
  6. Reload by hand, then send notify_reload so the counts are 2 again. The viewer's Reload / disarm button sends the same message, and on the POAC it does not disarm.
  7. Claw (only on the poac-multiple-servos build): publish claw_move_s 2.0 to open for 2 s, then −2.0 to close. The viewer's Open claw and Close claw buttons do nothing on the robot (see the panel above).

How to reload. The loading procedure (order, where the servo must be, how to compress the springs) is not written down in code or in the team's docs. Ask the mechanical lead to show you before your first time.

# watch the state and the reasons for any refusal
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/actuator/status
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/actuator/cmd_feedback

# arm, fire, disarm through the firmware topics (always there)
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/arm std_msgs/msg/Bool "{data: true}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/torpedo std_msgs/msg/Empty "{}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/arm std_msgs/msg/Bool "{data: false}"

# or through the services (needs actuator_service_translator, launched with autonomy)
ros2 service call /talos/command/actuator/dropper std_srvs/srv/Trigger

# after reloading by hand
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/notify_reload std_msgs/msg/Empty "{}"

# claw, poac-multiple-servos build only: open 2 s, then close 2 s (never send 0, see below)
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/claw_move_s std_msgs/msg/Float32 "{data: 2.0}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/claw_move_s std_msgs/msg/Float32 "{data: -2.0}"

Don't send 0 to the claw on the robot. The branch firmware works out the direction as time_ms / abs(time_ms), an integer divide by zero when the time is 0, and what the RP2040 does then is undefined. The viewer's Stop claw button sends exactly that (claw_move_s = 0). In Nereus, 0 is a clean stop.

Re-homing. torpedo_marker/set_home (only while disarmed) reads where the servo is now and makes that home; go_home (armed, idle) drives it back there. The new home lives in RAM only: after a reboot it is 120° again. The firing angles are fixed numbers, so they don't move with home.
