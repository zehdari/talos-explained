Control · Chapter 10

Commanding Talos: goals, teleop, kill

The MPC flies whatever reference it holds, and only while the hardware lets it. This chapter covers who gives it that reference, how its output reaches the propellers, and how the kill system stops all of it.

The short version
  • The MPC takes a goal two ways: a setpoint per half on controller/linear and controller/angular, or a follow_path action that flies waypoints as one curve. Autonomy uses both; people use the Nereus viewer.
  • Every 50 ms the MPC turns its thrust into RPM and writes one raw CAN frame per ESC board.
  • The POAC board kills if the physical switch is out, or if any of its three software slots asserts kill or (for heartbeat slots, which must repeat) goes silent for 500 ms. The viewer owns slot 1.
  • Several layers stop the thrusters on their own: ESC power, the ESC boards' 0.5 s command timeout, and the MPC's zeros when killed, disabled or without odometry.
  • A kill doesn't clear the MPC's goal: when the switch goes back in, Talos flies to its last setpoint. Only a DISABLED command clears it.

The MPC side of this lives in mpc_controller_node.cpp; the kill side lives in the POAC and ESC board firmware. Topic names are relative to the robot's namespace: controller/linear is /talos/controller/linear.

From a goal to spinning props

Between "go there" and a turning propeller sit several programs on the Orin and two kinds of circuit board. Pick a story and step through it. Each step shows only the boxes it involves; Every box lists all the links.

Giving the MPC a goal

The node holds one reference (where to be, or how fast to move). Three inputs set it:

InputWhat it doesSent by
controller/linear, controller/angular
topic, ControllerCommand		Sets one half of the reference: position or velocity, attitude or turn rates. Ends any running path.Autonomy's PublishToController node, the viewer, pool_identify (Chapter 8), a terminal
follow_path
action, FollowPath		A list of waypoints flown as one curve. The result comes back once Talos has settled on the last one.Autonomy's FollowPath node, a terminal
controller/FF_body_force
topic, Twist		A fixed body wrench (force and torque), used only in FEEDFORWARD mode.controller_overseer, at 1 Hz

Autonomy reads back settle trust (0 to 1: has Talos arrived and stayed?) on controller/scale/trust, and the action's result. Its tree files wrap these in three subtrees: WaitForController (trust and distance), KillController (DISABLED on both halves) and WaitForKillSwitch (until state/kill reads false). See tree nodes.

Driving by hand means the viewer. Drag the pose gizmo, type a target and press Command, or use Dive in place, Zero roll & pitch, or Drive with keys (W/S, A/D, Space/Shift, Q/E, one step per press). Ctrl+P takes typed moves such as forward 0.5. All of them send POSITION commands, and the viewer locks them while a tree runs. There is no gamepad input (viewer chapter).

ControllerCommand: one message per half

modeOn linearOn angular
DISABLED = 0Either half disabled: zero thrust from the whole controller.
FEEDFORWARD = 1Both halves feedforward: the FF_body_force wrench is split straight onto the thrusters, with no MPC.
VELOCITY = 2setpoint_vect in m/s, body framesetpoint_vect as body rates, rad/s
POSITION = 3setpoint_vect: where base_link should be, in odomsetpoint_quat: the attitude. All zeros means "hold the current attitude".
  • The node starts with both halves DISABLED (mpc.hpp), and either half disabled gives zero thrust (mpc.cpp), so nothing moves until both have a mode.
  • A POSITION setpoint isn't chased directly. The reference glides to it along the S-curve from Chapter 4.
  • The reference outlives a kill. Only a DISABLED command clears it, so when the switch goes back in, Talos returns to its last setpoint. A kill mid-path aborts the path (PROGRESS_FAIL, in tick) and makes its last point the setpoint (finishPath): Talos then flies straight there, past the waypoints in between. A tree's FollowPath fails instead, and whenever a tree ends, the tree runner sends DISABLED on both halves (DoTask.cpp).
  • The viewer sends DISABLED on every Enable and KILL, so anything it starts begins from a disabled controller.

Paths: the follow_path action

A single setpoint stops at the end of every move. To fly through a list of points, autonomy sends a FollowPath goal, and the MPC plans it as one curve with one speed profile (Chapter 4).

path_points
Poses, each in its own TF frame. If a point's frame moves (a mapped object, say), the path moves with it. A zero quaternion keeps the previous point's attitude.
segments
Empty (straight lines), or one PathSegment per point: a line or an arc, a heading rule (blend, face the travel direction, or look at a point or frame), plus extra spin.
max_speed
A cruise cap in m/s for this path. 0 keeps the controller's 0.6 m/s.
OutcomeWhen (startPath, updatePath)
RejectedBAD_WYPTS or MISSING_FRAME_ID: no points, a frame TF can't resolve, no odometry, a path the planner can't build.
SucceedsAt the end with settle trust 0.8, or within 10 cm and 0.1 rad of it, nearly still.
FailsPROGRESS_FAIL: under 5 cm of progress in 10 s, a kill, stale odometry or disabled motion. The last point becomes the setpoint (see above).
CanceledHolds where the reference can stop smoothly.
PreemptedA new path, or any ControllerCommand.

The tree's FollowPath node sends a list built by AddWaypoint and AddArc, as in PreQualMpcTree.xml (trimmed; … marks left-out attributes):

<!-- until state/kill reads false -->
<SubTree ID="WaitForKillSwitch"/>
<SubTree ID="Dive" depth="-1"/>
<!-- 1: behind the gate; a new list -->
<Action ID="AddWaypoint"
  out="prequal_path"
  frame="{gate_frame}" heading="path"
  x="{gate_offset}" y="0" z="0"/>
<!-- 2: through the gate -->
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" …
  x="{neg_gate_offset}" y="0" z="0"/>
<!-- 3: out beside the pole -->
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" …
  x="{pole_x}" y="{arc_start_y}"/>
<!-- 4: round the pole, facing it -->
<Action ID="AddArc" frame="{gate_frame}"
  cx="{pole_x}" cy="{pole_y}"
  radius="{pole_radius}" sweep="3.1416"
  look_frame="{pole_frame}" …/>
<!-- 5, 6: back through the gate -->
<Action ID="AddWaypoint" …/> ×2
<!-- fly all six as one curve -->
<Action ID="FollowPath" timeout="300"
  waypoints="{prequal_path}"/>
<!-- DISABLED on both halves -->
<SubTree ID="KillController"/>

Forces, RPM and two CAN frames

Each tick the MPC publishes its thrust on thruster_forces: 8 values in newtons, in the order VUS, VUP, HUS, HUP, HLS, HLP, VLS, VLP from talos.yaml. The letters read Vector/Heave, Upper/Lower, Starboard/Port, so HLP is heave, lower, port (where each one sits). In Nereus the simulator reads that topic. On the robot, sendRpm goes further:

  1. Converts each force to RPM by inverting the propeller law (Chapter 2). Zero force gives 0 rpm.
  2. Publishes them as a DshotCommand on command/requested_rpm, only so bags record them.
  3. Writes two raw CAN frames on can0 with the C function the old Simulink controller used (send_thruster_cmd_canbus.c): 0xB0 to ESC board 0 (thrusters 0 to 3) and 0xF0 to board 1 (4 to 7). Each frame holds four RPMs as signed 16-bit numbers, high byte first.

Step 3 needs a hardware: section in the model, wall time (so a simulator on the Orin never drives real thrusters) and a can0 (configureHardwareOutput). Three parameters change it:

hardware_output
auto (default) runs those checks, off never writes CAN, on skips the wall-time check.
table_mode
For the bench: RPM = 500 (1 − e−|F|/2), never more than 500 rpm. Off by default (mpc.yaml).
hardware.rpm_offset
Added to every nonzero RPM, away from zero (not in table mode). Default 0; it was 140 while an ESC CAN decoding bug, now fixed in firmware, needed it (mpc.yaml).

Each ESC board (Thrusters and ESCs) keeps its targets for 500 ms. A PI loop per thruster tracks the RPM that each of its four on-board APD ESCs reports over bidirectional DShot600 (core1.c), and the ESCs drive the T200s out on the frame.

What can stop the thrusters

Stopping doesn't depend on any one program. These layers act on their own:

LayerStops whenEffect
Physical kill switchPulled outESC power off.
POAC board, software slotsAny slot asserts kill, or a heartbeat slot goes 500 ms without a reportESC power off, state/kill true.
POAC board, ROS linkMore than 7 failed heartbeats to the micro-ROS agent, 100 ms apartKilled until it reconnects.
ESC boardsstate/kill true or silent 500 ms; no ESC power, or under 5 s since it returned; no RPM target for 500 ms (then a 1 s lockout); speed loop not yet configuredThrottle 0 (core1.c).
controller_overseerMore than two flagged telemetry messages in a row, or none for 2 scontroller/motion_enabled false.
MPCKilled, motion disabled, odometry older than 0.5 s, estimator not ready, solver error, a DISABLED halfZero thrust. The first four also abort a path (what that leaves).
On the bench

An enabled robot with control_system running spins the thrusters. On the bench, never start it as a plain launch. The pre-test tool starts it only while it holds a software kill on slot 3 and both ESC boards report SAFETY_KILLED. With hands near the propellers, use the physical switch: it cuts ESC power in hardware and doesn't depend on any software or firmware working.

The kill system

The kill is decided in firmware on the POAC board (kill_switch.c). It has four slots, one per source of kill reports:

SlotUsed by, today
0 KILL_SWITCH_PHYSICALThe red handle on the port bow window (photo), read by the board itself.
1 KILL_SWITCH_RQT_CONTROLLERThe Nereus viewer (the name is from the old rqt panel).
2 KILL_SWITCH_TOPSIDE_BUTTONA topside kill box, riptide_kill_switch (last changed 2023). The opbox client's slot-2 publisher is commented out.
3 KILL_SWITCH_DEBUGThe command line and scripts, like the pre-test tool.

The rule, checked on every safety tick:

\text{killed} = \text{no slot enabled} \;\lor\; \exists\,\text{enabled slot}: \big(\text{asserting} \lor (\text{needs\_update} \land \text{silent} > 500\text{ ms})\big)
  • A slot is enabled from its first report until the board reboots. Slot 0 is always enabled (safety_interface.c), so with the switch in and no software slot asserting, Talos is enabled: software slots can only add kills.
  • switch_needs_update = true makes a report a heartbeat: it must repeat within 500 ms, so if the sender dies, the robot is killed. false latches: the slot keeps that value until the next report on it.
  • A kill takes a lock: only the same sender_id can clear it. Anyone else's clear is rejected with FAULT_ROS_BAD_COMMAND, and a new kill from anyone takes the lock over (ros.c).

Slot 1 belongs to the Nereus viewer. It reports every 50 ms, asserting kill until you press Enable (uwrt_motion.cpp). Enable latches, so unplugging the tether or closing the viewer leaves slot 1 clear and an untethered run continues. Any other sender on slot 1 counts as a competing operator: the viewer drops its enable and refuses Enable until that sender has been quiet for 1 s.

Try it below, with the real rules and timeouts: step through a story, or open the controls and work them yourself.

The POAC board's verdict drives a soft-kill pin that cuts ESC power (safety_interface.c) and goes out on state/kill. The ESC boards treat that as a heartbeat of their own: killed, silent for 500 ms or not yet heard, and they kill too (ros.c). After power returns they wait 5 s (ESC_POWERON_DELAY) for the ESCs to start. The hardware path (switch, DG448, P-FETs) is in The circuit boards.

Gotchas

  • Short sender ids. sender_id must be under 32 characters, or the board rejects the report with FAULT_ROS_BAD_COMMAND and nothing tells the sender (ros.c). The viewer's is viewer_<host>_<pid>_<n>: on a machine with a long host name the board ignores every report from it, KILL included.
  • The Enable key becomes KILL whenever the robot is seen enabled, even if this viewer didn't enable it (motion_panel.cpp). KILL also stops any running behaviour tree, even one started elsewhere (composition.cpp). It is a button, never a keyboard shortcut.
  • The viewer's watchdogs never kill. They only release manual control.
  • The board starts killed, and stays killed while it has no micro-ROS connection (main.c).
  • Actuators stay live. A kill blocks arming and torpedo fire (ros_torp.c), but not the dropper, and it doesn't disarm armed actuators (a TODO).

Old tools you'll see in the repo

  • Joystick teleop doesn't work with the MPC. riptide_teleop2 has a COLCON_IGNORE and isn't built. RocketLeague.py is still installed, but was written for the old controller: under the MPC it would fly Talos to the odom origin (L314).
  • The launch default is the old controller. active_control_model in control_system.launch.py starts riptide_mpc only when it is mpc. It defaults to hybrid, the old Simulink-built complete_controller, which is no longer built, so nothing flies. Bringup and Nereus keep that default unless you say otherwise. In the simulator: 
    ros2 launch nereus sim.launch.py \
  active_control_model:=mpc \
  mpc_model:=sim
    Of the remote-launch files, only the three ending in _mpc.yaml pass mpc; plain talos_launch.yaml does not.
  • Still used from riptide_controllers2: controller_overseer.py (motion_enabled, FF_body_force, and a setTeleop service the viewer calls that now just answers success), calibrate_drag.py, and thruster_test_canbus, the bench thruster test: 300 rpm on each thruster in turn for 3 s, and Ctrl-C sends zeros (source, Pool test day).

Try it from a terminal

In Nereus, or on the robot with the propellers clear and someone at the switch. First, who is killing it?

# true = killed
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/kill
# every slot report, with its sender
ros2 topic echo /talos/command/software_kill
# kill_switches_* bitmasks, per board
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/firmware

Move 1 m forward of where Talos is now, slowly, keeping its attitude. A point in the robot's own frame is resolved once, when the goal arrives:

ros2 action send_goal --feedback \
  /talos/follow_path \
  riptide_msgs2/action/FollowPath \
  "{path_points: [{
     header: {frame_id: talos/base_link},
     pose: {position: {x: 1.0},
            orientation: {w: 0.0}}}],
    max_speed: 0.2}"

Hold a setpoint with the topics. A POSITION setpoint is a point in odom, not an offset, so read where Talos is first:

ros2 topic echo --once \
  /talos/odometry/filtered \
  --field pose.pose.position

Send both halves. An unset quaternion becomes w: 1 ("face +x"), so write w: 0.0 to hold the attitude. Here Talos is at x = 2.1, y = −0.4 and goes to 1 m depth:

ros2 topic pub --once \
  /talos/controller/angular \
  riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand \
  "{mode: 3, setpoint_quat: {w: 0.0}}"
ros2 topic pub --once \
  /talos/controller/linear \
  riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand \
  "{mode: 3, setpoint_vect:
     {x: 2.1, y: -0.4, z: -1.0}}"
# thrusters off: Talos floats
ros2 topic pub --once \
  /talos/controller/linear \
  riptide_msgs2/msg/ControllerCommand \
  "{mode: 0}"

A software kill of your own goes on slot 3, with a short sender id of your own (under 32 characters):

ros2 topic pub -r 20 \
  /talos/command/software_kill \
  riptide_msgs2/msg/KillSwitchReport \
  "{sender_id: cli_$USER,
    kill_switch_id: 3,
    switch_asserting_kill: true,
    switch_needs_update: true}"

Stopping that publisher doesn't un-kill: slot 3 times out, which counts as a kill. To clear it, stop the publisher, then send a latched clear from the same sender. That keeps slot 3 clear until the next report on it (or a reboot).

Before you clear slot 3

If no other slot is asserting kill when you clear it, Talos is enabled at once, with nobody driving. Make sure something else holds a kill first: the viewer open and not enabled, or a needs_update kill of your own on slot 1 that will time out into a kill. That is the order in the simulator's Bench hold story.

# after Ctrl-C on the kill publisher
ros2 topic pub --times 10 \
  /talos/command/software_kill \
  riptide_msgs2/msg/KillSwitchReport \
  "{sender_id: cli_$USER,
    kill_switch_id: 3,
    switch_asserting_kill: false,
    switch_needs_update: false}"

In Nereus none of this does anything: the simulator only honours slot 1 (the viewer's) and has no timeout (bridge.yaml), so practise slot 3 only against the real POAC board, with the propellers clear.
