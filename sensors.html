The vehicle · Chapter 7

Navigation sensors

Before Talos can steer, it has to know how it is tilted, how fast it is turning and moving, and how deep it is. Four sensors answer those questions, and a hydrophone listens for the pinger. What each one measures, how far to trust it, and how it fails.

The short version
  • Four navigation sensors: a VectorNav VN-100 IMU (tilt, turn rates, acceleration), a fibre-optic gyro (yaw rate), a Nortek DVL1000 (velocity over the pool floor) and a Bar02 pressure sensor (depth).
  • No sensor measures x, y or compass heading. Talos gets them by adding up velocity and yaw rate, so they drift, and the camera map re-anchors them.
  • Rates: IMU 400 Hz, gyro about 475 Hz, depth 20 Hz, DVL 8 Hz. Each DVL reading is about 125 ms old when it arrives.
  • The EKF and the MPC's estimator fuse them (Chapter 9). Nereus simulates all four with measured noise, but not their failures.
  • Most sensor trouble is mundane: a wrong covariance, depth zeroed underwater, a DVL too close to the floor, a stale config file.

Cameras have their own chapter (Chapter 8). The plots here use real data: two bench recordings of Talos sitting still (2026-10-27) and an untethered pool run (2026-10-26).

Frames

  • Poses. talos.yaml gives each sensor a pose in the origin frame (the front port screw of the camera cage); the xacro turns them into talos/imu_link, fog_link, dvl_link and pressure_link (how TF works).
  • Most are on /tf, not /tf_static. The DVL, pressure, FOG and hydrophone joints are revolute in snippets.xacro, so they're on /tf; imu_link is fixed, on /tf_static. A tool that only reads /tf_static won't find dvl_link.
  • Two sensors are mounted turned: the IMU upside down and turned 90°, the DVL turned 45°. TF rotates their data into base_link. One exception: the IMU driver already re-expresses the VN-100's orientation (main.cpp L219); its rates and accelerations stay in imu_link axes.
  • Moving a sensor? Change talos.yaml and restart. The MPC reads the IMU and DVL mounts from that file itself and, once TF is up, checks them against TF once (checkMountsAgainstTf, retried every 2 s until TF answers): its log says "mount matches TF" or warns "mount in the vehicle YAML differs from TF by … deg / … cm".

Rates and timestamps

The sensors run at very different rates and stamp their messages differently. One real second from the pool run, as messages reached the Orin:

  • Stamped on arrival: the IMU and the gyro. Their real age is a few milliseconds of serial transfer.
  • Stamped when measured: the DVL (the driver subtracts the age the DVL reports).
  • Depth: stamped when the board publishes on master (35 to 45 ms late, on a clock that drifts); stamped at the middle of the pressure conversion only on the unmerged fix/depth-timestamps branch (see Depth).
  • Why it matters. The MPC's estimator replays measurements in stamp order (mpc_controller_node.cpp). The EKF has smooth_lagged_data off, one reason its velocity lags the DVL by 0.2 to 0.35 s (Chapter 9).

The IMU: VectorNav VN-100-T

An IMU measures how Talos turns and accelerates. The VN-100 has MEMS gyros, accelerometers, magnetometers and a barometer; its own Kalman filter blends the drifting gyros with gravity into roll and pitch.

Model
VectorNav VN-100-T (T: temperature calibrated), sponsored, 2022 (2026 design report)
Datasheet
Gyro noise 0.0035 °/s/√Hz, in-run bias <10 °/h; pitch/roll 0.5° RMS (VectorNav)
Measured
Rate noise 0.00066 to 0.0011 rad/s per sample at 400 Hz, as the datasheet predicts (0.00086 rad/s over a 200 Hz band)
Link
Serial (RS-232 through the Orin HAT, Chapter 3), 460,800 baud, /dev/ttyTHS1
Driver

  • The driver doesn't configure the output. Rate and content are saved on the VN-100 itself; change them through the register service and save. (vectornav_imu_config.yaml is for a third-party driver Talos doesn't launch.)
  • It reboots the sensor on every connect (vs->reset()), so a serial hiccup leaves a gap in the data while the VN-100 restarts.

Axes and what gets used

The IMU's pose is roll 180°, yaw −90°, so body roll rate = −y, pitch rate = −x, yaw rate = −z (on the bench, the IMU's z rate and the FOG's correlate at −0.999).

  • EKF (talos_ekf.yaml): roll and pitch, their rates, and the three accelerations. Not yaw, not yaw rate.
  • MPC estimator: IMU roll and pitch rates, FOG yaw rate (the IMU's yaw rate only if the FOG goes quiet for 0.1 s, state_estimator.cpp), and roll and pitch as a slow correction with a 1 s time constant.
  • Nobody uses the magnetometer or the IMU's yaw. The driver still creates vectornav/magnetometer and vectornav/magheading but no longer publishes them (main.cpp); the EKF has IMU yaw off and the MPC takes only tilt from the IMU orientation. Yaw is the integral of the FOG.

A covariance that is 10,000× too big

The EKF weighs each sensor by the covariances in its messages, which the driver copies from its YAML. angular_velocity_covariance is 0.01 (rad/s)², σ = 0.1 rad/s. The real noise is about 0.001 rad/s, a variance near 10−6. With its own rate process noise near 10−5, the EKF believes its model far more than the gyros.

With 0.01, the EKF's roll rate is about a third of the size and almost half a second late; its angles are fine (25 to 55 ms late). The MPC isn't affected, but mapping reads EKF rates (How far to trust a detection), so the fix is worth making in vectornav.yaml. IMU failures: see Troubleshooting.

The fibre-optic gyro

A MEMS gyro's bias wanders by degrees per hour, and gravity can't correct yaw. So Talos has a better gyro for yaw alone. A fibre-optic gyro (FOG) sends light both ways around a fibre coil; turning shifts the phase between the beams (the Sagnac effect). No moving parts, so its bias stays put.

Model
Fizoptika VG103S-2LND, bought 2024 (design report), ±200 °/s (distributor)
Axis
Body z. Its pose is all zeros: every point of a rigid body turns at the same rate, so only the axis matters.
Link
RS-422, 921,600 baud, /dev/ttyTHS2. 8-byte frames: sync 0xDD, 3 bytes of rate, frame id, a status byte (temperature, supply, light source or diagnostics, by turns), checksum.
Driver
riptide_gyro (driver.cpp), config gyro_config.yaml
Publishes
gyro/twist (angular.z only, variance 1e-4), throttled to ≤500 Hz; gyro/raw (counts, same rate); gyro/status (50 Hz)
Measured
0.0012 rad/s per sample; bias after tare −0.3 and −0.7 °/h (two bench runs)

Per sample the FOG is noisier than the VN-100, but fast noise averages out when you integrate, and bias doesn't. Integrate both over 90 s of sitting still:

  • Run 1 started right after tilting Talos by hand. For about 50 s the VN-100's yaw rate was off and built up 0.57° of heading error, then it levelled off.
  • Run 2: the VN-100 wandered by at most 0.06° (1.2 °/h on average).
  • The FOG stayed within 0.02° in both runs.
  • So a MEMS yaw error changes with motion and from run to run. Even the datasheet's in-run bias (up to 10 °/h) adds up to 3° over a 20-minute mission, enough to miss a gate. That is why yaw comes from the FOG.

Calibration: from counts to rad/s

The FOG sends a signed 24-bit number. The driver turns it into rad/s with a surface fitted on the team's calibration rig, with x the raw reading and T the FOG's temperature, both normalized by a mean and standard deviation from the config:

\omega_z = a + b\,x^3 + c\,x\,T + d\cos(f x + g) \;-\; \omega_{\text{tare}}

Tare

  • What it does. The gyro/tare action averages num_samples readings (viewer default 100,000, 20 s timeout) and subtracts the average from every later reading. It's saved to a tare file, so it survives restarts.
  • Only with Talos still and level. The FOG feels the Earth turn (about 9.7 °/h about the vertical in Columbus) and tare cancels that too, so a tare taken tilted or moving bakes an error into every yaw. The pre-test tool never tares on its own.

FOG failures (temperature, hardware flags, a bad tare): see Troubleshooting.

The DVL: Nortek DVL1000

A Doppler velocity log pings sound at the floor through four slanted beams. When Talos moves, each echo comes back slightly shifted in frequency, which gives the speed along that beam.

Model
Nortek DVL1000 (300 m rating), on the team since 2018 (design report)
Datasheet
1 MHz, four beams 25° from vertical, 0.8 cm/s single-ping noise, 8 Hz max, bottom track from 0.1 m to 75 m above the floor (Nortek)
Link
Ethernet, 192.168.1.212, data on TCP 9004
Driver
nortek_dvl, branch DvlWaterTrackFallback (dvl_driver.cpp)

dvl_twist (bottom track, when valid), dvl/bottom_lock, dvl/water_twist, dvl_sonar0–3 (beam ranges), dvl and dvl/status. All but dvl/bottom_lock and the sonar topics are only published while something subscribes.
Covariance
The DVL's figure of merit squared, per ping: median 1.7×10−6 (m/s)² in the pool
Measured

Settings live on the DVL

The reference is the DVL's own web page (Measurement Configuration, http://192.168.1.212/cgi-bin/cgi_dvlGetConfig), not the out-of-date DVLConfig.txt. Where they differ:

  • Read the page with a plain HTTP GET. Entering command mode (ports 9000, 9001) stops the measurement.
  • Speed of sound sets the scale. Velocity is proportional to it, and in fresh water it depends on temperature: about 1482 m/s at 20 °C, 1500 m/s at 27 °C. The DVL is set to 1480 fixed, so in a warm pool it may read about 1 % low. Compare with the pool temperature (state/depth/temp).

What the driver does with a ping

  • Sorts the sentences: bottom track ($PNORBT9) to dvl_twist, water track ($PNORWT9, velocity relative to the water) to dvl/water_twist, a fallback nothing uses yet.
  • Stamps the past: arrival time minus the echo's age (dt2, which the DVL reports as a negative number) (L150), median 125 ms in the pool (123 to 175 ms, 5th to 95th percentile).
  • Flips to ROS axes (y and z negated) and decides lock: true when x, y and one of the two z velocities are valid.

A DVL turned 45°

dvl_link has yaw −45°, so driving forward shows up as equal x and y in the DVL's axes. The DVL is also 24 cm below the centre of mass: pitching at 0.5 rad/s alone makes it read 12 cm/s of surge. The MPC estimator removes that lever arm (v_\text{DVL} = v + \omega \times r, state_estimator.cpp).

DVL failures (lost lock, no data, missing topics): see Troubleshooting.

Depth: a pressure sensor on the camera cage

Water pressure grows by \rho g, about 9.8 kPa per metre, so:

h = \frac{p - p_\text{surface}}{\rho\, g}, \qquad \rho = 997\ \text{kg/m}^3,\; g = 9.80665\ \text{m/s}^2
Sensor
Blue Robotics Bar02 (TE MS5837-02BA, 2 bar range) on I²C; the firmware starts it as an MS5837_02BA
Board
The camera cage board (CameraCageBB, Chapter 3): its RP2040 publishes over CAN through micro-ROS (Chapter 4)
Rate
20 Hz; oversampling 8192, about 16 ms per conversion
Publishes
state/depth/raw (variance 0.003, σ ≈ 5.5 cm: very cautious, the real noise is under a millimetre) and state/depth/temp (water, 1 Hz)
Then
depth_converter.py publishes depth/pose: base_link's z, negative below the surface
  • Zeroed at power-up. The firmware discards 20 readings, then filters the next 20 (an exponential average) into the surface pressure (depth.c), and keeps it across a watchdog reset. Powered up underwater, every depth is off by the starting depth. CLEAR_DEPTH (a viewer button) re-zeroes it at the surface.
  • Fresh-water density is built in. In salt water (1025 kg/m³) the same pressure means 2.7 % less depth.
  • Lever arm. The sensor is 13.5 cm above, 16.5 cm behind and 15.5 cm to starboard of base_link; at 10° pitch that changes its height by about 3 cm, so depth_converter.py rotates the offset with the attitude.
  • Timestamps. On master, the board syncs its clock with ROS once at start-up and stamps each reading when it publishes, 35 to 45 ms after sampling; its clock then drifts about 150 ms per hour on Talos. The fix/depth-timestamps branch (not merged yet) re-syncs every 5 s and stamps the middle of the pressure conversion.

Acoustics: which side is the pinger on

At RoboSub a pinger marks either the torpedo or the table task. Talos doesn't find its direction. It compares how loud it sounds at two places:

  1. An Aquarian Audio H1C hydrophone, the same one the inter-vehicle link (IVC) listens with, goes through a preamplifier and analog band-pass filters to the IVC board's RP2040 ADC (2025 design report).
  2. Firmware filters the samples at the selected pinger frequency (20, 25, 30, 35, 37 or 40 kHz) and streams the amplitude on ivc/pinger/selected_freq_amp_stream (IVCBoard2 on the blake/ivc branch; master has no pinger topics).
  3. Only in pinger mode. The board boots with pinger mode off and streams nothing until a true arrives on ivc/pinger/enable (ivc.c). Nothing in the robot software sends it: the Nereus viewer's electrical panel does (Pinger, on by default) and re-sends it every second, so a rebooted board recovers (talos_uwrt_panels.yaml). The same panel shows the amplitude stream. No viewer, no amplitudes, and the mission's pinger step fails.
  4. pinger_broker re-sends the frequency to the board (ivc/pinger/set_freq_khz) every second, in case it reboots. It starts at 30 kHz; the viewer's frequency choice (25, 30, 35, 37 or 40 kHz) reaches it on ivc/pinger/set_freq_broker_khz.
  5. riptide_acoustics fills buffer 0 between acoustics/start_sample and stop_sample, then buffer 1, then publishes acoustics/buffer_0_closer (true: buffer 0 louder) every second. In max mode (the launch default) it compares averages if the peaks are within 1000; otherwise the old answer stays.
  6. The competition mission's RandomPingerTree (RepairCompMpcTree.xml) listens 10 s at pre_torpedo_pose (buffer 0), then at pre_table_pose, and reads the Bool into is_table. If nothing arrives in 3 s, GetBoolTopic writes false (torpedo first) and fails (more).
Known gaps
  • The order looks inverted. buffer_0_closer true means the torpedo stop was louder, but the tree stores it as is_table, so a louder torpedo pinger sends Talos to the table first (RoboSub).
  • A stale answer in max mode. If the peaks differ by 1000 or more, compare_max_buffers sets nothing, and the node keeps publishing the previous result (false after start-up).
  • Nereus has no pinger. In sim the comparison fails ("Cannot compare") and the run goes torpedo first.
  • talos.yaml lists two hydrophone poses, port and starboard. They fit the two-hydrophone direction-finding design in the 2024 design report; nothing in the current code uses them.
