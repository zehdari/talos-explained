Mechanical · Chapter 1

Hull, frame and layout

Talos is a sealed two-tube hull held in an open aluminium frame, with eight thrusters, two battery pods and its tools bolted on outside. Where everything sits, why it sits there, and how to get inside.

The short version
  • An open aluminium frame carries everything. In the middle sits the main hull: two clear tubes, one for the computer, one for the circuit boards. Batteries, sensors and tools mount outside.
  • 925 mm wide, 639 mm tall, 582 mm long without the claw; 32.4 kg dry on a scale.
  • All eight T200 thrusters sit in one cross-section: four at the corners push fore and aft, four inner ones are tilted 45°.
  • Each tube opens at its stern lid, and its electronics cage comes out with the lid. Every time the hull is closed it gets a leak test.

The numbers come from the team's SolidWorks CAD (Talos 7-11-26, saved 11 July 2026), the vehicle config talos.yaml and the MPC's model of the real robot. Where the CAD disagrees with the robot or a scale, the robot wins (what the CAD doesn't show).

The numbers

Size
925 × 720 × 711 mm (width × height × length) with the claw and magnet poker; 925 × 639 × 582 mm without (CAD). The RoboSub box is 0.9 × 0.9 × 1.8 m, so the width only fits along its long side.
Mass
32.4 kg dry on the scale (34.1 kg weighed wet); the MPC model uses 34.3 kg (talos.yaml, model file).
Inertia
1.84, 1.01 and 1.86 kg·m² in roll, pitch and yaw (July CAD). Pitch is the easy axis: the heavy parts are spread sideways.
Thrusters
8 Blue Robotics T200, limited to ±24 N each and 73 N in total (Thrusters and allocation).
Hull
Two polycarbonate tubes and bow windows with machined 6061 aluminium rings and lids, made by Xometry in 2021 (2026 report, parts table).
Frame
Aluminium C-channel and 5052-H32 plates in the corner modules, 6061 angle in the cross bars, stainless cables and 316 turnbuckles; from 2023. Fasteners are 18-8 stainless.
Batteries
Two MaxAmps 8000 mAh 5S2P 18.5 V LiPo packs in milled 6061 aluminium pods (Batteries and charging).
Printed parts
Cages, mounts, spacers, launcher and claw bodies, the kill switch handle; TPU for the claw jaws and some cage mounts (2026, 2024 reports). Ask the mechanical lead which filament each part uses.

SolidWorks gives 34.7 kg for the visible model (talos.yaml), 2.3 kg more than the scale, so use the CAD to compare parts, not to weigh the robot.

Why it looks like this

Talos was built for RoboSub 2023. Three choices from the team's report that year still shape it (2023 report):

  • Triangles, not sheets. The previous AUV hung everything on polyethylene sheets. Talos uses aluminium triangles tied with steel cables, checked by finite-element analysis: 2.7 kg lighter, 47 cm narrower.
  • Tools under the hull: central, out of the thrusters' wash, and in the cameras' view.
  • Adjustable balance. The battery pods slide fore and aft on indexed rails to move the centre of mass; foam blocks move the centre of buoyancy.
YearChange since
2024Turnbuckles, so the frame comes apart. L-channel bars instead of square tube (screws fell inside). Bottles on top for buoyancy. ¼-20 battery rods instead of bending 8-32. Colour-coded SubConns, rubber bumpers (2024 report).
2025Rails press-fit inside the hull; the cages slide in with no screws. A ZED X Mini down camera against the bottom of the hull (2025 report).
2026A new claw with ribbed TPU jaws on a hinged mount, and a neutrally buoyant torpedo (2026 report).

The frame

  • Four corner modules: two upper ("Dorito" in the CAD) and two lower ("Taco"). Each C-channel triangle carries two T200s with guards, a foam block and rubber handles; the upper two also have crane hooks.
  • Two 432 mm cross bars under the hull carry the tools; four rails carry the battery pods.
  • Eight stainless cables brace the modules. Cables only pull, so turnbuckles tension them; slacken those to take the frame apart.

Eight thrusters in one plane

All eight T200s sit 1–2 cm ahead of the centre of mass, in one cross-section. Their names in talos.yaml say where they are: V, "vector" (the outer corners, pushing fore and aft), or H, "heave" (inner, tilted 45° to push up or down and sideways); then Upper or Lower; then Port or Starboard.

Surge, pitch and yaw use only the four corners; heave, sway and roll use almost only the tilted four. More in Thrusters and allocation (the 6×8 matrix) and Thrusters and ESCs (motors and wiring).

The main hull

The dry space for the computer, the boards and both cameras: two tubes side by side, joined by aluminium blocks at the bow and stern.

Tubes
Clear polycarbonate cylinders, 146 mm long, each clamped between two aluminium rings by four stainless tension rods. With the rings, window and lid, the whole housing is about 400 mm long.
Bow
A flat 3 mm polycarbonate window about 202 mm across on each tube. The forward camera looks out of the starboard one.
Stern
A removable lid on each tube, held by draw latches. Every cable into the hull goes through SubConn bulkhead connectors on the lids.
Seals
Static O-rings: four X-profile lid O-rings (nitrile), four square-profile O-rings where the tubes meet the rings, and two lid gaskets.

Keeping water out

  • Connectors: MacArtney SubConn bulkheads (MC, HP and D series; 2026 report), colour-coded so plugs go back where they came from.
  • The leak test (the depressurization test, PVT): pump the hull down through the port lid's vacuum valve to 0.05 bar below air pressure, then watch pressure and temperature for five minutes; a leak lets air back in. Pumping down is safer than pressurising (2025 report). When it passes, the green SubConn cover goes on: it means sealed and tested.
  • No leak sensor any more: a rising humidity reading from the boards is the early warning (housekeeping sensors).

Inside: two cages

  • Starboard, the camera cage (also called the computer cage): the Jetson AGX Orin, its GMSL2 camera card, the forward ZED X Mini, the VN-100 IMU, a 40 mm fan, and shelves for the network switch, fibre-optic gyro and breakout board (Computers and networks).
  • Port, the board stack: circuit boards behind a 90 mm fan, whose plate holds the Hall-effect kill switch sensor right behind the red handle (The circuit boards).
  • The down camera is a second ZED X Mini inside the starboard tube, against its bottom wall, looking down through the clear plastic; mounting it right against the wall keeps the curved shell's distortion small. It meets its cage through a slide-in adapter, so there's no cable to plug in by hand (2025 report; The cameras).
  • Rails: since 2025 the cages slide on rails press-fit into the hull (the old screwed-in ones stripped).

Battery pods

  • Two milled aluminium pods, one per side on the frame's rails, each about 82 × 150 × 380 mm. The lid seals with an O-ring, each pod has a pressure-relief valve, and a window shows the pack's display (2023 report).
  • Swapped without tools. Compliant clips snap the pods onto indexed rails, and each has its own SubConn, so changing one never opens the main hull and its leak test still holds. What's inside: Batteries and charging.

Sensors and tools outside the hull

Anything that has to touch the water, or can be waterproofed on its own, mounts outside.

Buoyancy and trim

Weight pulls down at the centre of mass (COM); buoyancy, the weight of the water Talos displaces, pushes up at the centre of buoyancy (COB). From the MPC's model (models/talos.yaml; volume and COB fitted in the pool, mass from the newer CAD):

Weight
34.307 kg × g = 336.4 N, at the COM
Buoyancy
34.5 L of water × g = 338.1 N, at the COB
Net
+1.6 N up: Talos floats, but just under RoboSub's minimum of 0.5 % of its weight (1.68 N), the rule that makes a killed robot surface. The untethered fit of 2026-10-26 put it right on the line. Check it on the robot before a competition (at the pool).
COB
22.5 mm above the COM, so Talos rolls back upright; 8.2 mm ahead of it, so with the thrusters off it hangs nose-up.
  • Barely positive on purpose: the thrusters hold every extra newton of float down for the whole run.
  • The float is mostly the air in the main hull, plus Blue Robotics R-3312 foam (one block per corner module, one between the tubes) and the two bottles added in 2024 when new mechanisms made Talos heavier.

Adding a part moves both the weight and its centre. Try it:

The MPC doesn't know you added anything: it learns the unexplained force and torque as a disturbance, so Talos still flies, but its model is now wrong. The model file's rule: "Change the body and re-identify." Weigh the robot, update the mass in talos.yaml and the model file, and run pool identification again, untethered.

Opening Talos up

  • Power off first: kill switch out, then unplug both battery connectors at the port lid (ask an electrical lead about shutting the Orin down first; Power and wiring).
  • Into the hull: release a stern lid's latches and draw the lid out. Its cage comes out with it on the rails, still wired to the lid's connectors (the second photo above). Check with the mechanical lead before your first time.
  • Batteries: unplug the pod's SubConn, then unclip the pod from its rails; the main hull stays sealed.
  • The frame: slacken the turnbuckles to free the cables before unbolting modules.
  • Carrying it: handles on every corner module, crane hooks on the upper two. The claw arm's hinged mount locks with retaining pins; rotate it up for transport so Talos can rest on a flat surface (2026 report).
  • The tether is Ethernet to the switch in the camera cage (the robot LAN and the tether).
Every time the hull is opened
  • Inspect and clean the O-ring sealing surfaces inside both lids before closing (the first sign-off in pretest bench).
  • Redo the leak test, and put the green SubConn cover back on only when it passes (how).
  • Before a pool test, once the checks are done, nobody opens Talos or swaps a part (pool test).

What the CAD doesn't show

  • Anything after July 2026. talos.yaml notes the robot's down camera is rolled 180° from the CAD, and gives the torpedo pose "as deployed 2026-10-03". The launcher here is the V8. The newer "TalosMostlyGood" assembly (2026-10-24, new external buoyancy; talos_untuned.yaml) isn't shown here.
  • A true mass (above), the tether, or any cables but the thruster, battery and DVL ones.
  • The config's origin. Shifting the CAD's camera positions by about (−0.14, +0.035, 0) m lands them within 6 mm of the ffc and dfc poses, which talos.yaml took from this CAD; the thrusters differ by 3–5 mm sideways. Treat CAD positions as ±5 mm.
