The vehicle · Chapter 9

Where am I? State estimation

Every move Talos makes starts from an estimate of where it is, which way it points and how fast it is going. No sensor measures all of that. A Kalman filter blends four of them, and two more layers tie the result to the course.

The short version
  • An EKF (robot_localization) fuses the IMU, fibre-optic gyro, DVL and depth sensor into odometry/filtered and odom → talos/base_link, 30 times a second.
  • Only depth, roll and pitch are measured outright. x, y and heading are added up from zero, so they drift.
  • An AprilTag calibration sets world → map, which ties that to the course.
  • The EKF runs late (velocity about 0.35 s, roll and pitch rates 0.34–0.46 s), from three settings. The fix, found in replays, isn't committed yet.
  • The MPC builds its own on-time state; mapping, autonomy and the viewer use the EKF.

What "where" means: frames

ROS keeps every position as a transform between named frames in one tree, TF (how TF works). Talos's pose is the transform from a fixed frame to talos/base_link, the body.

  • Axes. Every Talos frame is x forward, y to port (left), z up (robot_base.xacro). Roll, pitch, yaw turn about x, y, z.
  • Velocity is in the body. odometry/filtered gives the pose in odom but surge, sway and heave in talos/base_link.

Where the sensors sit: the URDF

Every sensor pose comes from talos.yaml; talos.xacro turns it into a URDF, which robot_state_publisher publishes (navigation.launch.py).

  • The datum and the body. Poses are measured from talos/origin, a CAD datum (a screw at the front of the camera cage). talos/base_link sits at [-0.14, 0.03, -0.09] m from it (L5).
  • Two sensors are mounted turned. The IMU is upside down and turned −90°; the DVL is turned −45°. The EKF rotates each message into base_link with TF.
  • The DVL measures its own point, 16 cm below base_link, so pitching reads as surge. robot_localization removes that lever arm, v = R\,v_\text{DVL} + r \times \omega (ros_filter.cpp).
  • Most sensor frames are on /tf, not /tf_static: zero-range revolute joints (snippets.xacro). A bag tool that reads only /tf_static won't find dvl_link.

The EKF: predict, then correct

A Kalman filter keeps its best guess of the state, x, and how unsure it is, the covariance P. Every message runs one loop:

  1. Predict to the message's time: position moves by velocity, velocity by acceleration, and uncertainty grows by the process noise Q times the time step (ekf.cpp).
  2. Correct with the measurement z and its covariance R. The gain K decides how far to move.
P \leftarrow F P F^\top + Q\,\Delta t, \qquad K = P H^\top (H P H^\top + R)^{-1}, \qquad x \leftarrow x + K\,(z - Hx)

For one number, K = P / (P + R): if the filter is much surer of itself than of the sensor, a measurement barely moves it. So R and Q are the tuning, and their ratio is what counts. The model is pure kinematics (velocity and acceleration carry on); it knows nothing of Talos's thrust, mass or drag.

What Talos fuses

The state has 15 numbers: position, angles, velocities, rates and accelerations, three of each. talos_ekf.yaml says which ones each sensor feeds.

  • The IMU gives no yaw. Its yaw and yaw rate are off (L35–39: "Taking out yaw from imu since fog rate is lower now"), and its magnetometer isn't published (the IMU). The FOG owns yaw rate.
  • Depth is the one absolute position. depth_converter.py corrects the reading for the sensor sitting 13.5 cm above base_link, turned by the attitude.
  • Left out: the ZED IMUs; the DVL's water-track velocity (relative to the water, not the floor) on dvl/water_twist (dvl_driver.cpp); and AprilTag odometry: talos_ekf_tag_odom.yaml fuses tag_odom/pose in place of the DVL and FOG, only with tag_odom_enabled:=True.

The filter trusts each message by the covariance in the message, copied by the drivers from config. Most are far off:

  • Under-trusted: the gyro by about 10,000×, depth by thousands, the FOG by 80×. The filter leans on its own model instead, and lags.
  • Over-trusted: the DVL. Its figure of merit is its own per-ping precision estimate, about 50× below its real scatter. That makes the estimate noisier, not later.
  • Leave the attitude alone. The IMU's roll and pitch come out of its own filter, so their scatter understates their error, and the EKF's angles were on time (below).

odom drifts; map holds the course

odom's origin is wherever base_link was when the EKF started, facing that way. From then on, every error in velocity and yaw rate adds up.

  • world → odom is a static identity, published by navigation.launch.py "until we get SLAM".
  • world → map is where the course is, from a chameleon_tf node, world_to_map (mapping.launch.py); identity until calibrated.
  • map → offset is mapping's correction, learned from detections; every course object hangs below it (Frames, targets and the mission tree).

The AprilTag calibration

Before a run, Talos faces the calibration board's tag and the operator presses Calibrate in the viewer's Mapping panel.

  1. apriltag_ros sees tag 36h11 id 0 (0.508 m) in the forward camera and publishes tag36h11:0 (apriltag.launch.py).
  2. A static transform puts estimated_origin_frame, the course map's origin, 0.4572 m (18 in) from the tag.
  3. chameleon_tf's map/model_tf action takes 10 samples of world → estimated_origin_frame, through the EKF's pose.
  4. It averages them, rejects a position spread over 0.5 m, and adopts x, y and yaw as world → map; z, roll and pitch are locked (mobile_tf.cpp).
Order matters
  • The calibration captures where odom is now. If the EKF restarts, odom starts over and the old world → map is wrong: calibrate again.
  • In Nereus it isn't needed: the scenario's world is map and Talos starts at (0, 0, −1) facing +x (scenario.yaml), so odom and map already agree.

Who uses the estimate

  • Mapping (mapping.py) grows the course's drift with distance travelled, widens detections taken while turning or moving (motion_at, How far to trust a detection), and places each detection with the TF pose at its image stamp.
  • Autonomy nodes such as GetOdometry read odometry/filtered and look up targets through TF.
  • The viewer draws Talos at the EKF's estimate on the robot; in sim, truth and estimate side by side.
  • The MPC takes its state from its own estimator (state_source: sensors, mpc.yaml). The EKF only anchors x, y and heading (2 s time constants), stands in for a stale sensor, and re-seeds it past 1 m or 0.5 rad of disagreement (state_estimator.cpp). Why its own? A late state is a wrong state for a controller planning 1.5 s ahead every 50 ms (Feedback and model error).

Checking it

  • Running? ros2 topic hz /talos/odometry/filtered: about 30 Hz. On the robot it comes from the navigation entry of talos_launch_mpc.yaml, which waits for it (the launcher).
  • Where? ros2 run tf2_ros tf2_echo world talos/base_link. In sim, the viewer's overlay gives the gap to truth.
  • Restart the navigation entry in the launcher, then calibrate again. With the controller running, stopping it stops the thrusters (why the order matters).

The EKF is late

The team measured the EKF against its own inputs on the bench, tilting Talos by hand (2026-10-27), and in the untethered pool identification run (2026-10-26).

  • Roll and pitch rates trailed the IMU by 455 ms at 12–15 % of the size on the bench; 335–340 ms at 19–23 % in the pool.
  • Velocity trailed the DVL by about 0.35 s (351 ms median over 16 surge and sway starts).
  • Fine: roll and pitch angles (0–55 ms) and yaw rate from the FOG (85–90 ms).

Three causes:

  1. The gyro covariance: 0.01 reported, about 10⁻⁶ real, against a rate process noise of 10⁻⁵. The filter believes its model over the gyro.
  2. Tiny velocity process noise, 2.5×10⁻⁵ (L81–91; the file says "This covariance was not calculated"): the filter trusts "velocity carries on" over each new DVL reading.
  3. Late DVL readings, about 125 ms old on arrival. With smooth_lagged_data off, each corrects the current state as if just measured (filter_base.cpp).

Try the first and third below. The second needs all 15 states, so this two-state toy can't show it.

  • Pitch rate. At 0.01 the toy is 0.55 s late at half size (the real filter: 0.34–0.46 s, ×0.12–0.23). At 10⁻⁵, 45 ms, as in the real replay. At 10⁻⁸ the lag vanishes but the gyro's noise passes straight through.
  • Surge. Without smoothing, even a fully trusted DVL stays over 125 ms behind: the readings' age. Smoothing rewinds to each reading's stamp, so the floor drops. Real DVL noise shows the trade: smoother, but later.

Finding the fix: replays

Smoothing took 59 ms off the velocity lag; the process noise took another 196 ms, at no cost in noise: the EKF's surge scatters 6.9 mm/s during the holds either way (the DVL, 8.1 mm/s). The change, if the team adopts it:

Status
  • Neither file has the change on followpath-speed yet: vectornav.yaml still says 0.01, and talos_ekf.yaml leaves smoothing off.
  • The MPC is on time regardless. Mapping and anything else reading odometry/filtered's twist get the late values.
