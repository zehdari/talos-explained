The vehicle · Chapter 7

Navigation sensors

Before Talos can steer, it has to know how it is tilted, how fast it is turning and moving, and how deep it is. Four sensors answer those questions, and a hydrophone listens for the pinger. What each one measures, how far to trust it, and how it fails.

The short version
  • Four navigation sensors: a VectorNav VN-100 IMU (tilt, turn rates, acceleration), a fibre-optic gyro (yaw rate), a Nortek DVL1000 (velocity over the pool floor) and a Bar02 pressure sensor (depth).
  • No sensor measures x, y or compass heading. Talos gets them by adding up velocity and yaw rate, so they drift, and the camera map re-anchors them.
  • Rates: IMU 400 Hz, gyro about 475 Hz, depth 20 Hz, DVL 8 Hz. Each DVL reading is about 125 ms old when it arrives.
  • The EKF and the MPC's estimator fuse them (Where am I? State estimation). Nereus simulates all four with measured noise, but not their failures.
  • Most sensor trouble is mundane: a wrong covariance, depth zeroed underwater, a DVL too close to the floor, a stale config file.

Cameras have their own chapter (The cameras). The plots here use real data: two bench recordings of Talos sitting still (2026-10-27) and an untethered pool run (2026-10-26).

The sensors at a glance

Pick a sensor to see where it sits on Talos and what it reports.

Topics are relative to the robot namespace (vectornav/imu is /talos/vectornav/imu). Rates are measured in the bags; talos.yaml still says 100 Hz for the IMU.

Frames

talos.yaml gives each sensor a pose, and the URDF turns those poses into TF frames (talos/imu_link, dvl_link, pressure_link, ...; how TF works). Two sensors are mounted turned: the IMU upside down and turned 90°, the DVL turned 45°. TF rotates their readings into base_link, the robot's own axes. Moving a sensor means changing talos.yaml and Nereus's copy (Adding or moving a sensor).

Rates and timestamps

The sensors run at very different rates and stamp their messages differently. One real second from the pool run, as messages reached the Orin:

  • Stamped on arrival: the IMU and the gyro. Their real age is a few milliseconds of serial transfer.
  • Stamped when measured: the DVL (the driver subtracts the age the DVL reports).
  • Depth: stamped when the board publishes on master (35 to 45 ms late, on a clock that drifts); stamped at the middle of the pressure conversion only on the unmerged fix/depth-timestamps branch (see Depth).
  • Why it matters. The MPC's estimator replays measurements in stamp order (mpc_controller_node.cpp). The EKF has smooth_lagged_data off, one reason its velocity lags the DVL by about 0.35 s (The EKF is late).

The IMU: VectorNav VN-100-T

An IMU measures how Talos turns and accelerates. The VN-100 has MEMS gyros, accelerometers, magnetometers and a barometer; its own Kalman filter blends the drifting gyros with gravity into roll and pitch.

Axes and what gets used

The IMU's pose is roll 180°, yaw −90°, so body roll rate = −y, pitch rate = −x, yaw rate = −z (on the bench, the IMU's z rate and the FOG's correlate at −0.999).

  • EKF (talos_ekf.yaml): roll and pitch, their rates, and the three accelerations. Not yaw, not yaw rate.
  • MPC estimator: IMU roll and pitch rates, FOG yaw rate (the IMU's yaw rate only if the FOG goes quiet for 0.1 s, state_estimator.cpp), and roll and pitch as a slow correction with a 1 s time constant.
  • Nobody uses the magnetometer or the IMU's yaw. The driver still creates vectornav/magnetometer and vectornav/magheading but no longer publishes them (main.cpp); the EKF has IMU yaw off and the MPC takes only tilt from the IMU orientation. Yaw is the integral of the FOG.

A covariance that is 10,000× too big

The driver reports angular_velocity_covariance 0.01 (σ 0.1 rad/s) against a real noise of about 0.001 rad/s (vectornav.yaml), so the EKF trusts its own model over the gyros and its roll and pitch rates come out late and small. The MPC isn't affected; the measurements and the fix are in Where am I?, The EKF is late. IMU failures: see Troubleshooting.

The fibre-optic gyro

A MEMS gyro's bias wanders by degrees per hour, and gravity can't correct yaw. So Talos has a better gyro for yaw alone. A fibre-optic gyro (FOG) sends light both ways around a fibre coil; turning shifts the phase between the beams (the Sagnac effect). No moving parts, so its bias stays put.

Per sample the FOG is noisier than the VN-100, but fast noise averages out when you add it up over time, and a bias doesn't. Here is each gyro's yaw rate added up while Talos sat still for about a minute and a half:

  • Run 1 started right after tilting Talos by hand. For about 50 s the VN-100's yaw rate was off and built up 0.59° of heading error, then it levelled off.
  • Run 2: the VN-100 wandered by at most 0.06° (1.2 °/h on average).
  • The FOG stayed within 0.02° in both runs.
  • So a MEMS yaw error changes with motion and from run to run. Even the datasheet's in-run bias (up to 10 °/h) adds up to 3° over a 20-minute mission, enough to miss a gate. That is why yaw comes from the FOG.

Calibration: from counts to rad/s

The FOG sends a signed 24-bit number. The driver turns it into rad/s with a curve fitted on the team's calibration rig, from the raw reading x and the FOG's temperature T (both scaled by a mean and spread from gyro_config.yaml):

\omega_z = a + b\,x^3 + c\,x\,T + d\cos(f x + g) \;-\; \omega_{\text{tare}}

The curve is nearly a straight line: a plain straight line would be off by about 0.4 °/s at 50 °/s and 3 °/s at 100 °/s. Across the calibrated temperatures (37.6 to 51.3 °C) the temperature term moves a 50 °/s reading by about 0.35 °/s. Outside that range the fit was never tested, and the driver reports temp_within_cal false.

Tare

  • What it does. The gyro/tare action averages num_samples readings (viewer default 100,000, 20 s timeout) and subtracts the average from every later reading. It's saved to a tare file, so it survives restarts.
  • Only with Talos still and level. The FOG feels the Earth turn (about 9.7 °/h about the vertical in Columbus) and tare cancels that too, so a tare taken tilted or moving bakes an error into every yaw. The pre-test tool never tares on its own.

FOG failures (temperature, hardware flags, a bad tare): see Troubleshooting.

The DVL: Nortek DVL1000

A Doppler velocity log pings sound at the floor through four slanted beams. When Talos moves, each echo comes back slightly shifted in frequency, which gives the speed along that beam.

  • Read the page with a plain HTTP GET. Entering command mode (ports 9000, 9001) stops the measurement.
  • Speed of sound sets the scale. Velocity is proportional to it, and in fresh water it depends on temperature: about 1482 m/s at 20 °C, 1500 m/s at 27 °C. The DVL is set to 1480 fixed, so in a warm pool it may read about 1 % low. Compare with the pool temperature (state/depth/temp).
What the driver does with a ping
  • Sorts the sentences: bottom track ($PNORBT9) to dvl_twist, water track ($PNORWT9, velocity relative to the water) to dvl/water_twist, a fallback nothing uses yet.
  • Stamps the past: each message gets the time of its echo, the arrival time minus the age the DVL reports (dvl_driver.cpp). In the pool that age was 125 ms (median; 123 to 175 ms for 90 % of pings).
  • Flips to ROS axes (y and z negated) and decides lock: true when x, y and one of the two z velocities are valid.

A DVL turned 45°

dvl_link has yaw −45°, so driving forward shows up as equal x and y in the DVL's axes. The DVL is also 24 cm below the centre of mass: pitching at 0.5 rad/s alone makes it read 12 cm/s of surge. The MPC estimator removes that lever arm (v_\text{DVL} = v + \omega \times r, state_estimator.cpp).

DVL failures (lost lock, no data, missing topics): see Troubleshooting.

Depth: a pressure sensor in the starboard lid

Water pressure grows by \rho g, about 9.8 kPa per metre, so:

h = \frac{p - p_\text{surface}}{\rho\, g}, \qquad \rho = 997\ \text{kg/m}^3,\; g = 9.80665\ \text{m/s}^2
Model
Blue Robotics Bar02 (2 bar range)
Rate
20 Hz
Topic
depth/pose: base_link's z, negative below the surface
Where
In the starboard lid at the stern of the hull, open to the water
  • Zeroed at power-up. The firmware takes the surface pressure from its first readings (it discards 20, then smooths the next 20 with an exponential average, depth.c) and keeps it across a watchdog reset. Powered up underwater, every depth is off by the starting depth. CLEAR_DEPTH (a viewer button) re-zeroes it at the surface.
  • Fresh-water density is built in. In salt water (1025 kg/m³) the same pressure means 2.7 % less depth.
Full spec, lever arm and timestamps
Chip
TE MS5837-02BA on I²C; the firmware starts it as an MS5837_02BA. The CAD shows a Bar30 in that spot; the firmware drives it as a Bar02.
Board
Read by the camera cage board (CameraCageBB, The circuit boards) on a short I²C cable (J15); its RP2040 publishes over CAN through micro-ROS (Firmware on the boards)
Sampling
Oversampling 8192, about 16 ms per conversion
Raw topics
state/depth/raw (variance 0.003, σ ≈ 5.5 cm: very cautious, the real noise is under a millimetre) and state/depth/temp (water, 1 Hz)
Then
depth_converter.py turns the raw reading into depth/pose
  • Lever arm. The sensor is 13.5 cm above, 16.5 cm behind and 15.5 cm to starboard of base_link; at 10° pitch that changes its height by about 3 cm, so depth_converter.py rotates the offset with the attitude.
  • Timestamps. On master, the board syncs its clock with ROS once at start-up and stamps each reading when it publishes, 35 to 45 ms after sampling; its clock then drifts about 150 ms per hour on Talos. The fix/depth-timestamps branch (not merged yet) re-syncs every 5 s and stamps the middle of the pressure conversion.

Acoustics: which side is the pinger on

At RoboSub a pinger marks either the torpedo or the table task. Talos doesn't find the pinger's direction: it listens at two stops and compares how loud it sounds.

The listening is done by a hydrophone (an underwater microphone) in a small pod under the frame. It hears both the pinger and the other robot's inter-vehicle messages (IVC). A separate transducer, to port, sends Talos's own IVC messages; it plays no part in the pinger task.

Known gaps
  • The order looks inverted. buffer_0_closer true means the torpedo stop was louder, but the tree stores it as is_table, so a louder torpedo pinger sends Talos to the table first (RoboSub).
  • A stale answer. In max mode, if the peaks differ by 1000 or more, nothing is compared and the node keeps publishing the previous result (false after start-up).
  • Nereus has no pinger. In sim the comparison fails ("Cannot compare") and the run goes torpedo first.
  • Stale poses. talos.yaml lists two hydrophones, out on the port and starboard frame modules, from the old two-hydrophone direction-finding design (2024 design report). The pod is not there, and no current code uses them.

Adding or moving a sensor

The pieces sit in two repositories and several chapters. In order:

  1. A driver. A node that reads the device and publishes stamped messages in the sensor's own frame, with an honest covariance (what a wrong one costs). Start it from a launch file included in riptide_hardware/launch/hardware.launch.py, as the DVL, IMU and gyro are; everything there runs under the /talos namespace. A sensor wired to a board publishes from firmware instead (micro-ROS).
  2. Its mount pose. Under the sensor's key in riptide_descriptions/config/talos.yaml: pose: [x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw] in the origin frame, metres and radians. urdf/robot_sensors.xacro turns the dvl, depth, imu, fog and acoustics keys into TF frames; a new kind of sensor needs its own block there and a macro in snippets.xacro (the URDF). The MPC reads the IMU and DVL mounts from the same file, and its log warns if they differ from TF (checkMountsAgainstTf).
  3. Fusing it, if the estimate should use it: one more input next to imu0, twist0, twist1 and pose0 in riptide_hardware/cfg/talos_ekf.yaml, saying which states it measures (What Talos fuses). The MPC's estimator is separate code (state_estimator.cpp).
  4. The launcher. Add its topic to the hardware entry's topics in talos_launch_mpc.yaml, so the entry shows it Not Present when the sensor is missing (Launching the stack).
  5. Its simulated twin. Nereus doesn't read talos.yaml. In content/packs/robots/talos/robot.yaml add a mount under frames and an entry under sensors (guide: docs/guides/robot.md); in content/packs/bridges/uwrt_talos/bridge.yaml map the mount to its ROS frame under frame_names and add a stream that publishes it on the real topic (guide: docs/guides/bridge.md). A moved sensor has to move in both places (Nereus).
  6. Test it. In Nereus first, then on the bench with pretest: its profile, robots/talos/robot.yaml in pretest_checks, holds a minimum rate per topic and the TF frames that must exist; add the new ones (Pool test day).
