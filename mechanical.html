The vehicle · Chapter 1

Hull, frame and layout

Talos is a sealed two-tube hull held in an open aluminium frame, with eight thrusters, two battery pods and its tools bolted on outside. Where everything sits, why it sits there, and how to get inside.

The short version
  • An open aluminium frame carries everything. In the middle sits the main hull: two clear tubes, one for the computer, one for the circuit boards. Batteries, sensors and tools mount outside.
  • 925 mm wide, 639 mm tall, 582 mm long without the claw; 32.4 kg dry on a scale.
  • All eight T200 thrusters sit in one cross-section: four at the corners push fore and aft, four inner ones are tilted 45°.
  • Talos floats very slightly (+1.6 N). Its centre of buoyancy is about 2 cm above its centre of mass, so it rolls back upright; it is also slightly ahead, so with the thrusters off the model says it settles nose-up.
  • Each tube opens at its stern lid and its cage slides out. Every time Talos is sealed: clean the O-ring surfaces, then pressure-test.

The numbers come from the team's SolidWorks CAD (Talos 7-11-26, saved 11 July 2026), the vehicle config talos.yaml and the MPC's model of the real robot. Where the CAD disagrees with the robot or a scale, the robot wins (what the CAD doesn't show).

The numbers

Size
925 × 720 × 711 mm (width × height × length) with the claw and magnet poker; 925 × 639 × 582 mm without (CAD). The RoboSub box is 0.9 × 0.9 × 1.8 m, so the width only fits along its long side.
Mass
32.444 kg dry on a scale, as deployed for the 2026-10-03 pool tests; 34.084 kg weighed wet straight after (talos.yaml L7-13). The MPC model uses 34.307 kg, from the newer "TalosMostlyGood" CAD (2026-10-24), with its volume and COB re-expressed to keep the identified net buoyancy (models/talos.yaml L4-7).
Inertia
1.84, 1.01 and 1.86 kg·m² in roll, pitch and yaw (July CAD). Pitch is the easy axis: the heavy parts are spread sideways.
Thrusters
8 Blue Robotics T200, limited to ±24 N each and 73 N in total (Thrusters and allocation).
Hull
Two polycarbonate tubes and bow windows with machined 6061 aluminium rings and lids, made by Xometry in 2021 (2026 report, parts table).
Frame
6063 aluminium C-channel and 5052-H32 plates in the corner modules, 6061 angle in the cross bars, stainless cables and 316 turnbuckles; from 2023. Fasteners are 18-8 stainless.
Batteries
Two MaxAmps 8000 mAh 5S2P 18.5 V LiPo packs in milled 6061 aluminium pods (Power and wiring).
Printed parts
Cages, mounts, spacers, launcher and claw bodies, the kill switch handle (the CAD tags ABS, PET or PBT; maybe not the real filament). TPU for the claw jaws and some cage mounts (2026, 2024 reports).

Every resolved CAD part adds up to 36.3 kg; without the hidden parts (an old ZED 2i, a bulkhead plate) and two duplicate foam blocks, about 34.9 kg (talos.yaml quotes 34.7). That is still 2.3–2.5 kg over the scale: the foam is weighed as water and the connectors as solid brass. Use the CAD to compare parts, not to weigh the robot.

Why it looks like this

Talos was built for RoboSub 2023. Three choices from the team's report that year still shape it (2023 report):

  • Triangles, not sheets. The previous AUV hung everything on polyethylene sheets. Talos uses aluminium triangles tied with steel cables, checked by finite-element analysis: 2.7 kg lighter, 47 cm narrower. (The report says 6061; the CAD tags the C-channels 6063-O.)
  • Tools under the hull: central, out of the thrusters' wash, and in the cameras' view.
  • Adjustable balance. The battery pods slide fore and aft on indexed rails to move the centre of mass; foam blocks move the centre of buoyancy.
YearChange since
2024Turnbuckles, so the frame comes apart. L-channel bars instead of square tube (screws fell inside). Bottles on top for buoyancy. ¼-20 battery rods instead of bending 8-32. Colour-coded SubConns, rubber bumpers (2024 report).
2025Rails press-fit inside the hull; the cages slide in with no screws. A ZED X Mini down camera against the bottom of the hull (2025 report).
2026A new claw with ribbed TPU jaws on a hinged mount, and a neutrally buoyant torpedo (2026 report).

The frame

  • Four corner modules: two upper ("Dorito" in the CAD) and two lower ("Taco"). Each C-channel triangle carries two T200s with guards, a foam block and rubber handles; the upper two also have crane hooks.
  • Two 432 mm cross bars ("Holy L Bars") under the hull carry the tools; four rails carry the battery pods.
  • Eight stainless cables brace the modules. Cables only pull, so turnbuckles tension them; slacken those to take the frame apart.

Eight thrusters in one plane

All eight T200s sit 1–2 cm ahead of the centre of mass, in one cross-section. Their names in talos.yaml say where they are: V ("vector", the outer corners, fore and aft) or H ("heave", inner, tilted 45° to push up or down and sideways); then Upper or Lower; then Port or Starboard.

  • Surge, yaw and pitch use only the corners: all four for surge (the lower pair a little harder, as the centre of mass sits nearer them), port against starboard for yaw, upper against lower for pitch.
  • Heave, sway and roll use almost only the tilted four: two 45° pushes cancel sideways and add up and down, or the other way round. In heave and sway the corners add 2–3 % to cancel a small pitch or yaw, because the tilted four sit 1 cm ahead of the centre of mass.

More in Thrusters and allocation (the 6×8 matrix) and Thrusters and ESCs (motors and wiring).

The main hull

The dry space for the computer, the boards and both cameras: two tubes side by side, joined by aluminium blocks at the bow and stern.

Tubes
Clear polycarbonate cylinders, 146 mm long, each clamped between two aluminium rings by four stainless tension rods.
Bow
A flat 3 mm polycarbonate window about 202 mm across on each tube. The forward camera looks out of the starboard one.
Stern
A removable lid on each tube, held by draw latches (four McMaster 6200A31 in the CAD). Every cable into the hull goes through bulkhead connectors on the lids.
Seals
Static O-rings: four X-profile lid O-rings (nitrile), four square-profile O-rings where the tubes meet the rings, and two lid gaskets.

Because the tubes are clear, the down camera can sit inside the starboard tube and look out through its bottom; mounting it directly against the wall keeps the curved shell's distortion small (2025 report).

Keeping water out

  • Connectors: MacArtney SubConn bulkheads (MC, HP and D series; 2026 report), colour-coded so plugs go back where they came from.
  • The pressure test: pump the hull down through the port lid's vacuum valve to 0.05 bar below ambient, then watch pressure and temperature for five minutes; a leak lets air back in. Below ambient is safer than pressurising (2025 report). When it passes, the green SubConn cover goes on (the depressurization test).
  • No leak sensor any more: a rising humidity reading on a board is the early warning (housekeeping sensors).

Inside: two cages

  • Starboard, the computer cage: the Jetson AGX Orin, its GMSL2 camera card, the forward ZED X Mini, the VN-100 IMU, a 40 mm fan, and shelves for the network switch, fibre-optic gyro and breakout board (Computers and networks).
  • Port, the board stack: circuit boards behind a 90 mm fan, whose plate holds the Hall-effect kill switch sensor right behind the red handle (The circuit boards).
  • Rails: since 2025 the cages slide on rails press-fit into the hull (the old screwed-in ones stripped). The down camera meets its cage through a slide-in adapter, so there's no cable to plug in by hand (2025 report).

Battery pods

  • Two milled aluminium pods, one per side on the frame's rails, each about 82 × 150 × 380 mm. The lid seals with an O-ring, each pod has a pressure-relief valve, and a window shows the pack's display (2023 report).
  • Swapped without tools. Compliant clips snap the pods onto indexed rails, and each has its own SubConns, so changing one never opens the main hull and its pressure test still holds. What's inside: Power and wiring.

Sensors and tools outside the hull

Anything that has to touch the water, or can be waterproofed on its own, mounts outside.

Buoyancy and trim

Weight pulls down at the centre of mass (COM); buoyancy, the weight of the water displaced, pushes up at the centre of buoyancy (COB). From the MPC's model (models/talos.yaml; volume and COB identified in the pool, mass from the newer CAD):

Weight
34.307 kg × g = 336.4 N, at the COM
Buoyancy
34.5 L × 998.2 kg/m³ × g = 338.1 N, at the COB
Net
+1.63 N up. RoboSub wants at least 0.5 % of the mass (1.68 N) so a killed robot surfaces; the model is just under, the untethered fit of 2026-10-26 right on it (at the pool).
COB
22.5 mm above the COM, 8.2 mm ahead of it and 2.3 mm to starboard.
  • Barely positive, because the thrusters hold every extra newton of float down for the whole run.
  • COB above COM, so a tilt makes a couple that turns Talos back; the higher the COB, the stiffer.
  • The float is mostly the air in the main hull, plus Blue Robotics R-3312 foam (one block per corner module, one between the tubes) and the two bottles added in 2024 when new mechanisms made Talos heavier.

Because the COB is also 8 mm ahead of the COM, the model says Talos would hang nose-up with the thrusters off. With them on, the MPC holds it level and pays for that offset all the time. Try adding a part:

The MPC doesn't know you added anything: it learns the unexplained force and torque as a disturbance, so Talos still flies, but the identification is off. The model file's rule: "Change the body and re-identify." Weigh the robot, update the mass in talos.yaml and the model file, and run pool identification again, untethered.

Opening Talos up

  • Into the hull: release a stern lid's latches; that tube's cage slides out on its rails.
  • Batteries: clip the pods off their rails; the main hull stays sealed.
  • The frame: slacken the turnbuckles to free the cables before unbolting modules.
  • Carrying it: handles on every corner module, crane hooks on the upper two. The claw arm's hinged mount locks with retaining pins; rotate it up for transport so Talos can rest on a flat surface (2026 report).
  • The tether is Ethernet to the switch in the computer cage (the robot LAN and the tether).
Every time the hull is opened
  • Inspect and clean the O-ring sealing surfaces inside both lids before closing (the first sign-off in pretest bench).
  • Redo the pressure test, and put the green SubConn cover back on only when it passes.
  • Before a pool test, once the checks are done, nobody opens Talos or swaps a part (pool test).

From CAD to code

The software never reads the CAD. The geometry reaches it through a few hand-kept files:

All positions in talos.yaml are measured from one point, the origin frame: "the farthest-port screw on the front of the camera cage". In metres (x forward, y to port, z up):

Pointx, y, zWhat it is

  • Into TF: navigation.launch.py runs xacro on talos.xacro, which reads talos.yaml; robot_state_publisher publishes the talos/… frames (how TF works).
  • Into the MPC: poses from talos.yaml, the body from its model file (mpc_controller.launch.py). At start-up it warns if its IMU and DVL mounts differ from TF (mpc_controller_node.cpp).
  • Into Nereus: its own copy of the frames, body and meshes in robots/talos/robot.yaml, kept in step with the MPC model by hand. Right now it still has the 2026-10-03 body (34.084 kg) and the old torpedo and dropper poses.
  • Moved or added something? Update talos.yaml and Nereus's copy, weigh the robot, re-identify.

What the CAD doesn't show

  • Anything after July 2026. talos.yaml notes the robot's down camera is rolled 180° from the CAD, and gives the torpedo pose "as deployed 2026-10-03". The launcher here is the V8. The newer "TalosMostlyGood" assembly (2026-10-24, new external buoyancy; talos_untuned.yaml) isn't shown here.
  • A true mass (above), the tether, or any cables but the thruster, battery and DVL ones.
  • The config's origin. Shifting the CAD's camera positions by about (−0.14, +0.035, 0) m lands them within 6 mm of the ffc and dfc poses, which talos.yaml took from this CAD; the thrusters differ by 3–5 mm sideways. Treat CAD positions as ±5 mm.
