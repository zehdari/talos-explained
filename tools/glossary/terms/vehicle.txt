# Glossary source: mechanical, electrical, boards, firmware, thrusters, actuators, sensors, cameras,
# state estimation and computers. Format: see start.txt.

## Frame (mechanical)
aka: chassis
part: mechanical
slug: chassis
The open aluminium structure that carries everything on Talos: the main hull, thrusters, battery pods, sensors and tools all mount to it.
> mechanical.html#frame

## Main hull
aka: hull
part: mechanical
The sealed centre of Talos: two clear tubes, the starboard one holding the [[camera cage]] (Orin, IMU, gyro, both cameras), the port one the [[board stack]].
> mechanical.html#hull
> mechanical.html#inside

## Battery pods
aka: battery housing
part: mechanical
Two sealed aluminium pods on the frame rails, one per side, each holding a [[smart battery housing]]. They swap without tools and without opening the main hull.
> mechanical.html#batteries
> batteries.html

## SubConn
aka: wet-mate connector, bulkhead connector
part: mechanical
MacArtney's wet-mate connectors: every cable that leaves a housing goes through one. They are colour-coded so each plug goes back where it came from.
> electrical.html#connectors
> mechanical.html#sealing
> sealing.html

## COM
exp: Centre of mass
aka: centre of gravity, center of mass
part: mechanical
Where Talos's weight acts. Measured with the team's three-load-cell COM scale; with the [[COB]] it decides how Talos floats and tilts.
> mechanical.html#trim
> control/model.html#restoring
> buoyancy.html

## COB
exp: Centre of buoyancy
aka: center of buoyancy
part: mechanical
Where buoyancy (the weight of the displaced water) pushes up. Talos's is identified in the pool, because CAD doesn't know every air pocket.
> mechanical.html#trim
> control/identify.html#statics
> buoyancy.html

## Buoyancy
aka: positively buoyant, net buoyancy
part: mechanical
The upward push of the water. RoboSub requires a killed vehicle to float up by at least 0.5 % of its weight (1.68 N for Talos). The MPC model gives 1.63 N, just under; the untethered pool identification of 26 October 2026 fitted 1.68 N, right on it. That is why [[trim]] is checked at every pool test.
> mechanical.html#trim
> pool-test.html#trim
> robosub.html#rules
> buoyancy.html

## Trim
part: mechanical
Adjusting weights and floats so Talos sits level and slightly positive in the water. It is checked in the water at each pool test.
> mechanical.html#trim
> pool-test.html#trim
> buoyancy.html

## Depressurization test
aka: PVT, pressure test, vacuum test
part: mechanical
The leak test before a pool test: pump the hull 0.05 bar below ambient through the vacuum valve and watch the pressure for five minutes. Passed means the green SubConn cover goes on.
> pool-test.html#pvt
> mechanical.html#sealing
> sealing.html

## Smart battery housing
aka: battery, batteries, SBH, smart battery, LiPo
part: electrical
Each of Talos's two batteries: a MaxAmps 8000 mAh 5S2P LiPo (five pairs of cells in series, 18.5 V nominal, about 148 Wh) sealed with its own [[BMS]] and microcontroller board, which report over CAN.
> electrical.html#smart
> boards.html#batteries
> batteries.html

## BMS
exp: Battery management system
aka: BQ40Z80
part: electrical
The protection board in each battery housing: a TI BQ40Z80 watches and balances the five cell groups (the pack is 5S2P) and switches the pack off when something is wrong.
> boards.html#batteries
> electrical.html#smart
> batteries.html

## Ideal-diode OR
aka: battery balancer
part: electrical
The circuit on the POAC that joins the two packs: whichever has the higher voltage carries the load, and current never flows from one pack into the other.
> electrical.html#two-packs

## Rail
aka: power rail, V+, MOTOR_PWR
part: electrical
A supply voltage distributed around the robot. `V+` is the battery bus; `MOTOR_PWR` feeds only the ESC boards and is the one rail the kill switch cuts.
> electrical.html#rails
> electrical.html#kill

## Kill switch
aka: kill, physical kill, kill handle, red handle, Hall-effect switch
part: electrical
The red handle on the port tube's bow window. A swimmer seats it to let Talos move and pulls it off to stop it; a Hall-effect sensor (a magnet detector) right behind the window, on the board stack's fan plate, feels its magnet. It cuts only the ESC supply, so the computer and sensors stay on. The cover at the stern is the [[SubConn cover]], not the kill switch.
> electrical.html#kill
> boards.html#kill
> commanding.html#kill
> mechanical.html#inside

## Kill slot
aka: KillSwitchReport, software kill, slot 0, slot 1, slot 3
part: control
One of four inputs to the kill decision on the POAC: slot 0 the physical switch, 1 the Nereus viewer, 2 a topside button (unused), 3 the command line and scripts. Talos is killed if any enabled slot asserts.
> commanding.html#kill
> commanding.html#viewer-slot

## needs_update
aka: switch_needs_update, heartbeat
part: ops
A flag on a kill-slot report. True makes it a heartbeat that must repeat within 500 ms or the robot is killed; false latches until the next report. The viewer sends false so untethered runs continue.
> commanding.html#kill
> viewer.html#enable

## state/kill
aka: SAFETY_KILLED
part: control
The POAC's verdict, published every 150 ms and on any change: true means killed. The ESC boards treat it as a heartbeat and report `SAFETY_KILLED` while killed.
> commanding.html#stops
> firmware.html#kill

## Board stack
part: electrical
The 3D-printed cage of circuit boards that slides out of the main hull's port tube. The camera cage, with the Orin, is in the starboard tube; one CAN cable chains the boards in both to the Orin.
> boards.html#glance
> boards.html#system

## POAC
exp: Power + actuator board
part: electrical
The board that takes both batteries in, makes the power rails, decides the kill and drives the torpedo/marker servo. CAN client 1.
> boards.html#poac
> electrical.html#rails
> actuators.html#poac

## ESC
exp: Electronic speed controller
aka: APD 80F3
part: electrical
Turns battery DC into the three-phase current a brushless motor needs. Talos has eight APD 80F3 ESCs, four on each of two [[ESC board|ESC boards]].
> thrusters-hw.html#esc
> boards.html#esc

## ESC board
part: electrical
One of two identical boards that each run four ESCs (thrusters 0 to 3 and 4 to 7). Its RP2040 runs an RPM loop per thruster on the targets the MPC sends over CAN.
> boards.html#esc
> thrusters-hw.html#rpm-loop
> firmware.html#thrusters

## Camera cage
aka: computer cage
part: mechanical
The slide-out printed cage in the starboard hull tube holding the Orin, its GMSL2 card, the forward ZED X Mini, the IMU, the FOG, the network switch and the camera cage breakout board ([[CCBB]]). The down camera ([[dfc]]) sits in the same tube against its bottom wall and meets the cage through a slide-in adapter.
> mechanical.html#inside
> computers.html#orin
> boards.html#ccbb

## CCBB
exp: Camera cage breakout board
part: electrical
The board in the [[camera cage]] that powers the Orin and DVL, reads the depth sensor and joins the Orin to CAN.
> boards.html#ccbb

## LED board
aka: status light
part: electrical
Drives Talos's RGBW status light: red flashes when the kill switch is pulled, blue when it goes in, and colours set by trees through `SetStatus`.
> boards.html#led-ivc
> mission-tree.html#leds

## IVC board
exp: Inter-vehicle communication board
aka: IVC, acoustic modem
part: electrical
An acoustic modem: it sends 17 and 19 kHz tones through the [[transducer]] and listens through the [[hydrophone]], for Triton and for the competition pinger.
> boards.html#led-ivc
> sensors.html#acoustics

## Orin HAT
part: electrical
An add-on board on the Orin with its CAN transceivers (`can0`, `can1`) and a serial port for the IMU.
> boards.html#orin-side

## Mark 1, Mark 2
aka: Mark 1, Mark 2, mk2
part: electrical
slug: mark-2
The team's electronics generations. Mark 1 (Tempest) shared I²C buses that failed on long runs; Mark 2 (Talos) put an RP2040 on every board and joined them with CAN.
> boards.html#rp2040

## RP2040
aka: Raspberry Pi RP2040, Pico
part: electrical
The Raspberry Pi microcontroller on every Talos board that runs firmware. Each board has its own, so a fault stays on one board.
> boards.html#rp2040
> firmware.html#boards

## Pico SDK
part: electrical
Raspberry Pi's C SDK for the RP2040, which titan_firmware is built on.
> firmware.html#repo
> firmware.html#build

## SWD
exp: Serial Wire Debug
part: electrical
The RP2040's debug and flashing port, on Tag-Connect pads on each board. Used for the first flash; after that updates go over CAN.
> boards.html#rp2040
> firmware.html#debug

## Bootloader
aka: titan_bootloader, upload_tool
part: electrical
The small program on each board that can take a new firmware image over CAN, so boards are updated without opening the hull.
> firmware.html#flash

## CAN
exp: Controller Area Network
aka: CAN bus, can0, can1
part: electrical
A two-wire bus where every node hears every frame. Talos's boards use it to talk to each other and to the Orin, which sees the internal bus as `can0` and the external (battery) bus as `can1`.
> firmware.html#can
> computers.html#can
> electrical.html#why-can

## CAN FD
aka: CAN flexible data rate
part: electrical
The newer CAN that can send a frame's data faster than its header. Talos's firmware configures the internal bus for it (1 / 5 Mbit/s), but every frame sent today is classic; the battery bus is classic CAN at 250 kbit/s.
> firmware.html#can
> boards.html#rp2040
> computers.html#can

## Termination
aka: terminator, TERM
part: electrical
A 120 Ω resistor at each end of a CAN bus, and nowhere else, so signals don't bounce off the cable ends. Talos's boards have a switch for it.
> boards.html#rp2040

## CANmore
aka: titan_canmore
part: electrical
The team's own protocol on top of CAN: the frame ID carries the board's client ID, the direction and a channel, so every data byte is payload. It also carries longer messages split over several frames.
> firmware.html#canmore
> firmware.html#can

## Client ID
aka: CAN ID
part: electrical
Each board's address on CAN (POAC 1, ESC boards 2 and 3, CCBB 4, …), from `client_ids.h`.
> boards.html#glance
> computers.html#can

## micro-ROS
aka: XRCE-DDS, Micro-XRCE-DDS
part: electrical
ROS 2 for microcontrollers. Each board runs a small XRCE-DDS client that asks the agent on the Orin ([[copro_agent]]) to create its publishers and subscribers, so its topics appear in ROS like any other node's.
> firmware.html#microros
> boards.html#system

## copro_agent
aka: micro-ROS agent, micro_ros_agent
part: electrical
The micro-ROS agent on the Orin that bridges every board's micro-ROS client on CAN into ROS 2 (`copro_agent.launch.py`).
> boards.html#system
> computers.html#can

## Watchdog
aka: titan_safety, safety_tick
part: electrical
A timer that resets a board whose main loop stops checking in. The firmware's safety code feeds it, and a reset board comes back with a fault bit saying why.
> firmware.html#safety

## DShot
aka: DShot600, bidirectional DShot, 3D mode
part: electrical
The digital protocol from an ESC board to each ESC: 16-bit frames with throttle and a checksum, at 600 kbit/s. In bidirectional DShot the ESC answers with its speed, which the RPM loop runs on.
> thrusters-hw.html#dshot
> firmware.html#thrusters

## T200
aka: thruster, Blue Robotics T200
part: electrical
Blue Robotics' shrouded brushless thruster. Talos has eight; their published force curves are what the MPC uses for thrust.
> thrusters-hw.html#t200
> control/thrusters.html#rpm

## Thruster names
aka: VUS, VUP, HUS, HUP, HLS, HLP, VLS, VLP
part: electrical
`talos.yaml`'s names for the eight thrusters: Heave or Vector, Upper or Lower, Port or Starboard (HLP is heave, lower, port). The index 0 to 7 is the order `VUS VUP HUS HUP HLS HLP VLS VLP`.
> thrusters-hw.html#layout
> nereus.html#bridge

## RPM loop
part: electrical
The control loop on each ESC board that holds each thruster at the RPM the MPC asked for, from the speed the ESC reports over DShot.
> thrusters-hw.html#rpm-loop
> firmware.html#thrusters

## Thruster test
part: ops
The bench step that spins each thruster in turn, 0 to 7, so you can check each one turns the right way. The only bench step that spins thrusters.
> thrusters-hw.html#test
> pool-test.html#thruster-test

## Force gauge
aka: thruster_calibration_tools
part: electrical
The team's rig for measuring a thruster's force against its command in a tank.
> thrusters-hw.html#gauge

## Launcher
aka: torpedo launcher, dropper, marker
part: mechanical
One servo, driven by the [[POAC]], fires two forward torpedoes and drops two markers. From its 120° home it turns one way for torpedoes and the other for markers.
> actuators.html#launcher
> actuators.html#shot

## Claw
part: mechanical
The gripper under Talos's bow, added for 2026 to pick up and sort the table objects.
> actuators.html#claw

## Magnet poker
part: mechanical
A passive magnet on a rod. Talos flies it close to a magnet light to switch it on; nothing on board drives it.
> actuators.html#claw
> mission-tree.html#magnets

## Arm
aka: armed, disarm, SetActuatorsArmed
part: mechanical
Making the actuators ready to fire. The POAC refuses to arm while the robot is killed, and refuses a torpedo unless armed; trees arm just before a task and disarm after.
> actuators.html#poac
> actuators.html#trees

## IMU
exp: Inertial measurement unit
aka: VectorNav, VN-100
part: sensing
A sensor for turn rates, acceleration and tilt. Talos's is a VectorNav VN-100-T, whose own filter gives roll and pitch from gravity.
> sensors.html#imu

## FOG
exp: Fibre-optic gyro
aka: fiber-optic gyro, gyro
part: sensing
A gyro that measures turning from light sent both ways round a fibre coil (the Sagnac effect). Its bias barely drifts, so Talos uses it for yaw rate.
> sensors.html#fog

## Tare
aka: TareGyro, gyro tare
part: sensing
Measuring the gyro's bias while Talos is perfectly still and level, then subtracting it. A tare on a tilted or moving robot makes the heading drift.
> sensors.html#fog-tare
> localization.html#trouble

## DVL
exp: Doppler velocity log
aka: Nortek DVL1000, DVL1000
part: sensing
A sonar that pings the floor through four slanted beams and measures Talos's velocity from the echoes' Doppler shift. Talos's is a Nortek DVL1000.
> sensors.html#dvl

## Bottom lock
aka: dvl/bottom_lock
part: sensing
The DVL is tracking the floor and its velocity is valid. Without it only the IMU's acceleration drives the velocity estimate, which drifts.
> localization.html#trouble
> sensors.html#dvl-driver

## Depth sensor
aka: depth, Bar02, MS5837, pressure sensor
part: sensing
A Blue Robotics Bar02 (MS5837) pressure sensor in the starboard hull lid at the stern, wired to the [[CCBB]], which publishes it at 20 Hz as `state/depth/raw`. It is zeroed at power-up, so power Talos on at the surface.
> sensors.html#depth
> firmware.html#depth

## Hydrophone
part: sensing
An underwater microphone (Aquarian Audio H1C), in a small pod under the frame. It only listens: it feeds the [[IVC board]], which uses it to hear the pinger and to receive messages from Triton. Talos sends through the [[transducer]].
> sensors.html#acoustics
> boards.html#led-ivc

## ZED X Mini
aka: ZED, Stereolabs
part: sensing
Stereolabs' stereo camera. Talos has two, [[ffc]] and [[dfc]], each giving colour and a depth image at 1920 × 1200 and 15 Hz.
> cameras.html#two-cameras
> cameras.html#settings

## ffc
exp: Forward-facing camera
part: sensing
The forward ZED X Mini, and its namespace: `/talos/ffc/zed_node/…`.
> cameras.html#two-cameras
> cameras.html#topics

## dfc
exp: Downward-facing camera
part: sensing
The downward ZED X Mini, used over the bins and the table: `/talos/dfc/zed_node/…`. It is inside the starboard hull tube, looking down through the tube's bottom wall.
> cameras.html#two-cameras
> cameras.html#topics

## GMSL2
part: sensing
A serial video link over one coax cable, from each ZED X Mini to a capture card on the Orin.
> cameras.html#two-cameras

## ZED SDK
aka: zed-ros2-wrapper, zed_node, zed.launch.py
part: sensing
Stereolabs' library that grabs, rectifies and computes depth on the GPU. The ROS wrapper runs one `zed_node` per camera, both from `zed.launch.py`.
> cameras.html#pipeline
> cameras.html#launch

## set_camera_is_dfc
part: perception
The detector's service that switches it between the forward and downward camera. It answers at once but swaps a moment later, so check the detections' frame.
> cameras.html#switching
> perception/detector.html#switching

## SVO2
aka: .svo2, SVO
part: ops
The ZED's own video file format with both eyes. The viewer's Recording panel writes one per camera on the robot; they replay through the ZED wrapper and become training data.
> cameras.html#try
> debugging.html#bags

## Odometry
aka: odometry/filtered, GetOdometry
part: sensing
The estimate of Talos's pose and velocity. The EKF publishes it as `odometry/filtered` (pose in `odom`, velocity in the body), 30 times a second. Tree nodes read it with `GetOdometry`.
> localization.html#ekf
> localization.html#users

## EKF
exp: Extended Kalman filter
aka: robot_localization, ekf_node
part: sensing
The filter that fuses the IMU, gyro, DVL and depth sensor into Talos's odometry: predict with the model, correct with each measurement. Talos uses robot_localization's. Mapping, the tree and the viewer use it; the MPC flies on its own [[MPC state estimator|estimator]] and uses the EKF only as an anchor.
> localization.html#ekf
> localization.html#fused

## Kalman filter
part: perception
An estimator that keeps a best guess and a covariance, predicts how both change, and blends each new measurement in by how much it trusts it. The EKF and mapping's per-prop trackers are both Kalman filters.
> localization.html#ekf
> perception/tracking.html#update

## Process noise
aka: Q
part: sensing
How much uncertainty the EKF adds per second while predicting: how much it expects reality to stray from its simple motion model.
> localization.html#ekf

## Covariance
aka: variance, uncertainty
part: perception
A matrix of how uncertain an estimate is, drawn as an ellipsoid. Trees wait for a prop's published covariance to shrink below a threshold before acting.
> perception/noise.html#model
> perception/tracking.html#confirm

## base_link
aka: talos/base_link
part: sensing
Talos's body frame: x forward, y to port, z up. The pose of Talos is the transform from `odom` to `talos/base_link`.
> localization.html#frames
> control/model.html#frames

## odom
part: sensing
The frame where odometry starts: wherever Talos was, facing that way, when the EKF started. It drifts as errors add up.
> localization.html#drift
> perception/frames.html#tree

## map
aka: map frame, world
part: sensing
The course's frame, set before a run by the AprilTag calibration. `world → map` is that calibration; props hang under `map → offset`.
> localization.html#drift
> perception/frames.html#tree

## AprilTag calibration
aka: AprilTag, tag calibration, Calibrate, apriltag_ros
part: sensing
Before a run Talos faces the tag board and the operator presses Calibrate; the tag's pose sets where the course map sits. Redo it if the EKF restarts.

## chameleon_tf
part: sensing
A node that publishes a transform which can be re-set at run time. Mapping uses one (`world_to_map`) for the AprilTag calibration.

part: sensing

## CAD

part: mechanical
The 3D model of the vehicle, in SolidWorks (this site uses the assembly `Talos 7-11-26`, saved 11 July 2026). The software never reads it: sensor and thruster poses are copied by hand into `talos.yaml`. Where the CAD and the real robot disagree, the robot wins.

## MCU
exp: Microcontroller unit
aka: microcontroller
part: electrical
A small computer on one chip that runs a board's firmware. Every Talos board with one uses the same [[RP2040]] block, so they all share one pin map and one CAN driver.
> boards.html#rp2040
> firmware.html#boards

## PCB
exp: Printed circuit board
aka: Altium, electric_boogaloo, JLCPCB
part: electrical
A circuit board: layers of copper traces that join the chips. Talos's are designed in Altium (repo `electric_boogaloo`), made by JLCPCB and assembled by the team.
> boards.html#design
> boards.html#explorer
> board-design.html

## PWM
exp: Pulse-width modulation
part: electrical
Sending a value as the width of a repeating pulse; Blue Robotics rates the T200 against 1100 to 1900 µs. Talos drives its ESCs with [[DShot]] instead, which is digital, needs no calibrating and reports the RPM back.
> thrusters-hw.html#dshot
> thrusters-hw.html#curves

## UART
exp: Universal asynchronous receiver-transmitter
aka: serial, RS-232, RS-422, ttyTHS1, ttyTHS2
part: electrical
A plain serial link: bytes one after another at an agreed baud rate. The IMU speaks RS-232 on `/dev/ttyTHS1`; the fibre-optic gyro RS-422 (a pair of wires with opposite signals, which shrugs off noise) at 921,600 baud on `/dev/ttyTHS2`. Every board also has a 115200-baud debug UART.
> boards.html#orin-side
> sensors.html#fog
> firmware.html#debug

## Port, starboard, bow, stern
aka: port side, starboard side, bow, stern, fore, aft, left side, right side
part: mechanical
slug: port-starboard
Boat words for directions on Talos, fixed to the robot rather than to you. The bow is the front, where the forward camera looks; the stern is the back; port is the left and starboard the right when you face the bow. In [[base_link]], x points to the bow and y to port. A behaviour-tree [[Port]] is something else.
> mechanical.html#hull
> localization.html#frames

## Transducer
aka: IVC transducer, underwater speaker
part: sensing
The underwater speaker the [[IVC board]] sends its messages through, outside the hull to port. It is a separate part from the [[hydrophone]], which only listens.
> boards.html#led-ivc
> sensors.html#acoustics

## O-ring
aka: seal, gasket, X-ring
part: mechanical
A rubber ring squeezed in a groove to seal a joint. The main hull's lids seal on X-profile nitrile O-rings, and the tubes meet their end rings on square-profile ones. Inspect and clean the lid sealing surfaces every time you close the hull.

## Foam
aka: foam block, buoyancy foam
part: mechanical
Blocks of foam in the frame's corner modules that add buoyancy. Moving them shifts the [[COB]], the way sliding the battery pods shifts the [[COM]].

## SubConn cover
aka: green cover, connector cover
part: mechanical
The green cover over the port lid's [[SubConn]] plugs, at the stern of the main hull. It goes on only after the hull passes the [[depressurization test]], so a cover on means sealed and tested. It is not the [[kill switch]], which is the red handle at the bow.

The CAD's names for the frame's four corner modules: the two upper ones are Doritos, the two lower ones Tacos. Each is an aluminium C-channel triangle carrying two [[T200|T200s]] with guards, a [[foam]] block and handles; the Doritos also have crane hooks.

The team's name for the Molex Mini-Fit Jr plug that carries power and CAN from board to board in one locking connector.

## AUX switch

## GPIO

part: electrical
A microcontroller pin the firmware can read or drive as a plain on/off signal. The ESC boards read GPIO 29 at boot to learn which four thrusters are theirs.

## ADC
exp: Analog-to-digital converter
aka: MCP3428
part: electrical
A chip (or part of one) that turns a voltage into a number. The POAC's 16-bit MCP3428 reads four power rails; the IVC board's ADC samples the [[hydrophone]].

## PIO
exp: Programmable I/O
part: electrical
Small state machines inside the [[RP2040]] that run a pin protocol on their own, without the main core. The ESC boards use them to speak [[DShot]].
> firmware.html#thrusters

## CRC
exp: Cyclic redundancy check
aka: checksum, CRC-18, CRC-32
part: electrical
A checksum that catches corrupted data. [[CANmore]] ends each long message with a CRC-18, and the [[bootloader]] starts a board's program only if its CRC-32 matches, so a half-written image never runs.
> firmware.html#canmore
> firmware.html#flash

## FOV
exp: Field of view
aka: field of view
part: sensing
How wide a camera sees, as two angles. After rectification the detector sees about 54.5° × 35.7° through the forward camera and 54.9° × 36.0° through the downward one.
