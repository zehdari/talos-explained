RoboSub and the course

Talos is built to score points at one competition. This chapter explains that competition, walks the 2026 course task by task, and shows which parts of Talos each task uses.

The short version
  • RoboSub is RoboNation's international student competition for autonomous underwater vehicles. In July 2026 UWRT placed 4th in its Autonomy Challenge with Talos.
  • During a run nobody may talk to the vehicle. It must pass the gate first; after that, any task in any order. Surfacing outside the octagon ends the run.
  • The 2026 course: a gate where you pick a role, a slalom, a torpedo board, an octagon with a table of objects to sort, bins with magnet lights, and back through the gate.
  • Talos runs all of it from one behaviour tree, RepairCompMpcTree, flying between stored positions of the props and letting its cameras correct them.

What RoboSub is

RoboSub is run by RoboNation with the US Office of Naval Research. Student teams build an AUV (a robot submarine that runs with nobody steering it) and bring it to one pool for a week in July. The pool is 2.1 m (7 ft) deep. Facts here come from the RoboSub 2026 Team Handbook unless a link says otherwise.

Two competitions run side by side and are ranked separately:

  • The Autonomy Challenge: runs in the pool, scored by judges watching the vehicle. Most of this site is about this one.
  • Design Documentation: what the team writes, films and presents about how and why it built the vehicle (below).

The 2026 course

The 2026 theme is Storm Response. At the gate the vehicle takes one of two roles, and the role decides which half of every later task is "correct". The tree this page follows, RepairCompMpcTree, takes Survey & Repair.

The handbook numbers the tasks 1 to 6 (gate, slalom, bins, torpedoes, octagon, return home). Talos does them in a different order: gate, slalom, torpedo, octagon, bins, home. A random pinger step between the slalom and the torpedo chooses whether the torpedo or the octagon comes first.

The handbook also describes two orange path markers on the pool floor and an acoustic pinger at the torpedo board and at the octagon. Talos's detector has no path-marker class: the tree flies straight to stored positions instead. The pingers are used once, to choose the order of the torpedo and table tasks.

How a run works

  • Gate first. Every other task needs the gate passed first. After that, tasks can be done in any order, and a score only goes up: going back to a task keeps the better result.
  • Start. The vehicle starts on the surface and dives before it leaves the start zone. For extra points the team can ask for a coin flip that decides which way it starts facing.
  • The last run counts. In the semi-finals each team gets a 20-minute slot a day. Starting a new run throws away the previous run's points.
  • Breaching. Surfacing inside the octagon is a task. Breaking the surface anywhere else ends the run.
  • Time bonus. A vehicle that passes one slalom row, drops one marker in a bin (or fires one torpedo through the board) and surfaces fully in the octagon earns 100 points per minute left.
Practice, semi-finals, finals and courses
  • Practice and qualifying. Teams book 30-minute practice slots. A team qualifies by passing through the gate on its own, or ahead of time with a prequalification video.
  • Semi-finals. Two days, one 20-minute slot each day, on two different courses. The first 5 minutes are setup with the vehicle out of the water.
  • Finals. The top teams, with all scores reset. The best run counts.
  • Several courses. The pool holds practice courses A, B1, B2, C1, C2 and D. Semi-finals run on A and D; the finals combine A, B1 and B2. Task positions differ between courses, and no three elements are ever in a line.
  • Attempts. Each task has an imaginary sphere of 3 m radius around it. Entering it starts an attempt; leaving ends it.

One tree for the whole run

On competition day the operator starts one behaviour tree, RepairCompMpcTree.xml, about 4 100 lines of XML. Talos does not detect its role: this file is the Survey & Repair mission, and RescueCompMpcTree.xml is the same mission for the other role. In order, it:

  1. waits for the kill switch to be put in, then dives in place;
  2. passes the gate and the slalom;
  3. listens for the pinger, which picks torpedo or table first;
  4. does the torpedo and the table;
  5. drops markers in the bins and turns off the magnet lights;
  6. goes home through the gate and stops.
The pinger order looks backwards

The tree listens at the torpedo stop, then at the table stop. As written, a louder torpedo pinger sends Talos to the table first, which looks inverted against the rule. It may be a bug, and it is not confirmed either way, so don't count on the pinger bonus. Details in The pinger picks the order.

Two ideas run through the whole tree:

  • A failed task doesn't end the run. Each task is wrapped so that a timeout or failed check counts as done, and the tree moves on. Points only go up, so giving up costs nothing but time.
  • The cameras correct, the map decides. Talos flies to where its prior map says a prop is, looks with the camera that task needs, and lets mapping move the prop before it commits to a shot, drop or grab (The course map).

How the tree is built, node by node, is in Behaviour trees and The competition mission.

How it scores

Judges score the run live. Nereus implements the same 2026 rules (robosub_2026.cpp, points in tasks.yaml), so a simulated run shows a scorecard in the viewer. A few rows are left to the judges: pinger awards, keeping control through the gate, weight, and inter-vehicle communication.

Three things stand out. The octagon and table is the largest single task, up to 7 700 points, and almost all of it needs a way to pick things up: that is why Talos got a claw for 2026. The torpedo's 1 400-point sequence bonus needs both shots through the right holes, large first. And the two coin flips at the gate add 450 points for a vehicle that can start facing any way and take either role.

Rules that shape the vehicle

A vehicle may not enter the water until it passes a static safety inspection. These rules explain a lot of Talos's design:

Fully autonomous
All decisions on board; no communication with people or off-board computers during a run (a team's two vehicles may talk to each other, which scores as inter-vehicle communication). So the computer, cameras and the mission tree all live inside Talos (Computers and networks).
Submerged
Everything attached must go under with the vehicle. No tether in a scored run.
Kill switch
Clearly marked, easy for a diver to pull, cuts battery power to all propulsion, and the props must not start spinning again when it is put back (Commanding Talos).
Shrouded props
Commercial shrouded thrusters count; Talos's are Blue Robotics T200s (Thrusters and ESCs).
Floats when killed
Positively buoyant by at least 0.5 % of its mass with the kill switch pulled (Hull, frame and layout).
Size
Fits in a 0.9 × 0.9 × 1.8 m box.
Weight
Over 60 kg is disqualified; over 38 kg costs points; under 38 kg earns 4.4 points per kg below 38. Talos's config gives 32.4 kg dry (weighed for the October 2026 pool tests, claw fitted), about +24 points (the official number is the competition's own scale).
Batteries
Sealed, at most 60 V open-circuit, never charged inside a sealed housing (Power and wiring).
Markers, torpedoes
At most two of each, each within 51 × 51 × 152 mm and 0.91 kg, marked with the team's name or emblem (Torpedoes, dropper and claw).

Prequalification

Before the event a team may qualify from home with an unbroken video of one autonomous run on a simple course: a 2 m horizontal gate 1 m under the surface, and a vertical marker 10 m beyond it. The vehicle starts submerged 3 m behind the gate, passes through, circles the marker and comes back through, without breaking the surface. Teams that pass go straight to the semi-finals. Talos prequalified for 2026 in March.

The tree for that run is PreQualMpcTree.xml: dive to 1 m, then one path through the gate, out 3 m to the side of the pole, a half circle behind it while facing it, and back through the gate nose first. The gate and pole are two stored positions, prequal_gate and prequal_pole, 10 m apart.

The team and its robots

UWRT has four subteams: Mechanical, Electrical, Software and Business. The lab is in Dreese Laboratory, room 457, 2015 Neil Ave. Two things come from a lead, not from this site: write access to the team's GitHub (Before you start) and access to the robot's network from your own laptop (Joining from your own laptop).

What each subteam does
  • Mechanical designs, machines and assembles the hulls, frames, thruster mounts and manipulators.
  • Electrical builds the custom circuit boards and power systems and integrates the sensors.
  • Software writes the autonomy stack: perception, mapping, control and mission planning.
  • Business runs sponsorships, finances, outreach and media.

Each technical subteam has a reading path under Where to start. Anyone coming to the pool should read First pool test. (Team website, src/config/subteams.ts and site.ts.)

UWRT started with ROVs and switched to AUVs for RoboSub in 2015. The software stack is still called Riptide after the first AUV, which is why every package is riptide_*. Old robot names survive in the code: riptide_descriptions/config has talos.yaml, tempest.yaml, puddles.yaml, liltank.yaml and simulator.yaml, and launch files load the one named by their robot argument. So puddles_ekf.yaml or a tempest default in an old launch file is history, not a second robot.

Every UWRT AUV, and the 2025–26 second vehicles
  • liltank. liltank.yaml (added May 2025) describes a 22.7 kg vehicle with two thrusters, an active ballast tank and only a forward camera. That matches the tracked, ballast-driven SS Flip Barker, so liltank is probably its code name. LiltankCompTree.xml is the tank's own run: it dives, then waits for Talos. DeployTankTree.xml is Talos's side: it lines up Talos's hitch over the table to drop the tank there; the release step itself is still a placeholder.
  • Triton, the 2026 second vehicle, runs a separate stack, Mercury. For 2026 the team planned it for the gate, the slalom and the return home, talking to Talos acoustically (inter-vehicle communication).
