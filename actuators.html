The vehicle · Chapter 6

Torpedoes, dropper and claw

The parts of Talos that act on the course: a launcher that shoots two torpedoes and drops two markers, a claw that carries objects, and a magnet on a stick. Here is how a shot gets from the mission tree to the spring.

The short version
  • Talos carries a launcher (two torpedoes pointing forward, two markers pointing down), a claw under the bow and a passive magnet poker.
  • One servo on the POAC board fires the whole launcher. From its 120° home it turns one way for torpedoes (200°, then 240°) and the other way for markers (50°, then 0°), then goes back home.
  • A tree calls a ROS service; a small node turns it into a topic; that reaches the POAC over CAN, and the POAC answers yes or no.
  • The POAC refuses a torpedo unless the robot isn't killed (kill switch in, Enable pressed), the launcher is armed and the servo is idle. Trees arm just before a task and disarm right after.
  • Nereus takes the same commands with slightly stricter rules and flies real projectiles, so trees can be tested before the pool.

Several RoboSub tasks need Talos to touch the course: torpedoes through holes in a board, markers into bins, objects moved from a table into baskets, and magnets held against targets (RoboSub and the course). This chapter is the hardware and code that do it.

The launcher: one servo, four shots

The CAD calls it Torpedoes + Markers V8. Four compression springs hold the energy; the servo only lets go. It turns a spur gear and a pair of helix gears that move a pushing arm, which releases one payload at a time: torpedoes from two tubes pointing forward, markers from two tubes pointing down.

Payloads
2 torpedoes forward, 2 markers down. CAD torpedo: PBT, 83 mm long, 26 mm across.
Energy
4 compression springs (McMaster 9435K123)
Where
18.8 cm ahead of base_link, 14.4 cm to port, 6.4 cm down; tubes 35 mm apart
Servo
The firmware drives a Hiwonder bus servo (one data wire shared by several servos, each with an ID). The CAD shows a Blue Trail SER-20XX. Which one is fitted today is unconfirmed.

The firmware knows five angles on the servo's 0–240° range (ros_torp.c): home 120° holds everything; torpedoes fire at 200°, then 240°; markers at 50°, then 0°. After every shot the servo goes back home. The board only counts what is left; it can't see the tubes.

The tree aims the first torpedo from the starboard tube (torpedo_0_link). Nothing confirms which tube the 200° move empties: check it on the bench, or the first shot is aimed 35 mm off.

The POAC drives the servo

The launcher servo plugs into the POAC (power + actuator) board in the port board stack. Its RP2040 runs the actuator code next to power and kill-switch duties (The circuit boards, Firmware on the boards).

  • Serial servo. One data wire at 115200 baud (UART, made in the chip's programmable I/O), level-shifted to 5 V. The servo is ID 1; a position of 0–1000 means 0–240° (hiwonder_driver.c).
  • Arming = torque on. arm switches the servo's torque on and reads it back; the board says armed only once the servo confirms (actuator.c).
  • A shot is a planned move. Angle ÷ 300°/s, 250 ms to settle, back home, another 250 ms: under a second, with busy true throughout.
  • Timers. Status and busy every 500 ms; the reply to a command on the next 100 ms tick (main.c). A servo that stops answering shows as ERROR.

Every command is checked before the servo moves (ros_torp.c, branch master). A refusal comes back as cmd_status false plus a reason on cmd_feedback (both explained in The ROS interface):

Safety
  • Treat the launcher as live whenever the POAC has power. The servo's 12 V comes from the board's own regulators, not the thruster power the kill switch cuts.
  • A kill does not disarm. safety_handle_kill still says // TODO: disarm actuators (safety_interface.c). After a kill, disarm by hand.
  • The dropper checks nothing but the count: not kill, not arming, not busy. Don't count on a disarmed servo staying put.
  • Keep hands and faces out of line of the torpedo tubes and from under the marker tubes whenever the board is on.

The ROS interface

The POAC talks to ROS through micro-ROS: its topics reach the Orin over CAN through the copro_agent. All names are under the robot namespace, so command/actuator/torpedo is /talos/command/actuator/torpedo.

NameTypeMeaning
Topics (the board's own interface)
command/actuator/armBooltrue arms, false disarms

command/actuator/notify_reloadEmpty"I reloaded by hand": counts back to 2

state/actuator/cmd_statusBoolreply to the last command: accepted or not

Services (for trees and scripts)
command/actuator/armSetBoolarm or disarm, answered with success
command/actuator/torpedo, …/dropper, …/notify_reloadTriggerthe same commands, answered with success

Why both? The firmware only takes topics, and a topic gives the sender no answer. A tree wants to know whether the shot was accepted, so actuator_service_translator offers each command as a service: it publishes the topic, waits for cmd_status and returns it as success. It starts with autonomy, not with the hardware. Two catches: it has no timeout (no reply, no answer; restart it if calls hang), and its reply never says why (the message is empty), so watch cmd_feedback for the reason.

How the trees use them

The competition tree is RepairCompMpcTree.xml (The competition mission). Four small subtrees do all the actuator work:

  • SetActuatorsArmed: wait 1 s, then call command/actuator/arm (1 s limit). A refused arm is silent (L2196).
  • FireTorpedo: call command/actuator/torpedo (1 s limit), then wait 2 s (L1004). The WaitForActuators after that gives up at once, because its timeout is never set (Things to watch).
  • FireDropper: check nothing is in ERROR, wait until idle, check a marker is left, call command/actuator/dropper, wait 1 s, wait until idle (L977).
  • ReloadActuators: call notify_reload up to 3 times. Every run starts with it, then a disarm.

The pattern is arm on the way in, disarm on the way out, so a stray command between tasks is refused. One gap: the bins task disarms only after two classifier service calls, so if either fails the launcher stays armed (L160).

TaskWhat the tree does
TorpedoesWait 1.5 m off the board until both holes' map estimates are tight (covariance 0.0005). Line torpedo_0_link up on hole 1, 0.6 m out, until the controller's trust is above 0.985 and the link is within 1 cm of the hole's axis. Fire. Slide across so torpedo_1_link faces hole 2 and fire again.
MarkersGet a tight estimate of the bin with the down camera, then hold droppers_link 1 m straight above it (trust 0.90, 1 cm). Drop. Twice, one bin each.
TableArm once. For each object: open the claw for 2.0 s, put claw_link on it, close for 2.0 s, carry it to the basket, open for 0.3 s to drop it, close.
MagnetsFly magnet_link 0.7 m out from the target, facing it, then straight onto it, 4 cm up. Hold 2 s.
A shot that "succeeds" without firing

SetActuatorsArmed, FireTorpedo and FireDropper each sit inside a ForceSuccess (a decorator that turns any result into success) named REMOVE ME. So a refused arm or shot reads as success, and the 3 retries around each shot never run. The likeliest case is a refused arm (robot still killed, or the translator stuck): every shot after it is refused with Not Armed while the tree carries on. In the Nereus media run the second torpedo hit exactly this: the tasks had just been reset, which disarms, the call was refused, and the tree went on to the table (step 7 of "In Nereus" above).

The claw and the magnet poker

The claw (CAD EMERGENCYCLAWASSEMBLY) hangs below the bow. Its servo turns a pinion between two gear racks; each rack carries one jaw, so the jaws close evenly on the centre line.

The claw is driven by time, not position. Its servo runs as a plain motor with no absolute position, so the command is a signed number of seconds on command/actuator/claw_move_s: positive opens, negative closes, full speed for that long. The tree then waits as long as it asked for.

That firmware is not on master. It is on the unmerged branch poac-multiple-servos (last commit 2026-06-28). Which build is flashed on Talos today isn't confirmed; state/firmware tells you on the robot.

The magnet poker is a plastic rod with a magnet zip-tied to its tip. Nothing on it moves: the course's magnet targets react to the magnet, so the tree only has to put magnet_link on the target and hold it there for 2 s. The files disagree about where the magnet is (see Details), so measure it on the robot before trusting the magnet task.

In Nereus

Nereus describes each mechanism in the Talos robot pack (robot.yaml, run by mechanisms.cpp). Its bridge (bridge.yaml) takes both the topics and the services and publishes the status with the POAC's numbers, so trees, the translator and the viewer work unchanged.

  • Launcher and dropper. Two slots each, starboard first. A shot releases a free body pushed by a 0.047 J spring: 2.49 m/s for a 12.2 g torpedo, 2.29 m/s for a 14.9 g marker, plus the vehicle's speed. Torpedoes and markers share a 0.5 s cooldown, standing in for the single servo.
  • Claw. Two jaws with 12 N of grip; an object counts as held after 40 ms in contact.
  • Arming. One flag for launcher, dropper and claw (safety).

Testing on the bench

Firing the torpedo and dropper on the bench is fine; the team's pre-pool tool (pretest_checks, step G5) does it the same way. The rule that matters is about the thrusters:

Before you enable
  • The controller must not be running. An enabled robot with the controller up spins the thrusters, and the launcher can only arm while the robot is enabled.
  • Clear the area in front of the torpedo tubes and below the marker tubes. Load only what you mean to fire.
  • On the poac-multiple-servos build the claw moves without arming or enable. Keep fingers out of the jaws whenever the board is on.
  1. Robot on the bench supply, hardware stack up. Check the board answers: state/actuator/status every 500 ms, torpedo_state not 0.
  2. Kill switch in, then Enable in the Nereus viewer.
  3. Arm from the viewer's Actuators panel or a shell.
  4. Fire one torpedo or marker. The count drops by one; busy is true for under a second.
  5. Disarm, then Disable in the viewer.
  6. Reload by hand, then send notify_reload so the counts are 2 again.
  7. Claw (poac-multiple-servos build only): claw_move_s 2.0 to open, then −2.0 to close.

How to reload. The loading procedure (order, servo position, compressing the springs) isn't written down in code or the team's docs. Ask the mechanical lead to show you before your first time.

# watch the state and the reasons for any refusal
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/actuator/status
ros2 topic echo /talos/state/actuator/cmd_feedback

# arm, fire, disarm through the topics (always there)
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/arm std_msgs/msg/Bool "{data: true}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/torpedo std_msgs/msg/Empty "{}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/arm std_msgs/msg/Bool "{data: false}"

# or through the services (needs actuator_service_translator, launched with autonomy)
ros2 service call /talos/command/actuator/dropper std_srvs/srv/Trigger

# after reloading by hand
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/notify_reload std_msgs/msg/Empty "{}"

# claw, poac-multiple-servos build only: open 2 s, then close 2 s (never send 0)
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/claw_move_s std_msgs/msg/Float32 "{data: 2.0}"
ros2 topic pub --once /talos/command/actuator/claw_move_s std_msgs/msg/Float32 "{data: -2.0}"

Never send 0 to the claw on the robot. The branch firmware works out the direction as time_ms / abs(time_ms), an integer divide by zero when the time is 0, and what the RP2040 does then is undefined. The viewer's Stop claw button sends exactly that. In Nereus, 0 is a clean stop.

Details and known bugs

Edge cases worth knowing once you work on this code.

The translator can pair a call with the wrong reply

The translator ends its wait on any cmd_status, even the reply to a command the viewer sent, and returns that as this call's success. Calls queued behind it then pair with the wrong replies too. It also handles one call at a time: the four services share one queue. The tree gives up after its 1 s limit either way; restarting the translator is the clean fix (L36).

What the status numbers mean

ActuatorStatus (ActuatorStatus.msg) carries actuators_armed, a state and a count for torpedoes and droppers, and claw and "rack" fields from older robots (the POAC always sends the claw state as 0). Torpedo states: 0 ERROR (servo not answering), 1 DISARMED, 3 CHARGED (armed, idle, one left), 4 FIRING (servo moving, for either payload), 5 FIRED (none left). Dropper: 0 ERROR, 1 DISARMED, 2 READY, 3 DROPPING, 4 DROPPED. 2 CHARGING is never sent: it belonged to an older coil-fired launcher whose code is still there, commented out.

Re-homing the servo

command/actuator/torpedo_marker/set_home (only while disarmed) makes the servo's current position home; …/go_home (armed, idle) drives it back there. The new home lives in RAM: after a reboot it is 120° again. The firing angles are fixed numbers, so they don't move with home.

A third way to fire: simple_actuator_interface

simple_actuator_interface.py, started with the hardware, listens on command/simple_torpedo_fire and command/simple_dropper_fire. It arms if needed, fires through the services, waits for busy to clear and leaves the launcher armed (its disarm step is commented out).

talos.yaml poses are not from base_link

The poses in talos.yaml are measured from talos/origin. base_link sits at (−0.14, 0.03, −0.09) in origin, so subtract that before comparing them with anything else (robot_base.xacro). This page has done that already.

Where is the magnet?

Three sources, all converted to base_link, and they disagree:

  • magnet_link (talos.yaml magnet:), the frame the trees fly: 15.5 cm behind, near the centre line, 30 cm down.
  • The July CAD poker: 15 to 33 cm ahead, 12–16 cm to starboard, tip about 10 cm down (the CAD placed by matching its eight thrusters to talos.yaml).
  • poker_link (talos.yaml poker:, also Nereus's poker_mount): 37 cm ahead, 2 cm to starboard, 8 cm down. No tree uses it.

At least one is out of date.
